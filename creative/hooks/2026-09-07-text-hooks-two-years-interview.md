## Who this is for, and what she should feel

**ICP:** `pays-and-still-owes`, the flagship persona in `personas-profile.md`. The script's guest is her on the surface: a mom on an employer plan, competent everywhere ("I manage projects at work. The persona voice library's read of what moves her (`persona-voice-library.md`, the felt sense): "She did the responsible thing, every month, for years. She paid. And when she needed the thing she paid for, it was not there." Her top message signal is `paying-twice`, "the most recurrent idea in the corpus by a distance." Age roughly 28 to 44 per the doc, so the first cut lands at 3 to 4 seconds. The "putting it off" premise itself has no corpus trace (zero hits on "put off," "putting off," "not dumb," "never understand" across `personas/`), so it is the script's, marked illustrative below where a hook carries it.

**Emotion:** recognition first, then relief. Not fear. She already knows her plan is bad; that's not news. The feeling the script sells is "someone else put it off too, and it was fine." In `hook-psychology.md` #11 terms that is a Zone 3 open (mild shared frustration, "I've been putting this off") resolving to Zone 1 (calm relief), and the script's own body holds that zone the whole way: no crying, a laugh at the end. The account's money says this is the right zone: calm and aspirational openers delivered $13.29 to $24.45 per lead in the September hook audit, the high-distress openers $29.85 to $38.09.

**Format read:** this is a two-voice interview, host off camera. The account has shot two-voice before, twice. The nearer proof is the two-woman podcast `MOMS25 BLAST - X1 - Copy 2`: 175 leads at $19.90 on $3,483.03 with a 10.29% hold, not repeated in ten months (`brand-lens.md`: "a second person in frame clears the hold floor; one woman alone with her phone does not"). The other is the `Moms40` street-interview family, 8 ads, $1,172.43 lifetime, 31 leads at $37.82, hook rates of 40.42% and 48.69% (`visual-vocabulary.md` shot 13). So the frame is production-proven, and the cheaper version of it is the kitchen-table two-hander, not the street. The organic reference for the register is the kitchen-table confession (`2026-09-04-the-calm-confession.md`, 872,100 views): a calm woman, framed family photos, no music.

**Where it sits in the strategy:** it's a switch story, which is the roadmap's Priority 2 ("lead with the switch, not the saving"), drafted and unapproved. It also touches the idea-bank entry `2026-09-04-you-do-not-have-to-wait.md`: "two years" is a wait, and no ad in the account's history has ever said she doesn't have to.

## The four hooks

### 1. Question hook (#20), aimed at her — the mom who's been putting it off, recognition

**Text overlay (held white block, first frame):**
> How long have you been putting off looking at your health insurance?

**Sound:** the host's voice, off camera, asking the script's first line: "So how long had you been putting it off?" No music. One side of a conversation is the precognitive-sound mechanic in `hooks.md` "The First Second": the brain stays for the answer.
**Visual:** medium shot, the guest at a kitchen table, framed photos behind her, warm lamp light (the calm-confession frame). She hears the question, exhales, half-laughs, looks down, then up. Candid face mid-emotion, before she speaks. Cut at 0:03 to a tighter shot as she says "Two years."
**Vibe:** late-night kitchen table. Calm. Slightly embarrassed.
**ICP and emotion:** `pays-and-still-owes`; recognition. She answers the overlay in her own head before the guest does, which is the mechanic in `2026-09-04-the-question-she-can-answer.md` (a question she can answer for you, not one only the brand can answer). The account's existing question-shaped overlays are brand-answered; this one is hers.
**Tag:** Untapped opportunity. No text hook in the top 15 is a question aimed at the viewer.
**Why:** it's the script's own first line turned toward her, so overlay, sound and picture all do one job: self-reference (`hook-psychology.md` #6). The overlay's edit changes "had you" to "have you" so it reads as her question, not the guest's. Second person, no number, no claim, so nothing to bid against. *Illustrative:* the "putting it off" framing is the script's, not a commenter's phrase.

### 2. Investment hook (#2), time paid in — the dutiful payer, sunk cost named

**Text overlay:**
> 2 years of paying every month for a plan that never actually helped us…

**Sound:** same off-camera question under it. The overlay and the answer ("Two years. Maybe more.") land together at 0:02 to 0:03.
**Visual:** same kitchen-table frame. Her hand comes up to her forehead on "two years," the exhaustion-by-posture move from the molly_daw organic (hand on head before she speaks).
**Vibe:** rueful, warm.
**ICP and emotion:** `pays-and-still-owes`; sunk-cost validation. `hooks.md` #2 defines the investment as time or money spent before the solution, and the process file wants "what these customers actually tried." What she tried was paying: the persona's `paying-twice` signal, and the commenter line "I still pay full price for everything all year until I pay $6k which has been never" (`MOMS38 - 1 - V5`, 2026-04-08). Two years is the script's own number, the time she says she accepted the bad plan, and it's time, not a dollar figure, so nothing to bid against and no savings claim in the hook. "Actually" is on the brand's own words-we-use list.
**Tag:** Untapped opportunity. The hook audit lists Investment as a format the account has never run, and `hooks.md` marks it "works incredibly well for 40+ audiences and women-skewed demographics" and "almost always test this in first round of creative."
**Why:** the script is an investment story once you read it the persona's way: two years paid into a plan that gave nothing back, then the fix. The overlay names that investment, so the "Two years" answer at 0:03 lands as the receipt. The trailing ellipsis is the corpus's own habit (the $11.89 static "Didn't know I could ditch my job's health plan… until I did.") and leaves the loop open.

### 3. Permission hook (#21) — the mom who kept paying because it was the responsible thing, release

**Text overlay:**
> You've paid every month for years. You're allowed to look somewhere else.

**Sound:** off-camera host question, or silence for the first second and then the question. Silence counts as a sound hook per `hooks.md`.
**Visual:** same frame, but open on her mid-sentence, laughing at herself, the beat where she says "I'm not a dumb person." Then cut back to the host's question so the interview restarts from the top. That's a 3-second cold open pulled from about 0:20 of the body; the grab doing the work is `hooks.md`'s candid face mid-emotion, a face caught mid-expression before the words land.
**Vibe:** kind. A friend saying it, not a brand.
**ICP and emotion:** `pays-and-still-owes`; release. `hooks.md` #21: "name an obligation she's already carrying, then convert it to a release." The obligation on record is not confusion, it is duty: `persona-voice-library.md`'s "She did the responsible thing, every month, for years. She paid." The overlay names that and grants the release. "Look somewhere else" stays a permission, not a pitch, so the zone holds into the calm body.
**Tag:** Untapped opportunity, nearly. The "At peace" ad brushed this format and its body didn't hold the zone (2.30% hold). This script's body does: no crying, a laugh at the end.
**Why:** it is the only one of the four that opens in pure Zone 1. `hook-psychology.md` #11 says relief converts on cold traffic where pressure makes her scroll, and this account's own CPL split says the same. Compliance: no claim, no figure, no villain named, no guarantee. "Every month for years" is the persona's own felt sense, and "which has been never" is the commenter's.

### 4. POV hook (#5) — the proven family, the relief of finally doing it

**Text overlay:**
> POV: You finally switched health insurance after putting it off for 2 years 😅

**Sound:** off-camera host question.
**Visual:** the flagship's grammar, adapted: open on her already relaxed, laughing at the host's "you should have done it sooner" (the script's 0:50 beat), then jump back to the top. Or the straight open on the kitchen table if the edit needs to stay linear.
**Vibe:** the calm-POV lane (`POV: At peace...`), not the wife-of-the-year brag.
**ICP and emotion:** `pays-and-still-owes`; relief, a little self-mockery (the 😅 is doing the "maybe more" work).
**Tag:** Safe bet. POV is the account's most-funded overlay family, on 6 of the top 15 spenders. *Illustrative:* "putting it off" is the script's framing, not a commenter's.
**Why:** it's the control arm. If the three untapped formats above don't beat it, you've learned the format ceiling on this script at almost no cost. Adapted per `hooks.md` #5, structure kept, content swapped: the flagship names a win ("saved $400 a month, AND the deductible is zero"); this one names the moment before the win, so no savings figure appears in the hook and the body carries the hedged claim.

## What to test first

Hook 1 with Hook 4 as the control.
