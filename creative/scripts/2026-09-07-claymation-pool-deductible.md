## The angle, and the one collision to know about

**The angle is the pool.** Your guidelines name it. Section 7, verbatim: *"Pool-pricing mechanic: this is the causal explanation for why deductibles are high. The education beat should explain why, not define terms — this sets up the private-plan pivot logically."* And the rule right above it: *"don't over-explain concepts the audience already understands (e.g., what a deductible is) — assume problem-awareness and reframe the cause instead."* So this script never defines a deductible. It reframes where the number comes from.

**The collision.** Explaining a risk pool honestly runs close to three of your own hard rules: no government or ACA reference, no punching down at demographic groups, and the underwriting screen that generates your loudest complaints (the word "pregnant" appears in 38 of 1,342 comments, the word "existing" in 38, per `voc-corpus-profile.md`). Your two proven claymation ads sidestepped all three by explaining a *different* mechanism: the cash price versus the insured price. `Moms46 - V2`'s real opening line is *"Same scan, same hospital. One price for cash, a much bigger one if you hand over your card."* That is the-system-is-the-villain, and `Moms46 - V1` on the same mechanism held 13.26% on the 2026-09-05 pull. This script stays on the pool because you asked for it and your guidelines call for it, but it does it without saying "healthy," "sick," "marketplace," or any program name. The pool is just "the pool." The private plan is "a different pool, different math, see if you qualify." That last phrase is the hedge every one of your winning scripts already carries.

**One thing I'm flagging rather than deciding.** The claim "private family plans are priced in a different pool" is your brand's own logic (`stated`, Section 7), not something the vault verifies from a carrier. Run it past whoever owns compliance before it airs. It is the honest version of the mechanic and it's how your partner agencies' own sellers describe it in your comments, but it is a claim about how plans are priced, so somebody should sign off.

## Why this script

Your audience already knows what a deductible is. She has told you 207 times. What she does not know is why hers is $6,000 when she pays over a thousand a month, and your guidelines say the answer is the pool. Nobody in your account has ever said that. Your claymation ads are the only creative you've made that demonstrates a mechanism instead of asserting a claim. Here is the honest hold-rate picture from the 2026-09-05 pull, because it's messier than the vault says: `Moms46 - V1` held 13.26% on $381.82 (12 leads at $31.82). `Moms46 - V2` held 11.06% on $86.06, just under the 12% floor. `Animation Agency #4 - H1` held 6.59% on $78.34 (3 leads at $26.11). Your biggest spender holds 3.35%. So one claymation unit clears the floor, one sits right under it, and the character-led one holds half as well as the desk-of-bills one. The vault's audit line that "Educational is the only format in the account whose hold rate clears the 12 percent floor" is true of V1 and not of the family, and that collision is why this script opens on V1's frame, not H1's. So this is the proven grammar, on the angle your own rulebook asks for, with the one character in your library that has never had a real run: the deductible itself, on the kitchen counter, arms folded, explaining why it's so big.

**Why the deductible narrates, and the risk in that.** `Animation Agency #4 - H1` opens on a personified bill saying "I am your deductible." The original ran $78.34 for 3 leads at $26.11 and held 6.59%; its two copies got $1.12 and $1.15. The built-frame entry calls the character the most distinctive unused asset you own, and on $78.34 that's still fair, but 6.59% hold on the one real run means the character alone did not keep people. So this script does not bet on the character alone. Beat 1 opens on the proven desk-of-bills frame from `Moms46 - V1` (31.25% hook, 13.26% hold) and lets the character speak from inside it, and the mechanism does the holding, the way V1's reveals did. If this unit holds under 12%, the read is that the character is the drag, and the next cut drops it for straight narration over the same beats.

**The AI-presenter rule, addressed.** Your audience rejected synthetic humans six times across three years. That rejection is aimed at photorealistic AI people posing as real testimonials. Claymation is obviously and honestly not a person. The built-frame entry says that distinction is "worth testing rather than assuming," and this is the test.
