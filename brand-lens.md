# Health For Moms — brand lens

The standing overlay of Health For Moms' own knowledge. Loaded on top of the general creative-strategy method on every creative-strategy output. **Where a line here conflicts with a generic rule, this line governs** — it is the brand speaking. Every entry carries its source and date and is marked **stated** (the brand told us, not independently checked) or **verified** (observed directly in their data, creative, or a confirmed correction).

Add to this file whenever the brand teaches Parker something durable — a correction, a constraint, a preference, a what-worked — rather than letting it evaporate into one conversation.

## Voice and language

- **Tagline:** "Better family health coverage for less." `stated`, brand context document section 7, 2026-09-03.
- **The named mechanism:** the **MomSmart Coverage Method™**. Whether it is a registered mark, and what the underlying mechanism actually is, are both open questions routed to the brand. `stated`, section 7, 2026-09-03.
- **Words never used in creative: cheap, discount, policy, ACA, exclusions, guaranteed.** `stated`, section 8, marked non-negotiable, 2026-09-03. Note the brand's own emotional promise trips its own list — "It's not about **policies** — it's about protection, simplicity, and relief" — using the word to reject the concept. The rule as written carves out no exception.
- **Tone the brand wants:** warm, solution-oriented, challenger. It casts the insurance system as the villain and itself as the one exposing it, but explicitly "warm and solution-oriented rather than fear-driven." `stated`, section 1.
- **Tone the brand bars:** corporate, overly clinical, scare-heavy. It says it "can use direct response principles but should focus on reassurance and clarity." `stated`, section 8.
- **How it describes its own agents:** "like your Mom BFFs - friendly and approachable," so moms can ask questions "without feeling pressure," and "licensed advisors who speak human, not 'insurance'." That register is the brand's own, worth matching. `stated`, section 1.
- **Risk tolerance: 75 of 100** — the brand context document reads this as moderate-to-high tolerance for direct response while staying inside clear guardrails. `stated`.
- **Section 7's tone list is explicitly labelled "stylistic preferences, not compliance rules."** Section 8 is the hard one. Keep the two apart; do not treat a style preference as a gate or a hard rule as a suggestion. `stated`.

## Claims and compliance constraints

These are hard. The brand heads the section **"THIS SECTION IS NON-NEGOTIABLE. Every constraint listed here is a hard rule."** All `stated`, section 8, 2026-09-03. The same list sits in `CLAUDE.md` because it outranks everything.

- **Savings: always "up to 30%," never a flat number.**
- **Deductible: always "$0 deductible option" or "options with no deductibles," never a flat or guaranteed outcome.**
- **No hard guarantees.**
- **No fabricated statistics, no named competitor attacks, no government or ACA references.**
- **No scare tactics, no political or partisan language, no punching down at demographic groups.**
- **The villain is always the system or mechanism, never a named company or individual.**
- **A doctor character requires a real licensed physician, or language adjusted so no false credential is implied.**

**No required disclaimer text exists.** The brand states none was provided; the two claim qualifiers function as its de facto disclaimers. Separately, the account already runs an on-screen dramatization disclaimer in its winning scripts that the brand never wrote down as a requirement. `stated` plus `verified` from the ad transcripts, 2026-09-03.

**The live breach, and the standing instruction it creates.** Two claims run on essentially the whole account against these rules. A live sweep on 2026-09-04 found *"saving you 20% or more"* on 127 of 128 ad-name groups carrying $100,066.79 of $100,067.42 in window spend, and the headline *"Approved by thousands of Mom's across America"* on the identical 127 groups and the identical dollar. Two sweeps, same count, same cent: they travel in one shared copy block. Re-confirmed live 2026-09-05, all five top-spending active ads still carry both. `verified`.

So: **never lift existing body copy as a reference or a starting point without checking it against the rules above.** The split is clean and worth holding in mind — the currently-scaling *video scripts* hold the qualifiers correctly ("save up to 30%," "you may qualify for," "see if you qualify for"), and the legacy *body-copy block* does not.

**The claims nobody can substantiate yet.** Nothing anywhere in this brain says what the 20%, 24% or 30% compares against. Zero customer reviews, zero press, no Better Business Bureau profile for the operating entity, and two pieces of praise in 1,342 comments, both from women who were told no. Every credibility marker this brand carries today is its own assertion. `verified`, `reputation-analysis.md`, 2026-09-04.

**Two ads already carry a Meta status of DISAPPROVED** — both variants of `B1 - Copy 7`, holding $8,553.74 and $5,048.37. Meta's reason is not visible and is not being attributed. `verified`, 2026-09-04.

## Do's and don'ts

- **Never use a synthetic or AI presenter.** Six rejections across three separate years and four ad families in the brand's own comment record. This is the closest thing to a rule in the entire customer dataset. `verified`, comment corpus, 2026-09-04.
- **No pregnancy-only targeting; focus on moms with kids, not expectant mothers.** `stated`, section 8. And it is more than a preference: the plans are medically underwritten and the funnel screens pregnancy out, so leading here means paying to create a public complaint the brand cannot resolve. The team confirmed on 2026-09-04 that it deliberately removed pregnancy from its own inspiration set. Demand is genuinely there — 38 of 1,342 comments over fourteen months, the highest like counts in the corpus, 63.2% of the category's organic feed — which makes this a recruitment cost to reduce, not whitespace to take.
- **Same rule, more force, for the chronic-condition family.** The most emotionally intense identity in the corpus, and medical underwriting screens her out too. Reaching her harder makes the problem worse.
- **No lab coat or clinical setting as the speaker.** The account already ran this experiment: the clinical setting delivered **52.7% of its spend to women 25 to 34** and 3.8% to women 45 to 54, the youngest delivery of anything inspected. `verified`, 2026-09-04.
- **Nothing political, and the brand has tested it.** Its own verdict, verbatim: *"Anything political doesnt work."* Three ads in the `MOMS25 - 5TH DEC - Y1` set carry framing that brushes this line and the no-ACA rule — verbatim verbal hooks *"This is what happens when you stop paying for everyone else's healthcare"* and *"making over 50k just disqualified this family from affordable healthcare, but it secretly qualified them for something way better."* Both are tiny in spend, both paused, and both are recorded here so nobody pulls them forward as references. `verified`, hook fields, 2026-09-03.
- **The villain rule is already held well.** The account's most aggressive hooks keep the antagonist as the category and never name a carrier: "Health Insurance is a scam 🙄", "Big Health Insurance is a scam!!!", "American health insurance is a scam". `verified`, hook fields, 2026-09-03.

## What has worked and what has failed

Every figure below cleared **gate one only** — cost per lead. Gate two, lead quality, lives with the partner agencies and Parker cannot see it. Nothing here is a winner.

**Statics convert twice as well as video, and the direction of the click rate proves it isn't junk.** Lifetime, `verified` live 2026-09-04: statics turned **16.57% of link clicks into leads at $12.30** across 308 groups and $168,907.73; video turned **8.28% into leads at $22.23** across 1,259 groups and $574,310.36. Video wins the click by 30% on CTR and then loses the lead by half. If statics were buying cheap junk the pattern would run the other way.

**The employer-switch statics are the cheapest proven thing in the library, and they are switched off.** `B1 - Copy 7` and `B1 samar- Copy 1` together: **$44,972.40, 20,038 link clicks, 3,815 leads, 19.04% leads per link click, $11.79 cost per lead.** Against the brand's own scoreboard, where under $15 is "fantastic," that is 21% below the ceiling, on the cheapest impressions in the account. Both dark. `verified` live 2026-09-04.

**Calm beats distress, and the account is currently building the wrong one.** Over the 30 days to 2026-09-02, calm openers delivered leads at **$13.29 to $24.45** while high-distress crying openers delivered at **$29.85 to $38.09**, blending to $33.23 across six ads — while posting *higher* hook rates at 57.02% and 52.40%. They stop more people and cost half again as much. The account is building more of the distress lane, six ads and climbing. `verified`, `monthly-hook-audit.md`.

**A second person in frame clears the hold floor; one woman alone with her phone does not.** Every configuration with a second character in frame or on the line clears or nearly clears the 12% floor; every solo-with-phone configuration sits under 6%. The two-woman podcast `MOMS25 BLAST - X1 - Copy 2` bought **175 leads at $19.90 on $3,483.03 with a 10.29% hold** and has not been repeated in ten months. The one-actor two-role phone call runs its two best builds at **$22.87 and $22.50** while holding **27.31% and 25.36%**. `verified`.

**The account's biggest ad wins the thumb and then has nothing to say.** `Moms43 - 4 - V3`: 45.7% hook rate against a 30% floor, **3.35% hold against a 12% floor**, and it took **$14,985.16 of the last 30 days at $24.98 per lead** while `moms-63 2b` delivered at $13.26. No spoken word until 0:18. `verified` live 2026-09-05.

**The state list buys the cheapest clicks in the account and converts them worst at scale.** `moms-53 3` holds the best CTR at **3.15%** and the cheapest link click at **$0.96**, both best in the file — then converts **5.97% of link clicks into leads** against `moms-63 2b`'s 12.25% in the same window. Strong qualifying device inside an ad. Not the lead. `verified`.

**"Wife of the year" works on gate one and rests on nothing in the customer record.** The POV family carries **$87,991.09 across 20 ads at $19.56 blended**, with no measured decay across twenty uses. But the literal string "wife of the year" returns **0 of 1,342 comments** — nobody repeats it and nobody rejects it. **Keep it running; do not build the next thing on it.** `verified`.

**Two destinations to know about.** All four `go.healthformoms.co/save/` ads together hold **$3,474.43, 2,188 link clicks and 18 leads — 0.82%**, and one of them took **511 link clicks and produced zero leads**. The validation came back inconclusive on *cause* (a healthy March cohort on the same subdomain converted at 8.69%), but the action is not in doubt: they are duplicates of creative that works fine on `www.`, so switching them off costs nothing. Separately `quiz.healthformoms.com` holds **$138.27 lifetime, 57 link clicks, 2 leads** — keep it small until it has a lead count worth reading. `verified` live 2026-09-04.

**December broke what November proved.** The 2025 Open Enrollment ramp worked on volume and was efficiency-neutral: November held cost per lead nearly flat at **$13.08** on the year's biggest lead month. Then December became the most expensive of thirteen readable months at a **$29.34 CPM**, with cost per lead up **39.8% to $18.28**, on largely the same creative and funnel. Layer for November. Do not buy December on the assumption it is November's second half. `verified`.

## Positioning and audience nuances

**The central divergence, and it governs everything.** The brand addresses a woman who is uninsured and wants rescuing. It reaches a woman who is insured, broke, and does not know she is allowed to leave the plan her job picked for her. **"Deductible" appears in 207 of 1,342 comments. "Uninsured" appears in 2.** She is not a gap in the market; she is somebody else's customer and she is angry about it. `verified` live 2026-09-04.

**The delivered audience has been stable for the account's whole life and does not need fixing.** Lifetime: $743,218.09, 39,569 leads, $18.78 cost per lead, with **77.0% of spend to women aged 25 to 44**. The trailing 30 days to 2026-09-04 tightens it to **79.8% aged 25 to 44 and 95% female**, on 98 active groups and $50,080.65. The served and delivered audiences agree. The work is changing what the account *says*, not who it looks for. `verified` live 2026-09-05.

**The most durable customer signal is the employer family.** 24 comments naming an employer, arriving steadily from March through August while nearly everything else went quiet after May. From 2026-08-15: *"Has anyone tried this? I pay quite a bit for an employee plan with a very high deductible. Is this worth it and is it widely accepted?"*

And the moment that decides the direction — on 2026-05-05, under `MOMS38 - 1 - V2`, one commenter answered another:

> "And if it's an employer insurance, you don't have to sign up with that employer insurance you can decline the coverage and go out and find your own insurance carrier."

**The brand has never said that sentence in an ad.** A stranger said it for free, under an ad the brand paid for, and in twenty months and 1,342 comments the brand has never replied once. `verified`.

**Using the words right is a credential in this category.** The audience corrects the ad's central term **103 times across 13 ads** — some identifying themselves as licensed brokers, one as a medical biller of thirty years. The brand owns a compliant authority it has never filmed: a real licensed partner advisor already inside its own funnel. Across 147 delivering ads, nobody credentialed has ever spoken. `verified`.

**The business model shapes every read.** Health For Moms does not sell insurance. It collects a mom's details, checks whether she qualifies in her state, and hands her to a pre-vetted partner agency whose licensed agent sells the plan. No pricing page, no checkout, the consumer never pays Health For Moms anything. Its own words: "It is not an insurance carrier — it is a connector." `stated`, section 1.

**The real competitor is the plan she already has.** The brand names employer-provided insurance as "the most common 'competitor' — the default option families already have." That is the hardest deal to beat, because employer premiums come out of payroll before tax. `stated`, section 1.

**Two things the brand's own record gets wrong about itself.** Its stated audience criteria have a live hand-typed override sitting in Slack that contradicts the written guidelines. And at least one profile in its canonical ICP list was written by Parker and then adopted by the brand — so it is not independent corroboration. Do not cite it as such. `verified`, 2026-09-03.

## Named preferences and standing instructions

- **Efficiency before volume.** The stated objective. When an efficiency finding and a volume finding conflict, efficiency wins. `stated`, intake 2026-09-03.
- **Two gates, always.** Never call an ad a winner on cost per lead alone. Name gate two as the open question every time. The team's words: *"CPL is important to scale but then we look at the lead quality as well before really scaling."* `stated`.
- **Never report ROAS, AOV, purchase value or add-to-cart.** Lead generation, zero purchase events. `verified`.
- **Always say "commenters under the brand's paid ads said," never "customers said."** Reviews and surveys are verified empty, Reddit is unreachable, and `author_name` is null on all 1,342 comment rows. Every persona in this brain is capped at mixed confidence because of it.
- **The spend-versus-efficiency tiebreak is unanswered.** When two ads in one ad set diverge on spend, **report both readings** — what the spend split implies about Meta's delivery, and what the cost-per-lead comparison implies about efficiency. Do not silently pick one. Re-ask when a real decision rides on it. `stated`, intake 2026-09-03.
- **Competitor tracking is deferred by the team's choice**, not by failure. Adding rivals in the Parker app backfills the whole branch without redoing anything else. `stated`, 2026-09-03.
- **No brief template captured yet.** Briefs follow the factory format in `parker-system/prompts/ideas-and-briefs/brief-creation.md` until the team supplies theirs.
- **No unit economics captured.** No revenue per lead, gross margin, lifetime value, payback window, or maximum tolerable cost per acquisition exists anywhere in this brain, and `list_custom_metrics` returns no revenue metric of any kind. So **"efficient" means better than this account's own trailing number, never profitable.** Say it that way.
- **The brand's own scoreboard:** under $15 per lead is "fantastic." The account has sat in the "meh" band for six straight months. `stated`.
- **The team names casting and creator sourcing as its number one creative bottleneck**, and production as its fourth. Recommendations that need no new casting are worth more here than their raw score suggests. `stated`, brand context.
