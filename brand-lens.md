# Health For Moms — brand lens

The standing overlay of Health For Moms' own knowledge. Loaded on top of the general creative-strategy method on every creative-strategy output. Where a line here conflicts with a generic rule, this line governs. Every entry carries its source and date and is marked **stated** (the brand told us, not yet independently checked) or **verified** (observed directly in their data, creative, or a confirmed correction). Add to this file whenever the brand teaches Parker something durable — a correction, a constraint, a preference, a what-worked — rather than letting it evaporate into a single conversation.

Seeded 2026-09-04 at the stamp step of the initial build. Everything below traces to the brand's own guidelines, the brand context document, the 2026-09-03 intake, or a live account pull. Nothing here is inferred to fill a gap; where the brand said nothing, the section says so.

## Voice and language

- **Banned words, verbatim from the brand's guidelines, section 8: "Words never used in creative: cheap, discount, policy, ACA, exclusions, guaranteed."** These are hard, not stylistic — they sit in the section the brand heads non-negotiable. `stated`, brand guidelines via `sub-context-docs/brand-identity-analysis.md`, 2026-09-03.
  - One live tension worth knowing: the brand's own emotional promise breaks its own list — *"It's not about **policies** — it's about protection, simplicity, and relief."* The word is used to reject the concept, which is almost certainly the intent, but the rule as written carves out no exception. Flag it rather than copying the pattern. `verified` observation, 2026-09-03.
- **Tone: five things the brand would never sound like** — a corporate insurance company; condescending or talking down; clinical or sterile; high-pressure sales; a call center script. The brand labels this list explicitly as *"stylistic preferences, not compliance rules."* Carry that distinction exactly: tone is a preference here, claims are rules. `stated`, guidelines section 7, 2026-09-03.
- **Say "commenters under the brand's paid ads," never "customers said."** Customer reviews and post-purchase surveys are verified empty, Reddit is unreachable, and `author_name` is null on all 1,342 comment rows. The only first-party language surface in this brain is comments under ads the brand itself paid for. `verified`, Phase 0 and Phase 1 pulls, 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-04.
- **Do not over-explain a deductible.** The brand's own rule: *"don't over-explain concepts the audience already understands (e.g., what a deductible is) — assume problem-awareness and reframe the cause instead."* The account's comments back this hard: "deductible" appears in **207 of 1,342** comments, "uninsured" in **2**. She knows the word. She wants to know why the number is what it is. `stated` for the rule, `verified` for the counts, 2026-09-04.

## Claims and compliance constraints

These outrank craft. The brand marks them itself: *"THIS SECTION IS NON-NEGOTIABLE. Every constraint listed here is a hard rule."* All verbatim, `stated`, brand guidelines section 8, 2026-09-03.

- **"Savings claims: always 'up to 30%' — never drop the 'up to'"**
- **"Deductible claims: always '$0 deductible option' or 'options with no deductibles' — never state it as a flat/guaranteed outcome"**
- **"Dont give any HARD guarantees"**
- **"No fabricated statistics; no named competitor attacks; no government/ACA references"**
- **"No scare tactics, no political or partisan language, no punching down at demographic groups"**
- **"The villain in any narrative is always the system/mechanism — never a named company or individual"**
- **"A doctor character in a script requires either a real licensed physician or adjusted language that doesn't imply a false credential"**

**Required disclaimers: none specified.** The brand context document says plainly *"No specific disclaimer text was provided."* The two claim qualifiers function as its de facto disclaimers instead. The account does carry an on-screen dramatization disclaimer in the winning scripts, which is one the brand practices without having written down. `stated` for the absence, `verified` for the practice, 2026-09-03.

**The live exposure, and it is the single most important line in this file.** The account is currently running copy that breaks the first rule at essentially full scale. A live sweep on 2026-09-04 for the flat string *"saving you 20% or more"* returned **127 of the account's 128 ad-name groups, carrying $100,066.79 of the trailing 90-day window's $100,067.42**. A separate sweep for *"Approved by thousands of Mom's across America"* returned the identical 127 groups and the identical $100,066.79 — same count, same dollar to the cent, which proves the two claims travel inside one shared body-copy block. Two variants of `B1 - Copy 7` already carry a Meta status of DISAPPROVED. **Never reproduce either string, and never carry the flat savings number forward into new copy to match the account.** `verified` live, 2026-09-04.

**Nothing in this brain states what the 20%, 24% or 30% compares against**, and the approval claim is backed by zero reviews, zero press, and no Better Business Bureau profile for the operating entity — two pieces of praise in 1,342 comments, both from women who were told no. `verified`, `sub-context-docs/reputation-analysis.md`, 2026-09-04.

**A second live exposure, found 2026-09-04, and it changes what Priority 2 means.** Seven static ad-name groups carrying **$35,234.91 lifetime and 2,871 leads at $12.27** are built as mock X posts with a **blue verified checkmark on an invented persona** and **printed engagement counts** — *"390 Retweets 7,314 Likes 751 Bookmarks"* and *"333.3k Views"* on `B1 - Copy 7`, *"503 Retweets 24 Quotes 2.5K Likes"* on five others. Three invented personas across the set: `@cindie_mama_23`, `@thehealthymom34`, `@Motherooffthree`. Printed engagement and view counts are statistics, and they are invented, against the brand's own **"No fabricated statistics."** `verified live`, 2026-09-04.

`B1 - Copy 7` is **one of the two statics the roadmap's Priority 2 wants to restart**, so **restarting that message is a rebuild, not a switch-flip.** The direction holds and the message is proven; the execution cannot be reused. And do not carry the old $11.79 forward as a forecast — the fabricated social proof is plausibly part of why it converted.

**A third rule breach, live and previously unrecorded.** `B2 - 10TH JUNE - Copy 1` carries *"no gimmicks, no government red tape"* against the brand's **"No government/ACA references."** Full record: `running-notes/missing-context.md`. `verified live`, 2026-09-04.

**Risk tolerance: 75** — the brand context document reads this as *"moderate-to-high tolerance for direct response approaches, while remaining within clear guardrails."* `stated`, 2026-09-03.

## Do's and don'ts

Standing preferences and prohibitions that are not legal gates. All `stated`, guidelines sections 7 to 9, 2026-09-03, unless marked otherwise.

- **"No pregnancy-only targeting — focus on moms with kids (not expectant mothers exclusively)."**
- **"Avoid corporate or overly clinical tone."**
- **"Avoid scare-heavy copy"** — the brand says it *"can use direct response principles but should focus on reassurance and clarity."*
- **"No named competitor attacks — the system is the villain, never a specific company."**
- **"No government/ACA references"** — the brand *"positions itself in the private market space and avoids any association with government programs."*
- **The hook-to-body contract:** *"the hook's promise must be paid off by the body. Mismatched hook/body is a top failure mode."* The brand names this as its own top failure mode, so treat a hook that the body does not pay off as a defect, not a style note.
- **Open loops must stay open:** *"hooks that deliver their own payoff collapse viewer motivation to keep watching. Prioritize mystery, tension, incomplete story, transformation, and countdown loops over closed-loop hooks."*
- **Specific numbers over round ones:** *"odd, specific numbers (e.g., $587) are more believable than round ones ($600)."*
- **Explain the cause, don't define the term.** The pool-pricing mechanic is the brand's stated causal explanation for why deductibles are high, and the education beat should explain *why* rather than define words — that is what sets up the private-plan pivot logically.
- **Direct response is welcome:** *"Scripts tend to do better with direct response principles."*

## What has worked and what has failed

- **Politics failed, and the brand tested it.** Its own verdict, verbatim: **"Anything political doesnt work."** `stated`, guidelines section 9, 2026-09-03.
- **The employer-plan switch worked and is currently switched off.** Two statics carrying the switching message returned **3,815 leads at $11.79 across $44,972.40**, converting **19.04% of their link clicks into leads** against video's **8.28%**. Both are dark today. This is the account's cheapest proven answer sitting in its own library. `verified` live, lifetime pull grouped by name, 2026-09-04.
- **Statics beat video on lead conversion in this account.** 19.04% versus 8.28% link-click-to-lead. Do not assume video is the default format here. `verified` live, 2026-09-04.
- **The live winners hold the claim rules correctly.** Every one of the eight top-spending ads pulled with full transcripts carries the qualifiers properly — *"save up to 30%,"* *"you may be able to get a $0 deductible plan."* The breakage is confined to the shared body-copy block, not the scripts. `verified`, 2026-09-03.
- **The account reaches the same woman it always has.** Lifetime **$743,218.09, 39,569 leads, $18.78 cost per lead**, with **77.0% of spend delivered to women aged 25 to 44**; the trailing 90 days tightens to **81.2% aged 25 to 44 and 95.3% female**. Twenty-one months of stable delivery. A recommendation that needs a different audience is a recommendation that needs new targeting evidence. `verified` live, 2026-09-04.
- **Spend fell 73.5% quarter over quarter while cost per lead held flat** ($374,508 to $99,267; $22.65 to $22.64). That reads as a budget decision, not a performance failure. Do not diagnose the drop as fatigue. `verified`, 90-day performance audit, 2026-09-03.
- **Concentration risk is real.** One ad carries 43.3% of all spend and four ads sharing a single text hook carry 60.5%. Worth knowing before any Open Enrollment ramp. `verified`, 2026-09-03.

## Positioning and audience nuances

- **This is lead generation, not ecommerce, and not an insurance carrier.** The brand's own words: *"It is not an insurance carrier — it is a connector."* It collects a mom's details, checks state eligibility, and hands her to a pre-vetted partner agency whose licensed agent sells the plan. The consumer never pays Health For Moms anything; there is no pricing page and no checkout. **Never report ROAS, AOV, purchase value or add-to-cart.** The account has zero purchase events by design and ROAS reads 0 everywhere. `stated` for the model, `verified` for the account shape, 2026-09-03.
- **The gap between the addressed woman and the reached woman is the central strategic fact in this brain.** The account addresses someone uninsured who wants rescuing. It reaches someone insured, broke, and unaware she is allowed to leave the plan her job picked for her. Four independent Phase-2 strategy inputs converged on this without seeing each other. `verified`, `strategy/strategic-roadmap.md`, 2026-09-04.
- **The sentence the brand has never said, said for free by a stranger.** On 2026-05-05, under `MOMS38 - 1 - V2`, one commenter answered another: *"And if it's an employer insurance, you don't have to sign up with that employer insurance you can decline the coverage and go out and find your own insurance carrier."* In twenty months and 1,342 comments the brand has never replied once. `verified` live, 2026-09-04.
- **The named mechanism is the MomSmart Coverage Method™**, described by the brand as tech that scans plans and matches each family based on needs, doctors and budget. It is trademarked in the brand's own materials; carry the mark. `stated`, guidelines section 7, 2026-09-03.
- **The brand's stated audience has a live override sitting in Slack that contradicts its written guidelines**, and its canonical ICP list contains at least one profile Parker wrote and the brand then adopted. Treat any persona claim as mixed confidence and say which source it came from. `verified`, 2026-09-03.
- **Open Enrollment opens 2026-11-01.** A season can move the buyer and the angle here, not just the offer.

## Named preferences and standing instructions

- **Efficiency before volume, and it is a sequence, not a tie.** The team's stated objective: fix cost per lead, then scale on the ads that proved out. **An efficiency finding outranks a volume finding when the two conflict.** Do not recommend scaling behind an ad whose cost per lead is drifting up, even if it produces the most leads. `stated`, intake 2026-09-03.
- **A winner has two gates and Parker can only see one.** Verbatim: *"CPL is important to scale but then we look at the lead quality as well before really scaling."* Gate one is cost per lead. Gate two is lead quality, and it lives with the partner insurance agencies where nothing in this brain reaches — the account fired **44 `Call` events across 16 ads against 4,421 leads** in the trailing 90 days, and `invitee_meeting_scheduled` returns no data at all. **Never call an ad a winner on cost per lead alone.** Say it clears gate one and name gate two as the open question, every time. `stated` for the rule, `verified` for the counts, 2026-09-04.
- **Read performance in Meta Ads Manager, in-platform.** No third-party attribution; Northbeam is not connected. Never imply multi-touch anywhere. `stated` intake, `verified` Phase 0 surface test, 2026-09-03.
- **On a spend-versus-efficiency split, report both readings.** Asked at intake; the team expressed no preference. Until they state one, when two ads in one ad set diverge, give what the spend split implies about Meta's delivery *and* what the cost-per-lead comparison implies about efficiency. Do not silently pick one. Re-ask when a real decision rides on it. `stated`, intake 2026-09-03.
- **"Efficient" here is relative, not absolute.** No unit economics exist anywhere in this brain — no revenue per lead, no gross margin, no lifetime value, no payback window, no maximum tolerable cost per acquisition. `list_custom_metrics` returns four auto-detected pixel events and zero formulas. So efficient means better than this account's own trailing number, never profitable. `verified`, 2026-09-04.
- **Three intake items are still open, none of them blockers:** the ad naming convention, the brief template, and unit economics. Until the brief template arrives, briefs follow the factory format in `parker-system/prompts/ideas-and-briefs/brief-creation.md`. The team's own tools came online mid-build and the answers are likely sitting in them. `stated`, 2026-09-03.
- **Campaign structure is an inference, not a stated convention.** Campaigns run under a `USHA - ABO - {SCALE|TEST} - MOMS` shape, ad sets named by audience cohort and iteration (`Moms43 - 4 (State Moment)`), ads suffixed by version (`- V1`, `- V3`). Treat as a working read until the team confirms. `inferred` from names, 2026-09-03.
- **The strategic roadmap is drafted and unapproved.** `strategy/strategic-roadmap.md` carries `status: drafted, awaiting review` and `approved_by: null`. Do Phase-3 work when asked, and carry the caveat that the lane comes from a roadmap nobody has signed. Never present a priority as agreed direction. 2026-09-04.
