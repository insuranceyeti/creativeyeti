# Idea evaluation — Health For Moms — 2026-09-04

Twenty ideas, graded against a roadmap nobody has signed.

## The call

**Read this first, because it governs the whole document.** The roadmap this grades against is **drafted and unapproved**. It carries `status: drafted, awaiting review` and `approved_by: null`, and the team was asked to approve it and expressed no preference. It is graded here as the best-evidenced direction available, and nothing more. **A re-approval, or a re-order of its three priorities, would re-order this shortlist.** If the team decides Priority 2 is really Priority 1, or that the older woman gets the lead slot after all, the four ideas below stop being the four. No priority named here is agreed direction. It is a proposal with good evidence under it.

With that said, here is the rank.

**1. `2026-09-04-you-dont-have-to-take-your-jobs-plan.md` — the permission line.** It is the only HIGH band in the bank, and it earns it on the brand's own money rather than on a hunch. Two statics carrying this message returned **3,817 leads at $11.79 across $44,993.83, turning 19.04% of their link clicks into leads**, re-pinned live by me today. That is $11.79 against the account's $18.78 lifetime blend and its $22.63 trailing quarter, and it sits 21% under the brand's own "fantastic" ceiling of $15. It clears gate one at real scale on tens of thousands of clicks. **Gate two — whether those cheap leads are leads a licensed advisor can actually close — is the open question, and it is the one answer that could invert this whole order.**

**But the asset is not reusable, and this is the finding of the run.** I pulled full creative analysis on both statics today. They are mock X posts in dark mode, each carrying a **blue verification checkmark on an invented persona** (`@cindie_mama_23`) and **fabricated engagement counts** printed on the image — 390 retweets, 7,314 likes, 751 bookmarks, 333.3 thousand views. Parker's own creative read calls them "manufactured high engagement stats" and "(simulated) engagement metrics." A lifetime sweep of every searchable ad field for the phrase "verified checkmark" returns **7 ad-name groups holding $35,234.91 and 2,871 leads at $12.27**, every one of them the same fake-post device; `B1 samar- Copy 1` carries it too and did not surface in that sweep, so the real exposure is **at least 8 groups and roughly $49,889 lifetime** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. The brand's own non-negotiable section bans **"No fabricated statistics."** So the message is proven and the vehicle breaks a hard rule. Restarting this lane is a rebuild, not a re-enable, and anyone who reads "restart the statics" as a switch-flip will ship the account straight back into the exposure Priority 1 exists to close.

**2. `2026-09-04-the-flat-confession.md` — say the shocking thing in a level voice.** The strongest MEDIUM in the bank, and the reason it ranks second is speed: it is a direction on delivery, not a shoot. Two instruments agree from opposite directions. From the category feed, **not one of the 17 hooks pulled from the relevant videos opens on crying, shouting or visible distress**, and the video carrying this exact register did 872,100 views. From the account's own money over the 30 days to 2026-09-02, calm openers delivered leads at **$13.29 to $24.45** while high-distress crying openers delivered at **$29.85 to $38.09, blending to $33.23 across six ads** — while posting the *higher* hook rates at 57.02% and 52.40%. They stop more people and cost half again as much. The account is currently building more of the expensive lane, six ads and climbing. This is the register Priority 2's message has to arrive in, and right now production is buying the opposite.

**3. `2026-09-04-red-against-green.md` — two numbers, two colours, no adjective.** MEDIUM, and it is the cheapest thing in the bank to make. It is a static, which is the format that turns clicks into leads at **16.57% against video's 8.28%** lifetime, and the brand already renders full-frame text graphics so it can be produced without a shoot or a casting call. It also does something Priority 1 needs: a comparison of two of the brand's own figures demonstrates value **without making an explicit claim**, which is exactly what a brand pulling two unsupported claims out of its copy has to replace them with. The counterweight is named and real — the account's one document ad buys its cheapest link clicks and then converts them **worst among real spenders, 5.97% against 12.25%**.

**4. `2026-09-04-the-correction-economy.md` — being publicly right is the credential.** MEDIUM, and it is the only idea in the bank that rebuilds the proof layer Priority 1 tears out. **103 comments across 13 distinct ad names** are people correcting the ad's central term, several signing with a credential — a licensed broker, an insurance agent, a certified application counsellor with thirty years in medical billing [`verified`, carried on the 1,322 denominator]. Against that, **across 147 delivering ads nobody credentialed has ever spoken**. The brand routes every lead to real licensed partner advisors, so a credentialed speaker is compliant by construction if the casting is real. Every credibility marker this brand carries today is its own assertion; this is the one idea in the pile that replaces an assertion with a person.

**What did not make the four, and why.** `2026-09-04-the-real-call-not-the-skit.md` is the first alternate and it is close. It holds the highest hold rate the account has ever recorded, by a wide margin — **24.48% hold against a 12% craft floor and the biggest spender's 3.81%** — and it sits on the largest video in the whole mining library at 6,200,000 views. It ranks fifth on two honest grounds: its three in-account tests deliver leads at $30.91, $22.90 and $16.65 on under about $2,100 of lifetime spend each, so the attention is proven and the lead cost is not; and the thing that makes the source work is a genuinely unscripted recorded call with a real institution, which is a question only the brand and its compliance people can answer.
