# The December reset — "we start all over"

## Idea

Every deductible resets on January 1, which means a family that has just fought its way to the end of its out-of-pocket maximum goes back to zero. Commenters name that moment with real feeling and the account has never spoken to it. The idea is creative built on the reset itself — the specific dread of watching a year of payments erase, rather than the generic dread of a monthly premium.

## Spark

A use case with a date already on it: she hates New Year's Eve for a reason nobody has ever put in an ad.

## Source read

The clearest verbatim, 2026-04-04, 6 likes: *"And then it's the end of the year, and we start all over. I literally hate NYE for this reason!"*

The same shape shows up as a trigger moment attached to real medical debt, 2026-03-31: *"It's still sucks! My deductible is $5000 and my out of pocket maximum is $7500. I owe that from a total emergency hysterectomy last year and starting over, cancelled all my prescriptions and just can't afford it."*

Roughly 5 comments carry the reset explicitly, on a corpus that is 64% concentrated in March and April, so it is almost certainly undercounted [as of `gaps-opportunities-inspo.md`, 2026-09-04].

Against that, December is the worst month in the account's entire readable series: CPM hit $29.34, cost per lead rose 39.8% from $13.08 to $18.28, and the share of landing page views becoming leads fell from 17.1% in November to 14.2%, on largely the same creative and the same funnel [as of 2026-09-04, thirteen consecutive monthly pulls].

## Winning elements

- **angle** — the reset, which is a date-stamped pain nothing in the library names.
- **emotional frame** — erasure. A year of paying, and the counter goes back to zero.
- **hook** — "we start all over" is customer language that already sounds like a first line.
- **pacing** — a countdown structure the category's own December content already uses.

## Justification

Thin on count, strong on specificity, and worth keeping because it explains something the account cannot otherwise explain. Something about the buyer changes between November and December and nobody knows what; the roadmap treats that as an open loop rather than a solved question. A message built on the reset is the first creative hypothesis anyone has offered for what that change is.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. She is not shopping — she is bracing. The overlay sits on any persona here, which is why this informs timing and offer rather than targeting.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits the brand's stated villain rule cleanly: the reset is the system, not a named company. The risk is the roadmap's explicit warning — do not buy December on the assumption that it is November's second half. This is an idea to layer, not a reason to ramp.
