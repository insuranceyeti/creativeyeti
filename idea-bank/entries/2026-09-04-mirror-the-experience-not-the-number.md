# Mirror the experience, not the number

## Idea

Open on a moment she has lived rather than a figure she can beat. The hallway. The hold music. The letter that says the anesthesiologist was out of network. A number invites her to top it; an experience leaves her nothing to dispute.

## Spark

She has a bigger number and she will post it — but she cannot out-bid you on the hallway.

## Source read

The two halves of the finding, both from the brand's own comment corpus [as of 2026-09-04].

**Mirroring a number produces a bidding war.** The `MOMS38 - 1` family opens on *"My deductible is $6,000."* The string "mine is" appears in **82 of 1,342** comments, 76 of them naming a specific figure, and **64 of those 76 — 84% — name a number higher than the ad's**:

- *"Wow only 6? Must be nice"* — 13 likes
- *"$6000???? Huh mine is $18000 .....where can I get the $6k"*
- *"6k....try 13k!"*
- *"I think I win mine is $9000"* — 3 likes, 5 replies

**Mirroring an experience produces recognition.** Roughly **12 clear recognitions in 1,342 comments** — under 1% — and almost all of them sit on the phone-call skit family:

> *"'It's out of network' 'But it's in the same building!' 'In a different hallway' 🤣🤣🤣🤣🤣 whoever came up with this ad deserves a raise!! Love this!"*

> *"Had a similar conversation recently with my soon to be former insurance company."*

The pain phrase attached to that same family names time rather than money: *"I literally was just on the phone for hours today figuring out something eith insurance...and still no answer."*

## Winning elements

- **hook** — a lived moment in frame zero, not a figure.
- **angle** — the experience as the shared ground.
- **emotional frame** — recognition instead of argument.
- **script** — the shape is a specific small absurdity, not a statistic.

## Justification

This is a cross-audit inference, layered on three upstream audits that each saw one half of it, and it is marked as an inference rather than a proof. What is `verified` underneath it: the 82 counter-bids and their 84% skew; the roughly 12 recognitions and their concentration on one cheap creative; and the fact that the recognised family holds 24.48% and 27.31% against a 12% floor.

`killer-performance-ads.md`'s zero-confusion rule explains the number problem exactly: a number the audience beats is not a promise, it is an invitation to disqualify the offer as unrealistic.

Note carefully what this does and does not say. The `MOMS38` family also holds attention better than anything else the account owns at scale. **The dead end is the specific device, not the grievance lane.**

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. Fits `pays-and-still-owes` first.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits the villain rule. The counterweight is the one that hangs over half this bank: the best-holding ad in this account produces a $30.91 cost per lead, in the brand's worst band. Deep attention here has not yet converted more cheaply, and whether that survives a real budget is genuinely unknown.

## Notes

The category feed mirrors the problem beautifully and does almost nothing else — its two highest-conversation videos are pure laments with no solution attached, at comment rates roughly twelve times the library's typical. Nobody in the category picks up the lament and hands the viewer something to do next. That join is the open lane: mirror the problem the way the feed does, then be the only one with a next step.
