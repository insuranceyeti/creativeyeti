# January first, and it all starts over

**concept_name:** January first, and it all starts over

**idea:** There is a dated moment in this category that nothing in the account speaks to. On January 1 the deductible resets to zero and everything a family paid toward it that year evaporates. Women in these threads name it with real venom, including one who says she hates New Year's Eve because of it. It is a trigger with a calendar date attached, it repeats every year, and it lands in the account's worst performing month.

"so by December just hit it...oh but its Jan. Now so it starts over...and they still never cover anything.... insurance is the biggest scam 😒"

Read those four together and the shape is a countdown that resets. She spends most of the year climbing toward a number, occasionally reaches it, and then loses the whole climb on a fixed date.

**justification:** The count is small and the timing of the corpus makes it almost certainly an undercount. Roughly **5 comments** carry the reset explicitly, on a corpus that is **64% March and April** [`verified`, `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md` and `personas/personas-profile.md`]. Nobody was reading this brand's comments in January, so the season that produces this feeling is the season the corpus barely samples.

What raises it above five comments is the account's own calendar. **December is the worst month in the entire readable series of thirteen consecutive monthly pulls.** Cost per thousand impressions hit **$29.34**, cost per lead rose **39.8% from $13.08 to $18.28**, and the share of landing page views becoming leads fell from 17.1% in November to **14.2%**, on largely the same creative and the same funnel [`verified`, `sub-context-docs/marketing-calendar-and-campaigns.md`]. Something about the buyer changes between November and December and nobody knows what.

And a much larger dated event sits right beside it. The enhanced ACA subsidies expire **2026-01-01**, an event that moved roughly 4.8 million people out of coverage they could previously afford, and this account has spent **$379.92 in its entire life** on creative that names a subsidy [`verified`, `sub-context-docs/category-and-market-research.md` and `strategy/strategic-roadmap.md`, drafted and unapproved]. `parker-system/creative-strategy-context/seasonality.md` is explicit that the useful question inside a season is never whether demand spikes, it is what shifts underneath it, and that evergreen winners stay on while seasonal creative layers in rather than replacing them.

**Evidence picture.** Five comments is a quote candidate, not a pattern, and it is marked that way. The account level seasonal evidence is `verified` and strong. So the moment is well evidenced and the *language* for it is thin, which is the honest way round to say it.

**stage_of_awareness:** Problem aware in December, and the whole point of the idea is that the calendar moves her. [`inferred`.]

**persona_or_audience_fit:** `pays-and-still-owes` carrying the `deductible-reset-dread` signal, which `personas/personas-profile.md` names as seasonal by nature. [`inferred`. The December account evidence is `verified` and the persona signal is named in the doc, but the language tying the two together is five comments on a corpus that barely samples January, so the fit is reasoned from a signal rather than shown in her own words.]

**brand_fit:** Careful. The reset itself is nameable inside the rules. The subsidy cliff is not, because the brand's own non-negotiable section bans government and ACA references [`stated`, `running-notes/brand-rules.md`], and the cliff cannot really be explained without them. So the idea splits in two: the deductible reset is fully writable today, and the subsidy story is a brand decision Parker does not get to make.

**notes:** The brand names Open Enrollment on 2026-11-01 as the most important date on its calendar. The account says something else. November 2025 took 5.4% of spend while March through May 2026 took 58.9% [`verified`, `strategy/strategic-roadmap.md`, drafted and unapproved]. This entry is the reason that argument is worth having before the budget is set, and the roadmap's own open loop 3 sits on it.
