# The persistent topic banner

## Idea

One block of text that names what the viewer is watching, held on screen for the entire runtime instead of vanishing at three seconds. Fourteen of the nineteen Relevant videos in the category library do this. It is the library's default solution, it costs nothing, and it is the single fix that the library's biggest underperformer needed.

## Spark

A viewer who arrives at second forty still knows what she is watching — that is the whole device, and it is free.

## Source read

The pattern, counted across the library [as of 2026-09-04]: **14 of the 19 Relevant videos hold one text block on screen for the entire runtime** rather than letting the hook text disappear at three seconds.

Executions worth holding side by side:

- @kaseyjaneanderson: `WE DON'T HAVE HEALTH INSURANCE family of 4`, top centre, bold white **serif**, held the full 88 seconds.
- @paumod: `PSA IF YOU'RE PREGNANT do this before you start shopping`, centre, high contrast, two type treatments in one block, held the full 73 seconds. It does three jobs at once — names the audience, makes a promise, and stakes an urgency claim with the word "before."
- @friencine: `A HILL I WILL DIE ON A SECOND TIME MOM!` top-centre for 46 seconds, **plus a second, timed label** — `Hospital Indemnity Insurance` appearing at 0:07, at the exact moment she says the unfamiliar term.
- @kclairemoore: `HAVING A BABY IN 2026` held for 2:02, plus the timed label `Indemnity Insurance` from 0:15 to 0:23.

That two-tier structure — a persistent filter plus a timed label for the word the viewer does not know — appears independently in two separate videos and looks like the library's default way of teaching a term.

The counter-example is the proof. @readra21's bill video took 213,100 views and produced the **lowest save rate of all 23**. There is no text hook, no overlay, no banner and no face. A viewer scrolling in at second six sees a piece of paper and has no idea what she is watching.

## Winning elements

- **visual** — one held block, high contrast, legible on a phone.
- **format** — the two-tier version: a persistent audience filter plus a timed label.
- **hook** — the banner is the hook for every viewer who did not arrive at second zero.
- **pacing** — it removes the cost of a viewer landing mid-video.

## Justification

The brand already does a version of this and has proof it works. Its held POV block — large white sans-serif, centre-aligned, held for the entire opener rather than flashed and dropped — is the account's signature and carries $87,991.09 across 20 ads at a $19.56 blended cost per lead with no measured decay across twenty uses [as of 2026-09-04].

But the account is inconsistent about it, and the inconsistency is measurable. It runs **two separate caption systems** with nobody having decided which is the brand's: white phrase-at-a-time centre captions on `moms54-3`, and yellow-with-black-outline word-at-a-time captions on the street interviews.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`, though a banner serves any stage. It is a structural device rather than a message, which is exactly why it is captured as a format and not as a concept.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits and costs nothing — this is an edit-level change with no reshoot. The risk is that a banner amplifies whatever is written on it, so a weak line held for sixty seconds is worse than a weak line held for three.

## Notes

Related: the account currently spends its entire measured watch window saying nothing — the first spoken word lands at 0:11, 0:10 and 0:18 on its three biggest recent files, against average play times of 4.03, 3.41 and 4.20 seconds. A banner is the cheapest thing that makes those silent seconds carry information. See `2026-09-04-open-your-mouth-in-the-first-second.md`.
