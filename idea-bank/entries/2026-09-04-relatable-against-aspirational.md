# The relatable pole against the aspirational one

## Idea

The account casts one pole of a two-pole axis. Its default is a stylish mother in good clothes outside a modern house with a large black door — someone the audience is meant to want to be. The other pole, someone the audience sees herself as, exists already in the same batch and has never been given a dollar. The evidence for testing it is not a hunch; it is a comment.

## Spark

The joint most-liked comment in the window is a mother saying the ad is not for someone like her, and the relatable version is already filmed and sitting at $14.07.

## Source read

**The aspirational pole**, `moms-65 1a`: a stylish mom in good clothes holds a young boy's hand and walks in front of a modern house with a large black door. The account has run this shape three times.

**The comment**, joint most-liked in the window, sitting under this casting family:

> *"I'm sure the mom that can afford matching outfits prob just pays in cash 🤦‍♀️"*

**The relatable pole, already filmed, never tested**: `moms-65 2a`, created 2026-08-27, **$14.07 of delivery**. A tattooed mother sitting on a nursery floor among children's books, holding a Paw Patrol book. Its sibling `moms-65 2b` runs a similar domestic setting with Hello Kitty books. Both are on the deductible-anger script and neither has ever been tested.

And the account's own numbers already point past its default casting. From `visual-vocabulary.md`'s open loop 4 [2026-09-04]: the cheapest lead and the highest CTR among the window's top spenders — **$16.07 and 3.15%** — belong to the account's only visibly mixed-race or Hispanic creator, who has a nose piercing and visible arm tattoos. The two best-holding talking-head units are fronted by women described as mid-forties and late forties to early fifties. The street interviews put two women in their forties or fifties in frame. Meanwhile almost every dollar at scale sits on a white woman in her late twenties in a bright house.

## Winning elements

- **creator** — the person the audience sees herself as, rather than the one she wants to be.
- **visual** — a nursery floor and a Paw Patrol book, which is a room nobody staged.
- **angle** — the same script, delivered by someone with no gap between her and the viewer.
- **emotional frame** — recognition rather than aspiration.

## Justification

`iterations.md` puts avatar last in the optimisation hierarchy — the lever you pull once angle and format are exhausted. This account has now exhausted angle and is starting on format, so the timing is right rather than premature. The specific evidence is a customer saying, in public, that the casting excludes her, and the counter-casting already exists as an unspent file.

A useful secondary signal to watch: a Facebook share of delivery above 50% on the relatable cut would say the casting reached a different pool rather than the same one, since the aspirational batch currently delivers 63.0% on Instagram.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. Fits `pays-and-still-owes`.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits. The strongest constraint in this whole area is not about relatability at all — it is that **no synthetic presenter belongs in either pole.** Six rejections across three separate years and four ad families, plus 32 comments across 7 ads calling the creative AI or fake, including *"This is the problem with AI online commercials. If you are using a fake person for your testimonial, it unintentionally sends a message to people that no real actual person would say this about your service."*

## Notes

The account's default younger woman is not failing — she buys leads at $21.55 on nearly two million impressions at a 45.84% hook rate. The problem is that she is doing every job at once. Read through `andromeda-v2.md`, uploading more of the same face in the same room produces different Creative IDs and the same Entity ID: shared delivery, higher frequency on the same women, no new reach. Keep her; stop making her the only door in.
