# One specific moment per headline

## Idea

The two cheapest leads in the account's entire static library are both one sentence about one specific moment happening to one specific person. Neither is a benefit. Neither is a claim. The idea is a family of headlines built that way — a named person, a named moment, a number that is odd rather than round.

## Spark

The account switched from talking to somebody specific to talking to nobody in particular, and paid about 45% more per lead to do it.

## Source read

The two cheapest statics in the library [as of `quarterly-whitespace-analysis.md`, 2026-09-04]:

- `5TH APR - Copy 8` — **$2,363.21 lifetime, 338 leads, $6.99 per lead**, the cheapest in the static library. Headline verbatim, typo included: *"I PAID $1,000 OUT-OF-PICKET FOR A 5-MINUTE ULTRASOUND."*
- `5TH APR - Copy 29` — **$7,188.80 lifetime, 747 leads, $9.62 per lead**. Headline: *"One ER visit could've wiped us out."*

Around them, the same grammar with a person named in it:

- `OMC-Health for Moms-[B1-C3-V3] - Copy` — *"I'm a single mom, and I didn't know my insurance was failing us... 😭"*, $16.11 per lead; its sibling ran $12.61.
- `B2 - 10TH JUNE - Copy 1` — *"If you're self-employed, between jobs, or just tired of paying too much..."*, $16.77 per lead, and it put **54.4% of its spend on women over 45** against 21.2% account-wide.
- `B1 samar- Copy 1` — *"Didn't know I could ditch my job's health plan… until I did."*, $11.89 per lead.

Now the thing the account bought instead: a woman in bed, no words, *"Wife of the year energy."*

The external reference for how far the grammar can go is Ethos's top-ranked static: a photograph of a clinical exam room with the whole ad written across it — *"Women in their 60s can get $200K life insurance with no med exam for $57/mo through Ethos — find out how,"* with the sample rate qualified in small print beneath. One sentence carrying a demographic, an amount, a friction removed and a price.

## Winning elements

- **headline** — one sentence, one moment, one person.
- **angle** — the specific over the general. Every one of these names a person and a moment.
- **format** — Headline Only, which needs no creator, no shoot, no script.
- **emotional frame** — a near miss ("could've wiped us out") rather than a promised outcome.

## Justification

$6.99 and $9.62 are the two cheapest leads in a 529-ad library, and they are the two most specific sentences in it. The pattern holds across the persona-named statics beneath them. And the brand's own guidelines say odd specific numbers read as more believable than round ones [`stated`, section 7].

The production case is the constraint the brand named itself: it lists casting first and production fourth among its bottlenecks and states a volume of one to five net-new concepts a month. A headline static needs one sentence and one photograph. That is the difference between five swings and twenty before November.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. The audience is set by the sentence rather than by the targeting — which is itself one of the roll-up's Tier 1 questions, and the self-employed static's 54.4% skew to women over 45 is the cleanest evidence for it.

## Brand fit and risk

**Compliance-conditional.** These headlines are testimonial in form and the brand has zero customers whose stories it holds. Any first-person claim must be either a real quoted ad comment, labelled as such, or written so it makes no factual assertion the brand cannot support. Savings stay "up to 30%" and the deductible stays a "$0 deductible option."

## Notes

`5TH APR - Copy 8` carries a typo, "OUT-OF-PICKET," and still bought the cheapest lead in the library. Worth noting rather than tidying: polish is not what is doing the work here.
