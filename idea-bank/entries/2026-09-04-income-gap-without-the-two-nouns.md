# The income gap, written without the two forbidden nouns

## Idea

A mother earns too much to qualify for help and too little to pay what the market asks, and she says so in the brand's own comment sections in a very particular register — flat, arithmetic, not self-pitying. Her whole story is normally told as a comparison between two government programs the brand's non-negotiable rules forbid naming. The idea is to write her story with those two nouns removed and find out how much of it survives, because the interesting answer is: most of it.

## Spark

Strip the two banned words out and the income number, the cliff, and the plan that costs more than the house note all still stand — that is a whole persona's story hiding inside the rule.

## Source read

The customer states the gate herself, plainly, without naming a villain:

- *"I don't make very much, just more than medicaid allows (which is far below poverty level)."* — 2025-08-03, under `B1 samar- Copy`
- *"There is no middle class on the marketplace unfortunately. You either pay next to nothing or almost all of it with no subsidy."* — 2025-08-09, under `B1 samar- Copy`
- *"Well you being a teacher is why. I'm self employed and can't find anything that doesn't have at least $5k deductible"* — 2026-04-02 under `MOMS39 - 2 - V2`, 7 likes
- *"Literally why I refuse to.get insurance as a self-employed person"* — 2026-03-22, same ad

Her category twin says the same thing in the same register on TikTok: @yolys.world, 14,700 views, opening *"If you're pregnant and you're looking for insurance and you didn't qualify for Medicaid like me, listen up."*

The brand has already run two ads in this lane, both created 2025-12-05, whose narrator says *"Making over 50k just disqualified this family from affordable healthcare"* and *"you're basically making a second mortgage payment every month."* Together they took $170.46 for 9 leads at $18.94 — inside the brand's own good band, on a volume too thin to call [as of `quarterly-whitespace-analysis.md`, 2026-09-04]. A sibling static, `B2 - 10TH JUNE - Copy 1`, headlined *"If you're self-employed, between jobs, or just tired of paying too much..."*, returned $16.77 per lead on $1,877.94.

## Winning elements

- **angle** — the cliff, stated as arithmetic rather than as grievance.
- **script** — "you're basically making a second mortgage payment every month" is the shape of her complaint in one line, already written and never funded.
- **emotional frame** — being punished for earning slightly more, which is a specific humiliation rather than generic money stress.
- **proof** — she reads everything before she calls anyone, so this angle wants something to read.

## Justification

She is the best-corroborated identity in a document with almost no corroboration available: a stated brand ICP, independent verbatims in the comment corpus, and a category twin in the organic feed. And she gets 0.25% of lifetime spend [as of 2026-09-04]. That combination — stated ICP, customer corroboration, near-zero investment — is the cleanest under-investment case in the vault after the employer switch.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. She knows exactly what her problem is and has already priced it. What she does not have is a number she can plan against. Audience fit is the self-employed mother; the brand's own context says she "will read every FAQ on a website before she picks up the phone" and "doesn't want to be sold to" [`stated`].

## Brand fit and risk

**Compliance-conditional, flagged deliberately.** The sharp version of her story names Medicaid and the Marketplace, and the brand's non-negotiable rules ban government and ACA references outright, with "ACA" on the never-used word list [`stated`, brand context section 8]. Seventeen comments name Medicaid and fourteen name the Marketplace [as of 2026-09-04]. The blunted version — income figure, cliff, second mortgage payment, no program names — is compliant and is what this entry captures. The blocked version is captured here too so evaluation can see exactly what the rule costs.

## Notes

There is an unresolved contradiction sitting under this idea and it belongs to the brand, not to Parker: the two December 2025 ads that speak her language say *"completely outside the marketplace system"* and *"not locked into Obamacare rules"* on screen, which reads as a breach of the brand's own rule, at good-band cost. Whether the rule is a hard legal line or a strong preference is roll-up loop M12 and it decides whether this whole persona is reachable. Do not brief the sharp version until that comes back.
