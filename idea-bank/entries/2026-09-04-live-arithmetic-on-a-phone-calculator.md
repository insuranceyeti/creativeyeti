# Live arithmetic on a phone calculator

## Idea

Somebody works the sum out on screen, in the phone's own calculator app, instead of presenting a conclusion. It is the cheapest credibility device in the entire category library — a phone doing ordinary arithmetic reads as a person working it out rather than a brand announcing a result.

## Spark

The calculator app is the payoff frame, and it costs nothing: she is not telling you the number, she is finding it while you watch.

## Source read

**@dannitangie, posted 2026-02-28. 28,200 views, 945 likes, 380 shares, 147 saves.** Small reach; here for the mechanic.

A woman in her late twenties or early thirties, long dark hair, floral loungewear, bright even light. She stands in front of a green screen and the background cycles through four things while she stays put:

- 0:16 to 0:34 — a hospital bill: `Total Charges $40,789.94`, `Balance Due $1,925.38`
- 0:35 to 0:54 — an anaesthesia bill: `Total billed $10,000`, `Total due $1,139.29`
- 0:55 to 1:10 — the baby's own hospital bill: `Billed $523.00`, `Outstanding balance $132.03`
- 1:11 to 1:42 — the iPhone calculator app, arithmetic visible on screen: `1,952.38 + 132.03 + 1,139.29 = 3,223.7`

She points at the numbers while she talks, so the eye is directed rather than left to hunt. The documents are real, from Orlando Health with Blue Cross Blue Shield details visible. Opening banner, held for the first fifteen seconds only: `How much does it cost to have a baby in the US with insurance?`

The structural surprise sits at the two-thirds mark, exactly where a list video normally loses people: *"what I didn't realize is that you also get billed for the baby. Like the baby gets billed and they send it to you."*

The older twin, @insurancebyalexa, does the same thing four years earlier, stacking `$80,000/yr`, then `x .09`, then `= $7,200/yr`, then `÷ 12`, then `= $600/mo` in the corner of frame.

## Winning elements

- **visual** — the calculator as the money shot, and a hand pointing at each figure.
- **proof** — arithmetic performed rather than asserted, which is what survives the sound being off.
- **format** — green screen, number ten on the brand's own untested-format list, executed in category on the exact subject matter the brand sells against.
- **pacing** — three bills in ascending surprise, then the sum. Running the largest bill last would make the baby beat an anticlimax.

## Justification

The comment corpus keeps producing the confusion this device answers: **103 comments across 13 ads** are people correcting each other about the difference between a deductible and an out-of-pocket maximum [as of 2026-09-04]. That spread across 13 ads matters more than the raw count. What those women are arguing about is the gap between a number they were quoted and a number they actually paid, and this reference does not explain the difference — it shows four documents and does the sum until it is settled.

The brand has proven the one-document version twice: the state list at $16.01 to $16.07 per lead, and the screenshot-overlay family at $27,775.42 lifetime for 1,213 leads at $22.90. Four documents in sequence behind one speaker is a materially cheaper production than a shoot with props.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. Fits `pays-and-still-owes` — the woman who already pays and cannot reconcile what she was told with what she owes.

## Brand fit and risk

**Compliance-conditional, and it is the same wall as every document idea in this bank.** The brand has no real customer bill to put on that green screen. The compliant substitute is a real ad comment carrying a woman's self-reported figure, labelled as a comment. Any invented number is out.

## Notes

The metaphor worth keeping from this source arrives at second sixteen rather than second zero: *"the first bill is definitely a jumpscare when it comes into the mail."* A customer reaching for an image rather than a description is where liftable language lives, and this brand has no review corpus to mine for any.
