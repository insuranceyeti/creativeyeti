# Colour as the argument — red billed against green paid

## Idea

Two numbers, side by side, in two colours, and the gap between them does the persuading with no sentence attached. The brand renders 41 state names in grey with green checkmarks and has never once put two numbers next to each other. Nowhere in this account does colour carry an argument.

## Spark

She never says the word expensive — the red and the green say it for her, and this brand already renders full-frame text graphics, so it is a design change, not a production one.

## Source read

Dr Ashley Hendry's cold call is the biggest video in the brand's whole mining library: **6,200,000 views, 661,800 likes, 208,000 shares, 22,700 comments, 108,770 saves**, posted 2025-11-12. That is 66.1% of the 9,376,300 views in the entire 23-video library. Like rate 10.67%, share rate 3.36% — roughly double the library middle on both.

The device: as each price comes back down the phone line she writes it in a notebook by hand, and the same number appears on screen in one of two colours. **Red for the billed price, green for the cash or all-in price**, stacked so the gap is visible:

- `SVD (everything) $5,730` in green
- `SVD $2,760` red against `SVD $2208` green
- `C-section $3,103` red against `C-section $2,482` green
- `SVD $5,840` red against `SVD $2,244` green

She never editorialises. The two colours argue for her. The notebook is doing a second job: a quoted price on screen is a claim, but a quoted price being handwritten in real time as a stranger reads it aloud is evidence.

A second, cruder version of the same device sits at @asap.kristy: at thirty-two seconds a screenshot reading `Amount Billed $1,100.00`, then nine seconds later a second screenshot reading `What I Owe $28.11`. Two real screens, held briefly, in the same frame position. 120,200 views.

## Winning elements

- **visual** — the money shot. Both numbers and the size of the gap land before a word is understood.
- **proof** — arithmetic the viewer performs herself, which is stronger than arithmetic she is told.
- **format** — extends the account's own proven document device rather than replacing it.
- **pacing** — the nine seconds between @asap.kristy's two screenshots is the tension; closing that gap to a snappy two-second cut removes the only suspense the video has.

## Justification

The brand already proves the underlying device works in its own paid: the Approved State List, whose visual event is a scannable document, returned $16.01 to $16.07 per lead with the account's highest CTR [as of 2026-09-04]. And `static-ad-design.md` explains why colour would extend it — hierarchy decides whether the message is seen at all, and the money shot is the image that makes the argument with no reading required. Two numbers side by side in two colours is a money shot for a category whose whole grievance is a price.

The capability read is favourable: the brand already renders full-frame text graphics with green checkmarks and already animates numbers inflating inside its claymation. *Inferred producible.*

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. It also serves the verification moment, because a number she can check is a different kind of proof from a number she is told.

## Brand fit and risk

**Compliance-conditional.** The brand cannot show a real customer's bill — it holds zero reviews, zero surveys, and no order data, so there is no real artifact to photograph. `problem-solution-video-ad-formats.md` is explicit that a statistic must come from the brand context, a real pulled review, an ad comment, or data the user supplied, with a flagged placeholder rather than an invention where none exists. What the brand does hold is 1,342 ad comments containing women's own self-reported figures. And savings must stay "up to 30%" with the deductible as a "$0 deductible option."

## Notes

Hendry's underlying argument — that the cash price beats the insured price — points toward leaving the category, which is the competitor with no ad account. The *device* transfers; the argument does not. Grade this as a visual mechanic, not as a message.
