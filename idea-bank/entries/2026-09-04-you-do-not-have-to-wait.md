# You do not have to wait for a window

## Idea

The brand sells private, medically underwritten plans, which means it has no enrollment window at all — a real, checkable, differentiating fact. In twenty-one months of advertising, no ad has ever said so. Zero of 2,122 ads in the account's history contain the word "deadline." Meanwhile the brand's own objection list names *"I should just wait for open enrollment"* as a timing objection that can cost months of delay, and commenters tell each other to wait for November. The idea is the one sentence that dissolves the wait.

## Spark

The product's oddest structural advantage — no window, ever — is the exact answer to the objection the brand already knows is costing it months, and it has never been spoken.

## Source read

From the roll-up [2026-09-04]: **zero of 2,122 ads** in the account's whole history contain the word "deadline." The string "open enrollment" appears in **1 of 1,322** comments. The brand's own stated objection list carries "I should just wait for open enrollment." And the category feed pushes the same way: 9 of the 19 Relevant videos in the TikTok mining library, 47.4%, were posted between October 1 and December 31, across three unconnected years, one of them opening *"It is December. Why is nobody talking about this right now?"*

So the calendar is telling this woman to defer at exactly the moment the category's content volume peaks, and the brand holds a fact that says she does not have to.

## Winning elements

- **angle** — a misconception to overturn rather than a value barrier to reframe, which `creative-strategy-fundamentals.md` treats as a different creative move.
- **hook** — the correction is itself the hook: an open loop the body then closes.
- **offer** — no window is a structural feature of the product, not a promotion, so it does not expire.
- **headline** — this is a one-sentence static as much as it is a video.

## Justification

Thin on the customer side and strong on the absence side, and the entry says so. The corpus barely mentions open enrollment, which the roll-up reads honestly as either a real-but-unspoken belief or an assumption the brand wrote about itself. What is not in doubt is the absence: 2,122 ads and not one deadline word. Scored Stakes 4, Confidence 3, Researchability 4, Novelty 5, total 16/20, Tier 2.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Solution aware, `inferred` — she knows plans exist and believes she is locked out until a date. The overlay sits on any of the three personas, which is why it is a timing and offer idea rather than a persona idea.

## Brand fit and risk

Clean. It says something true, checkable and specific, in a category where the brand's binding constraint is legitimacy. The risk is over-claiming: "no enrollment window" is a fact about private underwritten plans, and the copy must not slide into implying the plans are unconditional, because underwriting is exactly the gate that turns applicants away.

## Notes

This idea pairs naturally with the seasonal sequencing question. `seasonality.md` says evergreen winners stay running while seasonal work layers in, and the roadmap warns against buying December on the assumption it is November's second half. A "no window" message is the one seasonal idea in the bank that works *outside* the window as well as inside it.
