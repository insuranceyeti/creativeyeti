# One house cut in two — the comparison that shows the number

## Idea

Every POV ad in this account says a number out loud — "saved $400 a month" — over a picture of a peaceful bedroom. There is no visual evidence anywhere in the library. A side-by-side comparison makes the number the picture, and the brand has never run one in either window.

## Spark

The account asserts the saving and never shows it, and the whole fix is one frame with two halves.

## Source read

The Insurify reference. Three stacked bands. On top, white background, the wordmark, then a headline reading *"Stop overpaying for home insurance."* with the word "overpaying" picked out in orange. The middle band is split down the centre. On the left, a grey background, a white pill-shaped bubble reading *"$1352/yr Old insurance,"* and beneath it the left half of an isometric suburban house drawn entirely in grayscale, dull and lifeless. On the right, a bright orange background, a matching white pill reading *"$699/yr Insurify,"* and beneath it the right half of the very same house in full colour — orange siding, white trim, brown shingles, green grass. **It is one house, cut in two, half drained and half alive.** At the bottom, a wide orange button: *"Check my rate."*

Delivery signal: Meta Ad Library impression rank 7, climbed six places from rank 13 over 12 days. Launched 2026-08-04, running 30 days at the time of the read [as of `90-day-diversity-audit.md`, 2026-09-04].

Insurify runs `Us vs Them` on 29.8% of its 191 statics.

## Winning elements

- **visual** — one object rendered twice, so the comparison is a single image rather than two.
- **format** — Us vs Them, which the brand put on its own list of formats to test and has never run.
- **proof** — the number and the picture make the same claim at the same time.
- **offer** — "Stop overpaying" is a loss frame, which is a sharper motivator than "you could save."

## Justification

The reason conviction is medium-high rather than high is a compliance question this particular source ad happens to answer. Health For Moms cannot attack a named competitor — its rules say the villain is always the system or the mechanism. That would kill most Us vs Them executions. It does not kill this one, **because Insurify does not name anybody either.** The left side simply says "Old insurance." That is a category alternative, not a rival, and it maps precisely onto the system-as-villain frame the brand has already built its positioning on.

The brand's own reference frameworks already name loss aversion, listing Kahneman and Tversky among its standing references [`stated`, section 7].

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Solution aware, `inferred`. That is a stage the current mix addresses emotionally — 55.7% of spend is solution-aware, almost all of it wordless lifestyle POV — and never once with a number she can check.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits under the villain rule as long as the left side names a category alternative rather than a company. The compliance limit is the number itself: savings stay "up to 30%," so the left-hand figure has to be a stated situation rather than an implied guarantee about what she pays today.
