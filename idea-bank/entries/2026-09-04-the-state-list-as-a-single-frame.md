# The state list rendered as one designed frame

## Idea

The Approved State List is the most distinctive thing this brand owns and the only genuine document in its whole library. It has been shot five different ways in video. It has never once been rendered as a static — in an account that ran 529 of them. That is a per-format hole in the brand's most proven mechanic.

## Spark

A wall of state names reads as an official register in half a second and makes the viewer hunt for herself — and the account has never let it be a single frame.

## Source read

The live version, `moms-53 3`, launched 2026-08-25. A woman in her early thirties, dark hair tied back, a nose piercing, visible tattoos on both upper arms, in a grey rib-knit tank top. She stands in front of a clean white screen filled edge to edge with a long list of US state names in two columns under a header reading **"Approved State List ✔️"**. The list is not an overlay — it is the room. Before you process a word she has said, your eye is already hunting for your own state. Her first line: *"I'm so thankful for the mom that told me about this."* At 0:02: *"If you're a mom and you live in one of these states and you're tired of paying high deductibles, high premiums for health insurance for your family, listen up."*

**$2,041.29 in the 90-day window for 127 leads at $16.07, a 35.49% hook rate, a 3.15% CTR and a $0.69 cost per click** — the highest CTR of any ad in the window's top spenders and by far the lowest cost per click [as of 2026-09-04].

The five video grammars already on file: a full-frame grey-on-white card cut in for two seconds (`Moms43 - 4 - V3` at 0:33); a printed backdrop the creator stands in front of for the whole ad (`moms-53 3`); a clipboard graphic dropped over live footage of a mother and child (`moms-66-816` at 0:26); the release after a comedy punchline (`Moms36 - 3 - A - 2 - V4c` at 0:50); and a clean white card cut into the distress lane (`moms54-3` at 0:26). **Static: never.**

The external reference that shows where it could go is Insurify's Interactive static: a tall off-white frame, no photograph and no person, five pill-shaped rows each holding an age bracket in heavy black type with a small rounded orange button reading "See prices." It looks like a calculator someone built, not an ad someone bought. Compare Rates Today runs that format on 401 of its 545 statics, 73.6% of its whole static library.

## Winning elements

- **visual** — a document, which is the recurring visual event across the whole category feed.
- **format** — Interactive: something that looks tappable even though it is not.
- **offer** — geographic eligibility, made checkable rather than claimed.
- **comment mechanic** — the list invites a small act of participation almost nobody scrolls past.

## Justification

The brand's own words: *"State angle has worked very well if you see the account. Emotional and direct response."* [`stated`]. The account agrees on clicks. And a static version routes entirely around the account's worst weakness — an Interactive static has no hold rate to break; it fails or succeeds in the half-second scan.

The counterweight is sharp and belongs on the entry. The state list buys the cheapest clicks in the account and converts them **worst** at scale: 2,127 link clicks producing 127 leads, **5.97% leads per link click**, against `moms-63 2b`'s 12.25% in the same window. Its landing rate is 76.2% against 85.4% for the rest of the clean account. The likely reading, `inferred`: a full screen of state names makes people stop and hunt, and the ones who do not find their state click anyway and get turned away. Three commenters said exactly that in the window — *"Why is this being shown in IL if it's not on the list???😭🤦🏼‍♀️"*, *"Ofc Georgia ain't on there 🤣"*, *"What a joke!! They don't have quote for me In tx"*.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. Its real job is self-selection before the click, in an account where 81.2% of spend lands inside one twenty-year age band and the format mix cannot tell a 28-year-old with a newborn from a 45-year-old with teenagers.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits, and it is the one device the brand already knows is its own. The risk is the qualification problem above: unless the state requirement is legible and early, a static version buys the same cheap unqualified clicks in a cheaper wrapper.

## Notes

The same frame could sort on something other than geography — family size, or brackets of "what my deductible is now." That variation is a concepting decision, not part of this entry.
