# Lead with the fight, not the figure

## Idea

The account currently leads on a number. The runner-up direction, and the closest thing to a real alternative in the whole messaging read, is to lead on the fight instead: the denial, the appeal, the call that goes nowhere. It is the only creative in the account earning agreement rather than argument, and it has spent under 1% of the account's money.

## Spark

Mirroring a number gets you out-bid; mirroring the fight gets you recognised — and the account already has the proof of both sitting in the same library.

## Source read

The failure mode first, because it is what makes the alternative sharp. The `MOMS38 - 1` family opens on *"My deductible is $6,000."* The corpus response was not agreement, it was a bidding war. The string "mine is" appears in **82 of 1,342** comments, 76 of them naming a specific figure, and **64 of those 76, or 84%, name a number higher than the ad's** [as of 2026-09-04]:

- *"Wow only 6? Must be nice"* — 13 likes, 2026-03-27
- *"$6000???? Huh mine is $18000 .....where can I get the $6k"* — 2026-03-26
- *"6k....try 13k!"* — 2026-04-02

Now the alternative. Under the phone-call skit sit roughly 12 clear recognitions in the entire 1,342-comment corpus, and almost all of them are on that one family: *"'It's out of network' 'But it's in the same building!' 'In a different hallway' 🤣🤣🤣🤣🤣 whoever came up with this ad deserves a raise!! Love this!"* and *"Had a similar conversation recently with my soon to be former insurance company."* That family holds 24.48% and 27.31% of viewers against a 12% craft floor and a 3.81% top spender [as of 2026-09-04].

## Winning elements

- **angle** — the fight rather than the figure.
- **hook** — an experience she has stood inside, which she cannot dispute or outbid.
- **emotional frame** — recognition. "That happened to me" instead of "mine is worse."
- **format** — the two-role call, the split-screen reaction, the comment response — all of them carry a fight better than a montage does.

## Justification

Two things make this more than a preference. First, the account's own recognition evidence is concentrated on one cheap creative, which means the mechanic reproduces where the money is not. Second, `killer-performance-ads.md`'s zero-confusion rule explains the number problem exactly: a figure the audience beats is not a promise, it is an invitation to disqualify the offer.

The counterweight is real and the messaging input states it plainly: the recognised version is a scripted comedy skit that produces a $30.91 lead, in the brand's worst cost band, on under 1% of account spend. What would settle it is a funded run of the denial format at real spend with the resolution built in, measured on cost per lead against the calm openers in the same window.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. Fits `pays-and-still-owes` first — she is the one who has made the call.

## Brand fit and risk

The villain is the process, which satisfies the brand's rule. The risk both `ad-account-analysis.md` and `killer-performance-ads.md` name: a grievance that dramatises the fight without resolving it encodes the anxiety against the whole category and gives her nowhere to go. Whatever gets built must land the resolution inside the ad.

## Notes

This is the direct competitor to the roadmap's Priority 2 lead call and should be graded against it rather than beside it. Note that the register question is separate from the angle question: the account's calm openers delivered $13.29 to $24.45 per lead while the high-distress crying openers blended to $33.23 while posting *higher* hook rates [as of `monthly-hook-audit.md`, 2026-09-03]. A fight told calmly is not the same idea as a fight told through tears.
