# A number I can plan against

**concept_name:** A number I can plan against

**idea:** Sell predictability instead of savings. For the self employed mother the promise that lands is not "you will pay less," it is "you will know what you are going to pay." She runs a business. She budgets. What breaks her is not the size of the number, it is that the number is unknowable until a bill arrives. Every dollar this account has ever spent has promised a smaller number. None has promised a knowable one.

**source_read:** No source media. The seeding language, verbatim from commenters under the brand's paid ads:

"There is no middle class on the marketplace unfortunately. You either pay next to nothing or almost all of it with no subsidy."

"Well you being a teacher is why. I'm self employed and can't find anything that doesn't have at least $5k deductible." (7 likes.)

"This year it took 35 days to hit our 8k OOP. Next year it could take as few as 1 depending on how prescriptions hit."

"Except the premium for that policy costs more than my house note. Who can afford that? If you get a lower premium then your deductible is much higher and no one can afford the higher deductible so either way people are screwed. Broken system."

Read those four in a row and the complaint is not really about size. It is about the fact that no arrangement she is offered produces a figure she can plan a year around.

**justification:** The persona doc reaches this on its own, from a different direction, which is what makes it worth logging rather than a hunch. `personas/personas-profile.md` names the emotion that moves `built-it-herself` in one line: "wanting a number she can plan against. Not relief, not pride. Predictability." Her voice signature backs it, because she describes the mechanism that traps her rather than her feelings about it.

She is also the best corroborated identity in a doc with almost no corroboration available, because she matches a stated brand ICP *and* has independent support in the comments, which is rare here [`verified`, `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md`]. And she gets **0.25% of lifetime spend** [`verified`].

The rest of the justification is what the account has never promised. Its trailing quarter emotion tags run **Pride at 49.6% of spend and Hope at 38.8%**, and its awareness split is **Solution Aware 55.4% against Problem Aware 38.8%** [`verified live` 2026-09-04, carried in `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md`]. Most of the money sits on the outcome. Predictability is neither pride nor hope, and nothing in the library carries it.

**Evidence picture.** No source media, one thin persona, four supporting comments, and a documented spend gap. `personas/personas-profile.md` marks `built-it-herself` as thin and emerging and names only four behavioral signals for her, saying plainly that four is honest rather than thorough. Confidence: **thin**.

**stage_of_awareness:** Solution aware. She has already read every FAQ before she picks up a phone. The brand's own document says she will read everything before she calls, and the account gives her nothing to read [`stated` for the ICP, `verified` for the absence].

**persona_or_audience_fit:** `built-it-herself`, carrying `reads-before-she-calls` and `income-gap-rejection`.

**brand_fit:** Good on the rules, hard on the substance, and the substance problem is the interesting part. Predictability is a promise about the product, and this brain does not know enough about the product to say whether it can be kept. What is known is that the funnel gives her no prices at all before she hands over a phone number, and that is the single loudest friction in the corpus. "Just give some pricing without making people sign up 🙄." "Why can't you give a quote without my email!" "Id be way more interested if I didn't have to talk on the phone immediately tbh." A predictability promise from a funnel that shows no numbers would read as the opposite of what it says.

**notes:** Her full story runs through a comparison between two public programs the brand's own non-negotiable rules forbid naming, and commenters name those two 17 and 14 times [`verified`]. `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md` works out what survives that rule, and the answer is most of it: the income figure, the cliff itself, the plan that costs more than the house note. This entry is deliberately written to need neither noun.
