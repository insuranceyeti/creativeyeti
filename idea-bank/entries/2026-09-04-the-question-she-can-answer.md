# The question she can answer

## Idea

Open on a question the audience can answer *for you*, not a question only the brand can answer. The distinction is the whole idea, and the library contains both versions with very different results.

## Spark

Eighteen seconds, one question, no answer, no product — and 557 replies, because the only thing left to do was reply.

## Source read

**@molly_daw, posted 2025-11-23. 31,200 views, 999 likes, 30 shares, 557 comments, 49 saves.** Comment rate **1.79%, tied for the highest in the entire library and roughly twelve times its typical rate.** Share and save rates near the floor. Eighteen seconds.

A woman in her late twenties or early thirties, hair pulled back, in a blue and white tie-dye sweatshirt, sitting on a neutral couch. A large round wall mirror behind her, warm indoor light. Handheld, eye level, visible shake. **Her hand is resting on her head** — that is the entire visual hook and it arrives before she speaks. Exhaustion delivered by posture rather than expression.

Standard platform subtitles, centre screen, white, mirroring the audio: `Hey, how are y'all affording kids?` then `I can barely take care of myself,` then `me and my husband can barely afford ourselves.` No banner, no graphic, no artifact, nothing to look at but her face.

Three segments. One, the question. Two, the personal admission that qualifies her to ask it: *"I can barely take care of myself, but me and my husband can barely afford ourselves. I feel like everywhere I turn and look, a new friend, family member or neighbor is having a baby."* Three, the question again, three times, degrading: *"How. How? What's going on? How are y'all doing this?"*

The counter-example sits in the same library. @nothingeversticks asks *"Do you really know what your health insurance pays for?"* on 13,100 views, a 2.26% like rate and **zero shares**, and never answers it. Both leave the question open. The difference is that @molly_daw's is one the audience can answer for her and @nothingeversticks's is one only the brand can answer.

## Winning elements

- **hook** — a genuine question rather than a rhetorical one. The audience can hear the difference.
- **script** — the degrading repetition, which is the sound of someone giving up on getting an answer.
- **pacing** — eighteen seconds, three beats, no filler.
- **comment mechanic** — with nothing to save and nothing to show a friend, the only thing left is to answer.
- **emotional frame** — exhausted rather than distressed, a third register the account has never tested.

## Justification

For a lead-generation account with zero purchase events, a reply is the closest free behaviour to a form fill, and this video produced 557 of them from 31,200 views by offering absolutely nothing. The account's own register split — calm openers at $13.29 to $16.01, high-distress at $29.85 to $38.09 — puts this exhausted middle register between two poles it already knows.

The honest limit, and it is large: this creator is **not a mom**. Her own hashtags are `childlessmillennial`, `millennials`, `dink`. She showed up unbidden in a scrape seeded with mom-and-insurance keywords and out-talked every mother in it. The brand targets moms exclusively and all five stated ICPs already have children.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. The audience-fit read is the weakest in this bank and is marked as candidate signal: one source, one video, and an audience the brand may not be able to sell to.

## Brand fit and risk

The mechanic transfers; the video's structure has no segment four. It names the problem and stops. `adapting-scripts.md` says a reference that is ninety-five percent right with no slot for the solution needs a segment added where it makes sense, and the brand's version of that added beat is a second voice — which is exactly the Comment Response structure, and the account has never run either format.

The other risk is timing. The brand's own objection list includes "I should just wait for open enrollment," and a video that only names the problem gives a woman waiting for November nothing to do today.

## Notes

`hooks.md` is firm that an ad must answer the question it asks. This entry is a hook mechanic with a known missing half, captured with the missing half named rather than papered over.
