# Two people in one frame

## Idea

Every face in this account is one woman alone, talking to her own phone, across 147 delivering ads. Every configuration that puts a second character in frame or on the line clears or nearly clears the 12% hold floor. Every configuration that leaves one woman alone with her phone sits under 6%. The idea is the second body — a conversation the viewer overhears rather than a monologue she is addressed by.

## Spark

Put a second person in the frame and the account's whole hold-rate problem changes shape, and it is proven four different ways already.

## Source read

The four proofs, all from the brand's own library [as of 2026-09-04]:

1. **The two-role phone skit.** One actor, two characters, one cut. 27.31% and 24.48% hold.
2. **The podcast pair.** `MOMS25 BLAST - X1 - Copy 2`, two women in a podcast studio talking to each other. **$3,483.03, 175 leads at $19.90, 46.86% hook, 10.29% hold.** Created 2025-11-24 and never repeated in ten months.
3. **The street interview.** `Moms40 - 3 - V4`: a young man in a light green sweatshirt in an urban park holds a microphone branded "Weekly Health" and approaches two women in their forties or fifties standing together in the same frame. He asks *"Do you feel like doctors listen to you or just the computer screen?"* They answer: *"It's like turn and burn. Just sort of rushing, not believing what we're saying."* Its sibling `Moms40 - 4 - V6` ran a 48.69% hook and an **11.46% hold** on $108.14.
4. **The hand-off pair, which does not count.** Two different women in one ad who never share a frame, joined by one hard cut. This is the account's default and it sits at the bottom of the hold table — proof that the mechanic is the shared frame, not the second person.

For contrast, the account's biggest spender plays for about four seconds and holds 3.81%.

## Winning elements

- **format** — a conversation, in any of its containers: skit, interview, podcast, or a call.
- **visual** — two bodies in one shot, which nothing at scale in this account has.
- **pacing** — a second voice creates natural cuts, and `hooks.md` puts the pacing guide for this age band at a cut every three to four seconds. The account gives its biggest ad one cut in eighteen seconds.
- **creator** — it lets an older woman and a younger woman appear together rather than alternately.

## Justification

Read through `andromeda-v2.md` this is more than a watch-time question. Meta groups visually similar ads under one entity ID, weighing the first three seconds most heavily. An account where every opener is one woman at arm's length in a bright room is feeding the algorithm far fewer distinct things than its ad count suggests. Putting a second body, a second voice and a second setting in frame is a Tier 1 format change in that doc's hierarchy — the highest-impact kind of differentiation available.

The counterweight: hold and cost per lead run backwards in this account, and every one of these formats sits at small spend.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. It also happens to be the cheapest way to buy some of the whitespace around `already-knows` — the street interviews put two women in their forties or fifties in frame without spending a lead slot on her.

## Brand fit and risk

Production-proven on all four counts, which means no capability question. The street interview is the expensive one: 8 ads, $1,172.43 lifetime, a $37.82 cost per lead, and it needs a location and a shoot day, which is a bottleneck the brand has named.

## Notes

Two upstream audits recorded public shooting as an *absent* capability. It is not absent, it is abandoned — a different fact with different consequences. The correction is `verified` from a tag-filtered pull plus a frame-level read of `Moms40 - 3 - V4`.
