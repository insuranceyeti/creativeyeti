# The cash price comparison — "$4,600 if I use insurance"

## Idea

An angle the account has never run at scale, sitting in a file that took $46.13: the moment a person finds out the price is *higher* with insurance than without it. It is the single most counter-intuitive fact in the category and the account has one starved file carrying it.

## Spark

The best cost per lead in its whole batch, on an angle the account has never funded, buried under a winner-takes-all drop.

## Source read

`moms-65 1c`, created 2026-08-27. A close-up to camera. It opens: *"I'm getting an MRI and the price if I use insurance is $4600."*

**$46.13 for 3 leads at $15.38** — the best cost per lead in the eleven-file Moms65 batch, on far too little spend to call [as of `biweekly-iterations-report.md`, 2026-09-04]. The batch's winner, `moms-65 1a`, took **93.4%** of the batch's $1,375.03. The remaining ten files split about $91.

The same mechanic is the biggest thing in the category feed. Dr Ashley Hendry's cold call, at 6,200,000 views, exists entirely to compare a billed price against a cash price. @readra21's bill video pans from total charges of $55,623.00 to a cash-pay balance of $1,404.00. And the account's own claymation `Moms46 - V1` opens on a narrator asking *"Did you know your hospital bill is cheaper if you don't use your insurance?"* — the only ad in the library that stages the comparison as a demonstration, at 13.26% hold.

The customer says it too: *"All insurance is a scam and robbery but a high DED and OPM with high coinsurance rates is absolutely outrageous. You are better off being self pay and asking for selfpay pricing than using insurance."* And, with 14 likes: *"I would rather have no insurance you would get better self-pay rates then you do with 'having coverage'."*

## Winning elements

- **hook** — a single arresting number attached to a real, ordinary procedure.
- **angle** — insurance costing more than no insurance, which is the category's most shareable fact.
- **proof** — two numbers, checkable, requiring no adjective.
- **visual** — the comparison renders as a static as easily as a video.

## Justification

Three independent surfaces carry the same mechanic: the account's own starved file at the best cost per lead in its batch, the account's own claymation demo at the best hold in its window, and the category's single biggest video. That is unusual convergence for an angle with $46.13 behind it.

The evidence is genuinely thin — three leads — and the entry marks it that way.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. Fits `pays-and-still-owes`.

## Brand fit and risk

**Compliance-conditional and the risk is strategic, not only legal.** The natural conclusion of "insurance costs more than cash" is to stop buying insurance, which is the competitor with no ad account, no brand and no sales rep — 33 comments across 10 ads argue exactly that. Any brief built on this angle has to land somewhere other than that conclusion, and the brand has already written the line that does it: *"And before you ask, yes it's real insurance. Not a health share, not some discount card."*

## Notes

The reason this file was never tested is not creative. Three separate drops, three weeks apart, each funded a single file: Moms53 gave 99.75% of its batch spend to one ad, Moms65 gave 93.4%, and the fifteen-file moms 68 batch drew $4.23 in total. If batches keep collapsing onto one file within hours, the constraint on this brand's learning rate is the ad-set structure rather than the creative. Give this its own ad set and its own budget or the test will not happen.
