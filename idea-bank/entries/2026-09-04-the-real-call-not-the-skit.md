# The real call, not the skit

**concept_name:** The real call, not the skit

**idea:** A doctor films herself phoning hospitals to ask what a delivery costs a self pay patient, and the video does 6.2 million views. The mechanic is not the lab coat. It is the phone call: one side of a conversation, an audible pause, and a stranger's voice supplying an answer the viewer has personally wondered about. The brand has already built this format three times and it produced the best hold rates in the account's history. Every one of its versions was a comedy sketch performed by actors. The idea worth holding is the same mechanic with the call left real.

**source_read:** A woman in her thirties in a white lab coat and a navy medical polo sits in her own examination room. Neutral wall, abstract painting, examination table behind her, bright overhead light, a practice logo in shot. She is already mid phone call when the video starts. Across the top of frame, black text on a white box: "Pregnancy Self pay pricing." She is calling hospitals and pretending to be a patient with no insurance.

The rep on the other end starts reading out numbers. "$5,730.37. This is for a routine vaginal delivery with pre and post-care in the hospital." She writes each figure on screen as it lands, **red for the price billed with insurance in play and green for the cash price**, stacked so the two sit side by side. Then she keeps pushing. "Does that cover the hospital facility fee or is that just the OBGYN group fee?" "It's both. It includes all of it." "How much of a discount is that from the normal price?" "That is the normal price. Actually gives all self-pay patients that rate." "Is the insurance price usually more?" "It depends on the insurance."

She never says the word expensive. The two colours do it.

**Evidence picture, including the part that argues against it.** All three of the brand's versions are scripted. The team's own ad set is named "20k skit v2," and the transcript of `MOMS38 - 2 - V3` has both voices performed by actors, including the line "You need to be declared clinically dead and revived twice" [`verified` from the script block]. It is genuinely funny, it grips harder than anything the brand has ever made, and it produces a **$30.91** lead, which sits in the brand's own worst tier. So the format's attention is proven and its cost is not. Confidence: **strong** on the mechanic, **thin** on the lead economics, and all three tests sit under about $2,100 of lifetime spend so a handful of leads swings each number.

**stage_of_awareness:** Problem aware to solution aware. The call assumes she already knows the system is confusing and shows her what a real answer sounds like. [`inferred`.]

**persona_or_audience_fit:** `already-knows` first, since the correction economy proves she rewards being right. `pays-and-still-owes` second, since the account's own phone skits are the one creative family that earned recognition rather than argument, roughly 12 clear recognitions in 1,342 comments [`verified`, `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md`].

**brand_fit:** Strong on compliance, gated on production. The brand's rules require a doctor character to be a real licensed physician or the language to imply no false credential [`stated`, brand context section 8], and it already routes every lead to real licensed advisors. Two constraints are real and named rather than buried. Every filmed unit in this account carries a paid actor disclaimer. And the gaps audit lists this in its out of play section for one specific reason: a genuinely unscripted recorded call with a real institution is the one thing in Hendry's video that cannot simply be restaged [`inferred`, routed to the brand]. Whether a partner advisor can be recorded doing real work is a question only the brand can answer.
