# Fund the phone call — the best hold in the account, at 0.5% of spend

## Idea

One actor plays both sides of a call to her insurer. It holds 27.31% and 24.48% of viewers, against a 12% craft floor and a 3.81% top spender. It has taken $3,774.02 in the account's whole life, which is about half a percent of everything ever spent. Nothing else in this library clears the hold floor at any budget. The idea is to find out what happens when it gets a real one.

## Spark

The brand built the mechanic that holds people better than anything it has ever made, proved it twice, and then stopped spending on it in March.

## Source read

`MOMS38 - 2 - V3` and `V4`, both launched 2026-03-11, in an ad set the team itself named "Moms38 - 2 - 1 (when you call your insurance)".

A woman in her thirties in a pink sweatshirt stands outside on a porch holding a phone to her ear, visibly annoyed. On-screen text: *"When you have to call your health insurance 🙄"*. She says *"I'm calling to see why this wasn't covered when y'all said it would be."* Three seconds later it cuts to the same actor playing the other end of the line, in a blue t-shirt and a headset, typing at a laptop in a living room, deadpanning:

> *"Hmm it looks like it's not covered cuz it happened on a Tuesday."*

The bit escalates: coverage only on Mondays, Wednesdays and federal holidays that fall on a Friday; *"For access to coverage, not necessarily coverage itself"*; *"You need to be declared clinically dead and revived twice."* A sibling version uses a red pencil as a makeshift microphone. At 0:54 the same actor, now in a beige sweater in a kitchen, drops the bit and pitches straight to camera.

V4: **27.31% hold on a 55.57% hook, $2,172.45 lifetime, 95 leads at $22.87**, 15-second average play. V3: **24.48% hold on a 51.78% hook, $1,823.59, 59 leads at $30.91**, 13-second average play [as of 2026-09-04].

## Winning elements

- **format** — the two-role solo skit, which needs one actor and two wardrobe changes.
- **hook** — one side of a phone call with an audible pause where the reply should be. `hooks.md` names that as a first-second mechanic outright.
- **script** — escalating absurdity, each denial worse than the last.
- **pacing** — the cut between the two roles does all the work; no second camera required.
- **emotional frame** — recognition rather than argument. She has stood in that call.

## Justification

Two things beyond the hold rate. The comments prove the mechanic lands on the persona: of roughly 12 clear recognitions in 1,342 comments, almost all sit on this family — *"'It's out of network' 'But it's in the same building!' 'In a different hallway' 🤣🤣🤣🤣🤣 whoever came up with this ad deserves a raise!!"*. And the category's biggest video is the same mechanic played straight, with a real hospital employee on the other end of the line, at 6,200,000 views.

The honest counterweight: $30.91 per lead puts the account's best-holding ad in the brand's worst cost band. Hold rate and cost per lead run backwards in this account, and all three high-hold formats sit under about $2,100 of lifetime spend, so a handful of leads swings each number. This is a funded test with a clear question, not a scaling call.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits the villain rule exactly — the antagonist is the process. The production risk is the cheap part: one actor, two rooms. The risk that matters is the unresolved one: whether deep attention here converts at all, or whether it simply costs more.
