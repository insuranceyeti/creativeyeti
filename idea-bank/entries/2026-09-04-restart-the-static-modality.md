# Restart the statics — the account's cheap half, switched off

## Idea

This account has run 529 static ads that bought 13,735 leads at $12.30 apiece, and it has not spent a dollar on one in 180 days. Three separate 90-day audits described the account as having never run a static, and a lifetime pull disproved all three. The idea is not "try statics." It is that the account's cheapest half is production-proven, dark, and needs restarting rather than inventing.

## Spark

The cheapest lead this brand ever bought came from a black frame with white text, and the whole modality went dark around March.

## Source read

The numbers, from three matched lifetime pulls [as of `visual-vocabulary.md` and `gaps-opportunities-inspo.md`, 2026-09-04]:

| Modality | Groups | Spend | Leads | Cost per lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static | 529 ads / 308 name groups | $168,907.73 | 13,735 | **$12.30** |
| Video | 1,597 ads / 1,259 name groups | $574,310.36 | 25,834 | $22.23 |

Of that static spend, $74,539.28 and 5,712 leads at $13.05 ran in the last 365 days and **$0.00 in the last 180**.

The direction of the click-through rate is what makes this a real finding rather than a flattering one. Statics convert **16.57% of link clicks into leads against video's 8.28%**, while video wins the click by about 30%. If statics simply bought cheaper junk, their click rate would be higher and their conversion lower. It is the reverse.

The static grammar is narrow and repeatable: a black ground, white type, either a mockup of a social post or a serif hook word over sans-serif body copy. No photography, no person, no motion.

## Winning elements

- **format** — the single designed frame, which has no hold rate to break. It fails or succeeds in the half-second scan.
- **headline** — the account's static library is where the *specific* sentences live, and the specific sentences are where the cheap leads are.
- **offer** — the claim carried as a first-person quote rather than a third-person assertion.
- **angle** — statics let the account take twenty swings in the time a video shoot takes one.

## Justification

The evidence is bought and paid for at real scale on two independent ratios, tens of thousands of clicks on each side. And the constraint it routes around is the one the brand itself named: casting is its number one creative bottleneck and production is fourth, and a headline static needs neither. The brand's own guidance permitted it — *"We want to focus mostly on video ads. MAYBE some image ads are fine to find angles"* [`stated`].

`ad-account-analysis.md`'s caveat survives and is worth carrying: statics skew toward the bottom of the funnel and should not be judged against top-of-funnel video on a level field. The gap narrows under that caveat and stays the largest one in the account.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`, though the modality can carry any stage. Fits every persona because it is a container.

## Brand fit and risk

The risk is not creative, it is unknown. **Nobody wrote down why the statics stopped.** If it was a staffing or agency change, this ships this week. If something was learned — a lead-quality problem, a platform issue — that is a hard constraint nobody recorded and it overturns the largest piece of the plan. This is a brand-routed question and it gates the whole entry.

## Notes

There is a second, sharper problem sitting inside this one: every cheap lead this account ever bought came with a flat savings claim the brand's own non-negotiable rules forbid. `B1 samar- Copy` at $12.21 carries *"saving you 20% or more"*; `B1 - Copy 7` at $11.74 carries a flat *"24% cheaper"*, with two of its five variants at a Meta status of DISAPPROVED. So restarting statics and repairing the claim are the same conversation. Flagged, not resolved.
