# Loss framing on the account's most-used line

## Idea

The account's signature text hook is pure gain: you saved $400 and your husband is impressed. The spoken script running directly underneath it is pure loss: you are swiping your card for thousands before the plan pays a dime. The ad has been running two opposite psychological frames stacked on top of each other for five months, and **only the gain frame has ever been tested as a hook.**

## Spark

The loss version of this line is already written — it is in the voiceover of the same file, thirty seconds later.

## Source read

The gain frame, on twenty distinct video ads carrying $87,991.09 and 4,499 leads at a $19.56 blended cost per lead, with no measured decay across twenty uses [as of 2026-09-04]:

> *"POV: Telling your husband you found better health insurance, saved $400 a month, AND the deductible is zero. Wife of the year energy. 💗"*

The loss frame, spoken inside `moms-65 1a` at 0:10, under that same text hook:

> *"When did family health deductibles start looking like a down payment on a car?"*

and immediately after: *"Seriously though, we pay this huge monthly bill, but the second one of our kids needs urgent care, I'm still swiping my card for thousands of dollars before the plan actually pays a dime."* At 0:25 she lands it in two words: *"It's ridiculous."*

`moms-65 1a` itself is the strongest riser in the account: eight days old, spend up **482%** week over week, and cost per lead *improving* 27% over the same step from $28.85 to $20.98. It also reaches a different pool — **63.0% Instagram against the account's 38.8%**, and a 51.0% skew to ages 35 to 44 against 44.6%.

And there is a matching hostile signal on the gain frame. The literal string "wife of the year" appears in **0 of 1,342** comments. The most-liked comment in the recent window sits under this casting family and reads: *"I'm sure the mom that can afford matching outfits prob just pays in cash 🤦‍♀️."*

## Winning elements

- **hook** — the loss version of a line the account has already proved at scale.
- **headline** — "a down payment on a car" is a comparison hook written by the creator, not by an agency.
- **emotional frame** — loss aversion against gain, which is the cheapest possible test of whether the account's most-used line is the right one.
- **angle** — the same fact, framed as what she is losing rather than what she might win.

## Justification

This is the lowest-cost, highest-isolation test in the whole bank: keep every frame of footage and every second of audio identical, rewrite the text hook in the creator's own words from the transcript, and run it as its own file. `iterations.md` lists Negative versus Positive Framing among its low-effort messaging iterations, and the variable is genuinely isolated.

If loss framing matches gain framing on cost, the account has a second proven hook family for the first time since March.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. Fits `pays-and-still-owes`.

## Brand fit and risk

Clean — no new claim is made either way. The risk is that the gain frame is carrying more than it looks: twenty uses with no measured decay is unusual, and swapping it out account-wide would be reckless. The right shape is a parallel file, not a replacement.

`seasonality.md` and the roadmap both say the evergreen POV family stays running while anything new layers beside it. This entry is a beside, not an instead.
