# You are allowed to decline the plan your job offers

## Idea

A large group of this brand's audience believes she cannot act, because her coverage comes through a job. She is wrong, and she does not know it. No ad in the account has ever told her. No video in the 23-video category library says it either. A stranger said it for free in the replies under an ad the brand paid for, and the brand has never replied once in twenty months and 1,342 comments.

## Spark

One sentence, already spoken by a stranger under the brand's own ad, unlocks the persona that bought the cheapest leads in the account's history.

## Source read

The sentence, posted 2026-05-05 under `MOMS38 - 1 - V2`, one commenter answering another:

> *"And if it's an employer insurance, you don't have to sign up with that employer insurance you can decline the coverage and go out and find your own insurance carrier."*

The women who need it, in their own words:

- *"nope. My job picks the plan that they offer."* — 2026-03-30
- *"Mine is $9,200/individual, $17,500/family and that's the only plan my employer offers 😬"* — 2026-03-27
- *"The hospital I work for only provides the option to use their insurance company. Last year it was over 18k."* — 2026-03-28
- *"Mine is $6k per person too but since it's thru my job I don't really have a choice"* — 2026-03-17
- *"Yep…I work for an insurance company and they now outsource our insurance and our deductible is 4k with no copays."* — 2026-05-03
- *"Has anyone tried this? I pay quite a bit for an employee plan with a very high deductible. Is this worth it and is it widely accepted?"* — 2026-08-15

This is the most durable signal in the corpus: 24 comments name an employer and 7 name "my job", and unlike nearly everything else in the corpus it keeps arriving from March through August rather than dying with one creative family. "Deductible" appears in 207 of 1,342 comments; "uninsured" appears in 2 [as of 2026-09-04].

The account has already proved the message. Two statics built for exactly this returned **3,815 leads at $11.79 across $44,972.40, converting 19.04% of their link clicks into leads** against video's 8.28% [as of `strategic-roadmap.md`, 2026-09-04]. Their headlines: *"Left my big insurance company for a mom-focused one. 24% cheaper and I choose my own doctor 😌"* and *"Didn't know I could ditch my job's health plan… until I did."* Both are dark.

## Winning elements

- **angle** — standing, not saving. She is allowed to leave.
- **hook** — the first-person past-tense switch: I didn't know I could, until I did.
- **headline** — both proven headlines are single sentences that work as statics without a shoot.
- **emotional frame** — permission, delivered calmly, rather than distress.
- **proof** — a stranger already said it in public, unpaid, which is the most credible form this brand can get.

## Justification

Four independent strategy reads converged on this lane without seeing each other — persona, product, messaging and casting. The absence shape is total: no ad in this account says it, no video in the category library says it, and the brand's own context names employer coverage as *"the most common 'competitor' — the default option families already have"* [`stated`]. Nothing tried it and stopped; the nearest thing to it converted cheapest at real scale and was switched off with the whole static modality rather than on its own merits.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Unaware, `inferred`, and that is the point — she does not know a choice exists. Fits `pays-and-still-owes` carrying the employer-plan state.

## Brand fit and risk

Clean under the brand's rules: no government programme is named, the villain is the arrangement rather than a company. Two honest risks. Her existing deal is genuinely hard to beat, because employer premiums come out of payroll before tax, so a cheap lead here may be a lead the agent cannot close — a gate-two question Parker cannot see. And the one complete customer outcome in 1,342 comments is a woman quoted $400 a month under an ad promising a $400 saving.

## Notes

The dormant statics that carry this message also carry the flat savings claim the brand's rules forbid, so the restart and the claim repair travel together. Whether the flat number or the switching story was doing the work is roadmap open loop 1 and it prices this entire idea.
