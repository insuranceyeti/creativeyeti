# The worst number wins

**concept_name:** The worst number wins

**idea:** When this account puts a dollar figure on screen, the comment section does not read it as a pain point. It reads it as an opening bid, and then a hundred women line up to beat it. The reusable thing here is not the number, it is the game. The audience has already built a contest under the brand's own ads where the person with the worst coverage wins, and the winning move is to congratulate the person with the good plan and then post something worse. That contest is a comment mechanic and a hook shape the brand has never run on purpose. It is also a warning label on every ad that leads with a figure.

**winning_elements:** comment mechanic, hook, angle, emotional frame

**spark:** Stop telling her the number and ask her for hers. The whole ad is the question, and the comment section writes the body.

**hunt_lane:** Customer language, incidental capture during the Phase-3 backfill of the existing brain.

**source_read:** The ad opens on a woman saying "My deductible is $6,000." Underneath it, this is what the audience does with that figure. "Lucky! Mine is $11,000" lands on 2026-03-17 with 19 likes and 4 replies. "I think I win mine is $9000" gets 3 likes and 5 replies. "Wow only 6? Must be nice" gets 13 likes. "You are lucky. Mine is over $10k" gets 10. "I win, we pay $1500 a month and still have $2500 deductible 😞" says the game out loud, sad face included. One woman posts the opposite and gets treated as the lottery winner she is: "I guess we're lucky our insurance through my husband's employer is free and our deductible is only $400 each and we have an oop max of 2500 a year," 11 likes and 7 replies. Nobody is arguing with the ad. They are playing against each other, and the prize is having the worst plan. The exact opening move is almost always the same two words, "mine is," followed by a figure.

**justification:** The counter-bid is the largest behavioral pattern this corpus holds, and it is `verified` from string counts anyone can re-run. The phrase "mine is" appears in **82 of 1,342 comments, 6.2%**, across 8 distinct ad names. Of those 82, **76 name a specific figure, and 64 of the 76, or 84%, name a number above the ad's $6,000** [`verified`, `personas/voice-of-customer/voc-pain-phrase.md`, counted on the 1,322 denominator and carried]. The related "lucky" frame runs to **11 records across 5 ads** and holds three of the four highest-liked rows in the metaphor pass [`verified`, `personas/voice-of-customer/voc-metaphor.md`]. The behavior is not an artifact of the $6,000 creative either. On 2025-07-28, under a different ad with a different figure, someone already wrote "600? Try 1000+, then this ad would be more realistic" [`verified`]. That is the same move, eight months earlier, on a different number.

The other half of the justification is the cost. `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md` puts it plainly and I agree with the read: mirroring a **number** produces a bidding war, and mirroring an **experience** produces recognition, because she has a bigger number and she does not have a different hallway [`inferred`, and the inference is the audit's, layered on three upstream reads]. So this entry is worth holding for two different reasons at once. The contest is a mechanic to use, and it is also the reason a figure in the first frame keeps getting beaten.

**Evidence picture.** One source type, one brand, one creative family carrying most of the volume. Every one of the 82 counter-bids is dated between 2026-03-13 and 2026-05-24, so this is a spring 2026 event on the `MOMS38 - 1` family rather than a standing behavior anyone has watched across a year. Confidence caps at **mixed** for the reason every persona claim in this brain caps at mixed: customer reviews and post-purchase surveys are verified empty, Reddit is unreachable, and no competitor is tracked. The honest sentence is always "commenters under the brand's paid ads said," never "customers said."

**stage_of_awareness:** Problem aware. She knows exactly what her plan costs her and she can quote it to the dollar. She is not looking for a solution in the moment she types. [`inferred`, from the shape of the behavior. Not proven.]

**persona_or_audience_fit:** `pays-and-still-owes`, carrying the `found-out-the-number` signal. `personas/personas-profile.md` names that signal from this exact evidence and draws the same implication: she answers a number with a number. [`inferred`.]

**brand_fit:** Close, with one real risk. The brand's compliance rules ban flat savings claims, named competitors, and any government or ACA reference [`stated`, `running-notes/brand-rules.md` and `sub-context-docs/brand-identity-analysis.md`]. A hook built on her number rather than the brand's number does not touch any of those. The risk is moderation of a different kind. An ad that invites people to post their worst plan will fill its own comment section with grievance, and this brand has never replied to a comment in twenty months, so the thread runs unattended. Rival brokers already poach inside these threads, 39 poaching comments across 10 ads, with one operator posting fifteen personalized pitches under a single ad in 54 minutes [`verified`, `sub-context-docs/competitive-landscape.md`].

The ads that generate the most of this behavior are not the ads that buy the cheapest leads. `strategy/strategic-roadmap.md` is **drafted and awaiting review, not approved by the brand**, and its open loop 2 sits on exactly this seam.
