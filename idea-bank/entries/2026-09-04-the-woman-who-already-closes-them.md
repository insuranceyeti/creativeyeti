# Open with the woman who already closes them

## Idea

The account's two biggest ads both hand the pitch to the same woman — Parker's read of the media describes a mature woman in her late forties or early fifties in a green floral dress. She has never once opened an ad. She arrives at 0:18 in one and 0:14 in the other. Move her to frame zero. She is already cast, already filmed, already in the two biggest ads in the account; what changes is the edit order.

## Spark

The one that reaches her four seconds sooner holds four and a half times as many viewers — and it is the one that is switched off.

## Source read

Same brand, same offer, same state list, same kind of woman delivering the pitch. Two ads within $3,300 of each other in spend and within 21 leads of each other in volume.

Against the craft bars in `ad-account-analysis.md` — hook floor 30%, strong at 45 to 50%; hold floor 12 to 15% — the flagship stops the scroll beautifully and then misses the hold floor by a factor of three. `killer-performance-ads.md` explains the failure precisely: seconds one to three owe the viewer a reason to keep watching and seconds three to five owe her the best benefit or a re-hook. A silent montage with an acoustic track and no spoken word until 0:18 gives her neither.

## Winning elements

- **creator** — a woman already cast, already filmed, already trusted with the close.
- **pacing** — the argument moved into the measured watch window.
- **format** — an edit, the highest-confidence and fastest-to-ship kind of work there is.
- **hook** — the person who is currently the payoff becomes the opener.

## Justification

That she already delivers the pitch in both giants is `verified` from `creator_demographic` and `ad_summary` on both ads. That casting acts as an age lever reproduces three independent ways. And it is the cheapest real thing on the list to produce: the brand names casting and creator sourcing as its number one creative bottleneck and production as its fourth, and this recommendation does not fight that bottleneck at all.

The weakest link, named rather than buried: the cost-per-lead gap between those two ads is **$1.40, about 6.5%**, on two ads that differ in more than one way. The hold gap is four and a half times and much harder to explain away. The cost gap is small.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. It serves `pays-and-still-owes` first, because that is who these two ads deliver to, and it is also the cheapest way to buy some of the whitespace around `already-knows` without handing her a lead slot.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits, and it rules three things out at the same time, which matters as much as what it rules in. **No lab coat as the speaker** — the account ran that and the clinical setting delivered 52.7% of its spend to women 25 to 34. **No synthetic presenter** — six rejections across three years and four ad families. **No husband on camera yet** — "wife of the year" returns 0 of 1,342 comments, so the idea rests on a naming pattern rather than a customer signal.

The one thing that genuinely undercuts it: hold rate and cost per lead run backwards in this account. The three formats with the strongest hold are among the most expensive per lead, at $32.00, $31.82 and $33.62 against the account's $22.64, all on under $2,100 of spend. That open loop decides whether this is a lead-cost move or only an attention move.
