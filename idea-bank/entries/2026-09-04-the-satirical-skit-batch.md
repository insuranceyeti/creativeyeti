# The satirical skit — fifteen files, never tested

## Idea

Fifteen satirical files went up on 2026-08-31 and the whole batch drew $4.23 across 163 impressions. The iterations report calls it the most creatively interesting thing in the account and says plainly that it cannot be iterated on, because it has never been tested. That is not a failed idea. It is an untested one, and the format underneath it is the account's best-holding family.

## Spark

The most interesting creative in the account has never been seen by anyone, and it costs $4.23 to find out.

## Source read

`moms 68 1#`, created 2026-08-31. A woman in a black leather jacket, opening on *"This is why health insurance in America is a scam..."* **$0.85 and 32 impressions** [as of `biweekly-iterations-report.md`, 2026-09-04].

The batch: fifteen files, **$4.23 total, 163 impressions**.

It is not an isolated case. Three separate drops, three weeks apart, each funded a single file:

| Drop | Date | Files | Winner's share of batch spend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moms53 | 2026-08-25 | 7 | 99.75% |
| Moms65 | 2026-08-27 | 11 | 93.4% |
| moms 68 | 2026-08-31 | 15 | the whole batch drew $4.23 |

Among the starved files sit real creative bets the account has never tested at scale: the $4,600 MRI cash-price comparison at a $15.38 cost per lead on $46.13; a split-screen reaction vehicle, `moms-53 5`, black-and-white reaction face over a woman venting under *"Health Insurance is a scam 🙄 $6,000"*, at $2.06 — the only split-screen in the drop; a tattooed mother on a nursery floor holding a Paw Patrol book, `moms-65 2a`, at $14.07, the relatable end of the casting axis, never tested.

Meanwhile the skit format is where the account's attention actually lives. `Moms36 - 3 - A - 2 - V4c`, the "I wish this was a joke... (health insurance)" skit, holds **14.43% to 15.17% lifetime** and sends 40.2% of its spend to women over 45 [as of 2026-09-04]. The two-role phone skits hold 24.48% and 27.31%.

## Winning elements

- **format** — satire, which is the account's proven attention format and its least-funded one.
- **hook** — a flat contrarian statement in the audience's own borrowed word.
- **emotional frame** — the exhausted register rather than the angry or the tearful one.
- **pacing** — a bit that escalates, which is a built-in retention device.

## Justification

The format's own track record inside this account is the justification: every skit family the brand has funded clears or nearly clears the 12% hold floor, and the account's overall spend-weighted hold rate is 3.78%. Fifteen satirical files were made and none was ever delivered.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. The skit family also happens to be one of the account's two accidental routes to women over 45, which makes this idea and the `already-knows` entry cheaper together than apart.

## Brand fit and risk

The villain is the system, which the brand's rules require, and satire keeps it there naturally. Two risks. The brand's guardrail against punching down means the joke has to be aimed at the process, never at a group of people. And the same grievance risk applies as everywhere else in this bank: a bit that only dramatises the fight leaves her with nowhere to go.

## Notes

**This entry is as much a delivery question as a creative one.** If drops keep collapsing onto one file within hours, every recommendation in the bank produces one test rather than three, and the constraint on this brand's learning rate is the ad-set structure. The fix named in the iterations report: give each variant its own ad set with its own budget, or the test will not happen.
