# The dark-mode fake post — a screenshot with no face in it

## Idea

The brand's most efficient creative ever made is a static mockup of a social post: black frame, white type, no photograph, no person, no motion. It is the same borrowed-proof mechanic as the account's video screenshot overlays, minus the human being. It is dormant.

## Spark

Take the overlay the videos are built around, throw away the face behind it, and you have the cheapest lead source in the brand's history.

## Source read

`B1 samar- Copy`, created 2025-07-17. A solid black frame. Top left, a small circular profile photo of a woman in a beige jacket beside a toddler in a mustard beanie, outdoors at golden hour. Beside it, in bold white, the name "Cindie" with a blue verified checkmark, then "@cindie_mama_23" in grey. The body copy is white sans-serif in the platform's own typography:

> *"Didn't know I could ditch my job's health plan… until I did. Now I have no deductibles and choose any doctors we want 👏"*

Below it, a rounded card with a pale lilac background carrying the HealthForMoms wordmark and its silhouette of a mother lifting a child. Under that, grey metadata: *"23:51 · 30/05/2024 from Earth · 333.3k Views"*, then *"390 Retweets, 7,314 Likes, 751 Bookmarks."*

**$35,219.98 lifetime, 3,046 leads at $11.56**, and in the last 365 days $33,580.51 and 2,878 leads at $11.67 [as of `visual-vocabulary.md`, 2026-09-04].

The video cousin of the same mechanic — a screenshot styled as a post from "Cindie Hayvern" composited over a woman's face — holds people roughly twice as well as the account's POV openers, at 5.01% to 7.59% against 2.39% to 3.28%.

## Winning elements

- **visual** — a readable artifact in frame zero, so the eye has something to do while the ear starts up.
- **format** — Social Interface executed as a native static.
- **headline** — the claim carried as a first-person quote in somebody else's mouth, which is a different grammar from the video's third-person assertion, and cheaper every time.
- **proof** — borrowed credibility. The complaint feels found rather than written.

## Justification

Cheapest lead source in the brand's history at real scale, 3,046 leads, and it is dark. The visual vocabulary read is blunt about what that means: the brand's most efficient creative ever made is a screenshot with no face and no motion in it.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Solution aware, `inferred` — the post reads as a peer who already solved it. Fits `pays-and-still-owes`.

## Brand fit and risk

**Compliance-conditional and it matters here more than anywhere.** A fabricated social post carrying engagement metrics that never happened, attributed to a person who does not exist, sits close to the thing the enforcement record is about — the FTC's April 2026 suit alleges carrier and government impersonation, and the Benefytt case named deceptive lead-generation websites as part of the violation. Separately, the brand's own audience rejects synthetic people loudly: six rejections across three years and four ad families, plus 32 comments across 7 ads calling the creative AI or fake. The mechanic is proven and the execution needs the brand's counsel, not Parker's judgment.

## Notes

The device and the persona are separable. What is proven here is the *grammar* — one line, black ground, no photograph. That grammar can carry a real quoted ad comment, which the brand owns 1,342 of, instead of an invented one. That version is captured separately in `2026-09-04-the-comment-as-the-hook.md`.
