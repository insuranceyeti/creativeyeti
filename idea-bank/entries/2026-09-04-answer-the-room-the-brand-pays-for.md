# Answer the room the brand already pays for

## Idea

Twenty months, 1,342 comments, and not one brand reply. Meanwhile strangers are doing unpaid care work in those threads — women explaining programmes to each other, offering to dig out an old notebook — and rival agents are working the same threads for free. The brand pays for the room and somebody else is standing in it. The idea is that the reply *is* creative: answering in the thread, and then putting the answer on screen.

## Spark

The brand is renting the room and letting other people host in it.

## Source read

**The silence.** Twenty months, 1,342 comments, zero brand replies [as of 2026-09-04].

**The rivals.** 39 comments across 10 distinct ads pitch a competing offer, 17 of them on `MOMS38 - 1 - V1` alone. One operator working for Blue Ocean Employer Benefits posted **15 personalised pitches under a single ad inside 54 minutes on 2026-04-07**, addressing women by first name with two rotating scripts, thirteen of them carrying the same URL. Another writes: *"You can get a health insurance without paying premiums $0/month... Pm me if you need health insurance."*

The ad they raided is the finding. `MOMS38 - 1 - V1` is not the account's biggest spender. It is the one people actually watch, at a **17.12% hold rate against 3.81%** on the current top spender. An agent farming comments goes where the argument is, and the argument sits where the attention sits. The creative that holds a mother longest gives a rival the longest window to stand in front of her.

**The unpaid care work.** Strangers answer each other under these ads — a woman offering to dig out her old notebook and phone someone about chronic Lyme disease, another walking a mother through the TEFRA and Katie Beckett programmes. The same behaviour appears in the open category feed: two creators in the 23-video library are individual licensed agents doing organic lead generation in their own comments, one closing with *"comment and I'll be in touch and we will run a quote."*

**The material.** 1,342 real comments on 112 ad IDs, dated 2025-01-08 to 2026-09-03, and not one has ever appeared on screen.

## Winning elements

- **comment mechanic** — the reply as the unit of creative.
- **angle** — being the one who answers, in a category where nobody does.
- **proof** — a public, checkable answer to a public, checkable doubt.
- **format** — Comment Response, number seven on the brand's own untested-format list.

## Justification

The behaviour appears in two unconnected places on the same day of reading, which `organic-social-analysis.md` treats as a real pattern rather than a coincidence. And the commercial logic is direct: the brand pays the CPM, and operators who compete on effort rather than media spend have no CPM standing between them and the same woman.

The honest uncertainty is size. Nobody has connected the comment-section leakage to anything downstream, and Parker cannot see what happens after the handoff.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Product aware, `inferred` — she is standing under the ad deciding whether to act. Sits on all three personas.

## Brand fit and risk

Fits. The brand's own stated objection, *"I don't want to get on a call and be sold to,"* says the conversational register is the one its audience is asking for — and a reply is a continuation of a conversation rather than a pitch.

Two risks. Moderation is a real operational job, not a creative one, and none of the rival operators can even be audited: Blue Ocean Employer Benefits, Health Insurance Confidant Consulting and DPC Frontier all return "Not in Parker's database." And answering publicly means answering the hard ones too — the pregnancy exclusion, the pre-existing condition denial — where the honest answer is a no.

## Notes

The listening post itself is closing, and that changes the urgency in both directions. Since 2026-07-01 the corpus holds **86 comments**, and 36 of the 59 received in August are the single word "Help," all on one ad. Some of that is a 73.5% spend decline over the same window; the comment drop is steeper than the spend drop, so something else is also happening.
