# The January cliff — the thing actually happening to her

## Idea

The single largest thing happening to this brand's buyer in the next four months is not Open Enrollment. It is that the enhanced subsidies expire on 2026-01-01, an event that moved roughly 4.8 million people out of coverage they could previously afford. The account has spent $379.92 in its entire life on creative that names a subsidy. The idea is creative built for the woman whose premium is about to change under her, aimed at December and January rather than at November 1.

## Spark

Everyone is planning around November 1 and the actual earthquake is eight weeks later — the account's fifth-biggest month is getting all the attention and the real event has almost no money behind it.

## Source read

From `strategic-roadmap.md` [2026-09-04]: November 2025 took 5.4% of spend while March through May 2026 took 58.9%. December is the worst month in the whole readable series. Meanwhile the enhanced subsidies expire 2026-01-01, and 47.4% of the category's relevant organic posts land October to December.

The category feed already has a voice for it. @insurancebyalexa opens *"It is December. Why is nobody talking about this right now? Have you heard about the family glitch?"*, posted 2022-12-02, 29,000 views — a licensed agent doing organic lead generation on exactly this seam.

## Winning elements

- **angle** — a real, dated, externally caused change in her costs, rather than a claim about the brand.
- **timing** — it lands in the month the account's own funnel historically gives way.
- **emotional frame** — the floor moving under her without her having done anything.

## Justification

The gap is stark and measurable: the biggest structural event in the buyer's year carries $379.92 of lifetime creative spend. The roadmap graded this an open loop precisely because it decides where the ramp points — if the cliff is the real event, the whole plan should be built to land in December and January rather than to peak on November 1.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Unaware, `inferred`, and that is unusual for this account: 0.0% of lifetime spend has ever addressed a woman who was not already unhappy with her insurance [as of `quarterly-whitespace-analysis.md`, 2026-09-04]. A woman whose premium has not changed yet does not know she has a problem. That makes this the single coldest idea in the bank and the one most likely to need a story lead.

## Brand fit and risk

**Compliance-blocked as written, and captured anyway so evaluation can see it.** The brand's non-negotiable rules ban government and ACA references and put "ACA" on the never-used word list [`stated`, brand context section 8]. The word "subsidy" and the policy event itself are difficult to name without tripping that rule. What may survive: the *effect* — your premium is about to change and it is not because of anything you did — stated without naming the programme or the legislation. Whether that survives is the brand's call, not Parker's, and it is the same reconciliation question that gates the income-gap entry.

## Notes

Two things to hold before anyone briefs this. First, the roadmap explicitly declines to recommend a hard December ramp, because 2025's December broke the efficiency trade on largely the same creative. Second, this idea and the "no window" idea are the two seasonal entries in the bank and they imply different calendars. Grade them against each other.
