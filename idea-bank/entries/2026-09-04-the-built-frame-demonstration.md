# The built frame — a demonstration instead of an assertion

## Idea

The account almost never demonstrates. It asserts: a claim spoken over a picture that does not prove it. There are exactly four places where it does demonstrate, all four hold above the 12% floor, and three of the four are dormant. The claymation and 3D family is the purest of them — the only creative in the library that stages an actual proof rather than making a claim — and it has been funded at about $506 in total.

## Spark

The one thing in this account that shows rather than tells holds better than anything in the window, and it was never put in market.

## Source read

`Moms46 - V1`, launched 2026-07-02. Not filmed at all. Built.

A claymation woman in her mid-thirties with brown wavy hair and a purple sweater sits at a desk late at night, buried under overflowing stacks of paper bills, lit by one desk lamp, looking exhausted. A narrator asks: *"Did you know your hospital bill is cheaper if you don't use your insurance?"* At 0:04 the style changes to clean 3D: a mother and her son in a bright hospital hallway beside an MRI machine, with a small green price tag and a much larger blue one floating above them. At 0:10 a cash icon and an insurance card icon each connect to a tag, and the insurance tag visibly grows. At 0:17 a tall office tower with a shield-and-umbrella logo looms over a small hospital and a pharmacy, glowing blue lines running between them. At 0:23 two identical twin women sit across a table from each other while a number between them inflates from $1.10 to over $59.50.

**$381.82, 12 leads at $31.82, 31.25% hook rate, 13.26% hold rate** [as of 2026-09-04].

Its sibling `Animation Agency #4 - H1 - Copy` goes further and personifies the bill itself: a rectangular paper character with heavy black eyebrows and folded arms, standing on a blue insurance card on a wooden kitchen counter, saying *"I am your deductible."* That one received **$1.12 across 34 impressions**. A third build, `Animation Agency #4 - H1`, stages the deductible wall: coins falling into a premiums jar, an arrow to a medical cross, then a stone wall labelled $6,000 blocking the path to a box marked "INACTIVE".

## Winning elements

- **visual** — the money shot: a claim made with no reading required.
- **format** — Graphic Video, the account's only one, where the whole frame is built rather than filmed.
- **proof** — the price does not get asserted, it visibly grows on screen when the insurance card connects to it.
- **pacing** — a sequence of small reveals, which is a retention device the account's montages do not have.

## Justification

Every one of the account's four real demonstrations holds above the 12% floor — the state list, the claymation price tags, the deductible wall, and the phone call — and three of the four are dark. The one that is live, the state list, is the cheapest lead source in the window. That is a pattern with four members and the brand has not acted on it.

The evidence is thin in dollars, and the entry says so: $381.82 is a handful of leads and a 13.26% hold on that spend is a candidate, not a proof.

## Stage of awareness and audience fit

Problem aware, `inferred`. A built frame casts nobody, which quietly solves the brand's number one stated bottleneck — casting — and its fourth, production.

## Brand fit and risk

Two real risks. First, the underlying claim in `Moms46 - V1` is that the cash price beats the insured price, which points toward leaving the category rather than buying a plan; the format is liftable, that specific argument needs care. Second, the audience's standing hostility to synthetic people is documented across three years — though it is aimed at photorealistic AI humans presented as real testimonials, and claymation is obviously and honestly not a person. That distinction is worth testing rather than assuming.

## Notes

`Animation Agency #4 - H1 - Copy` at $1.12 across 34 impressions is not a format that failed. It is a format that was never put in market. Its personified-deductible character is the single most distinctive unused asset in the library.
