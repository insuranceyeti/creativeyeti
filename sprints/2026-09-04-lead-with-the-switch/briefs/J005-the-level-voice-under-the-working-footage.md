# Brief — The level voice under the working footage

## The concept

**Take the footage that is currently carrying this account and move the delivery into a flat, unhurried register. Put the shock in the fact and take it out of the voice.**

This is the cheapest correction available to a production line that is currently building the opposite, and it is the only concept in this round filed as an **iteration** rather than a net-new. `iterations.md`'s Step Zero is exact about what that means: an iteration is a focused, intentional modification to an ad that is already performing, living inside the same concept and the same creative DNA. Someone watching the original and the iteration back to back should recognise them as related. That is the whole design here.

**What is currently working, checked live rather than carried from a doc.** All three of the account's cheapest leads at real spend are new footage under lines the account already knew converted, all created 2026-08-14 to 2026-08-25, all pointed at `www.healthformoms.co/save/`, all `ACTIVE` today [all `verified live`, trailing 30 days, 2026-09-04]:

| Ad | Spend, 30d | Leads | CPL | Hook | Hold | Click to lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `moms-63 2b` | $5,529.74 | 417 | **$13.26** | 40.15% | 2.39% | 9.29% |
| `moms-63 3e` | $5,449.11 | 353 | **$15.44** | 42.89% | 2.88% | 7.34% |
| `moms-53 3` | $2,041.42 | 127 | **$16.07** | 35.48% | 3.89% | 4.29% |

Against the incumbent in the same window, `Moms43 - 4 - V3` took **$15,104.97 for 612 leads at $24.68** on a 45.59% hook and a 3.32% hold. Same words, older picture, nearly twice the lead cost.

**And what the production line is actually building.** Six distress ads and climbing — women crying in tight close-up. Over the 30 days to 2026-09-02 those openers delivered leads at **$29.85, $34.94 and $38.09, blending to $33.23 across six ads**, while the calm openers delivered at **$13.29, $15.14, $16.01, $19.52, $21.56 and $24.45**. And the distress ads posted the *higher* hook rates — **57.02% and 52.40% against the flagship's 45.55%** [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/monthly-hook-audit.md`]. **They stop more people and cost half again as much.**

`moms54-3` is the sharpest single example: **57.02% hook, 13.66% hold, an 8.08-second average play — the strongest attention numbers anywhere in this account — and a $29.85 cost per lead** on $1,283.69 in the trailing 30 days [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. It is `ADSET_PAUSED` now. Its siblings ran $34.94 and $38.03.

**So the concept in one line: same footage, same body, same message, different register.** Hold the delivery flat. Let the fact carry the weight.

### Why the register is the variable and not something smaller

`iterations.md`'s signal-change ladder ranks the levers by how loud a signal each one sends: format loudest, then vehicle, then persona, then hook, then messaging quietest. **Delivery and pacing are not on that ladder, which is exactly why this is cheap** — it is a direction applied inside work already in production, not a new asset class. In the tier language of `persona-research-and-creative-strategy-process.md` this is **Tier 1**, the highest-confidence and fastest-to-ship kind of work there is.

And `iterations.md`'s isolate-your-variables rule is what makes it readable: **hold the footage, the body and the message fixed, and move only the delivery.** If the register changes and the script changes and the creator changes, the round learns nothing regardless of the outcome.

### Where the register comes from — the source, read properly

The organic anchor is **@kaseyjaneanderson**, and the media read is pulled rather than inferred from a label. A woman in her late twenties in a ribbed maroon top sits at a wooden dining table, a wall of framed family photos behind her in warm light. An overlay holds the entire time. She says the line **like it is not a big deal** — that her family of four has been without health insurance for three years, and that it was the best decision they ever made. Her own caption carries the feeling her voice does not: *"I've wanted to make this video for so long but was embarrassed that we couldn't afford regular health insurance."*

**872,100 views, 75,400 likes, 12,100 shares, 1,316 comments, 17,993 saves**, re-pinned live 2026-09-04 [`verified`, Parker MCP `search_tiktok_videos`]. The third largest video in the library and the second largest outside the pregnancy set.

**Borrow the delivery, not the confession.** The specific thing she confesses — that a family went uninsured on purpose — belongs to the woman leaving the category, who is **33 comments across 10 ads** in this brand's corpus and who will not buy this product [`verified`]. That fit read is `inferred`: the judgment that the delivery travels to a different woman than the confession does is a judgment, not an observation.

### The wider pattern, from a genuinely independent instrument

Across the category feed, **not one of the 17 hooks pulled from the relevant videos opens on crying, shouting or visible distress.** Six are questions, four name a demographic in the first sentence, three are flat statements that break a norm. The audit's own line: the strongest performers put the shock in the fact and keep it out of the delivery [`verified`, across 17 of 19 relevant videos, `audits/2026-09/monthly-organic-tiktok-audit.md`]. And **10 of the 19 relevant videos have no background music at all** [`verified`].

`creative-strategy-fundamentals.md` treats two independent instruments agreeing as much harder to explain away than one being loud. Here the account's own money and the category's own feed arrive at the same instruction from opposite directions.

## TEEP phase and emotional shift

**Where she is, in plain speech.** She is mid-scroll, on a break, decompressing. She did not open the app to solve her insurance. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 11 is the frame: the ad lands **on top of** a mood she did not choose for you, and a hook that demands a feeling far from where she already is reads as an ambush. She scrolls before she can name why.

**So this ad's job is to say something big without raising its voice, so she can actually take it in.** `emotional-delivery-and-timing.md` explains the mechanism plainly: high-arousal states **narrow** attention onto whatever is in front of her and suppress the reflective processing that builds identification; low-arousal states **expand** attention and engage the self-referential processing that makes someone take information and make it part of who they are. **The same asset, experienced in two different states, is neurologically two different pieces of creative.**

That is why a crying close-up can win the thumb and lose the lead. It buys attention in the state least able to convert it.

**The shift the ad has to create:** *from* "here comes another ad shouting at me about how bad my life is" *to* "she just said the thing out loud, plainly, and it's true."

**And the specific craft warning that applies to whoever writes this.** `hook-psychology.md` says it directly, and it is aimed at a model: a system trained on engagement **over-reaches for high-intensity negativity**, because dramatic reads as compelling. The fix is to start from her specific lived micro-moment in real comment language rather than the idealised high-stakes version, and to default to calm on cold traffic. **This account has been doing the over-reaching thing at production scale, and its own numbers are the receipt.**

**What this forbids.** No crying. No tight close-up on a face mid-emotion — that grammar is `in play, verified` on `moms54-3`, `moms54-4` and `Moms36 - 3 - A - 2 - V4c`, and this concept is the correction to it. No music swell doing the emotional work the voice is deliberately not doing. No scare tactic, which is a brand rule anyway.

## Variations

Three. The sprint plan set the count and the reason is that this is cheap and it rides on the footage currently carrying the account — the highest-value place in the round to spend three variations.

`iterations.md` warns against the opposite failure too: **do not over-iterate into sameness.** Three flat reads of one line is not three variations. So these diverge on **which part of the register is doing the work**, while holding the footage, the body and the message fixed.

**Variation A — flat delivery on the existing body, nothing else changed.** The control, and the purest read of the hypothesis. Same footage family, same script structure, same length. The performer holds level where the current cut lifts. This is the isolate-your-variables version and it is the one that actually answers the question.

**Variation B — flat delivery plus slower pacing.** `emotional-delivery-and-timing.md` is explicit that pacing is a cognitive variable, not an aesthetic one: fast cuts hold high arousal and narrow attention, which is good for "receive and act on a clear message"; slow pacing opens the reflective processing that *feeling* something requires. This variation lets shots hold. Fewer cuts, longer beats, the same words given room. It also happens to be what `advertising-to-older-audiences.md` says works on the 45-and-over band, which is a free second read.

**Variation C — flat delivery with the sound stripped back.** No music bed, or none until well after the open. This is the direct transfer from the source: 10 of the 19 relevant category videos carry no background music at all, while this account's biggest ad opens on an acoustic track playing mid-phrase over a silent shot and does not land a spoken word until 0:18, against an average play time of about four seconds [`verified`, `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md` and `90-day-creative-strategy-audit.md`]. **In this category the voice is the sound design and it has to arrive immediately.**

**What all three hold fixed:** the `moms-63` footage family and its creator pool, the body copy structure, the message, the destination, and the compliance rules. **What they must not do:** change the script. The moment the words move, this stops being an iteration and starts being a different test, and the account learns nothing about register.

**One thing worth flagging to whoever schedules this.** The account's incumbents stay running. `seasonality.md`'s rule and the sprint plan's guardrail both say evergreen winners stay on while new creative layers in, and with Open Enrollment on **2026-11-01** — 58 days out — switching off the thing that currently buys leads in order to test something unproven would be the expensive kind of brave. **Layer. Do not swap.**

## Creator direction

**Talent need: the existing creator pool. No new casting.** That is a large part of why this concept sits in wave one — the brand names casting and creator sourcing as its number one creative bottleneck, and this concept does not touch it.

**The brief shape.** `creator-briefs.md`'s split applies cleanly: **direction on structure, freedom on delivery** — except here the direction *is* about delivery, so it inverts. The words and the structure are already proven and stay fixed. What the performer gets is a precise instruction about register, and freedom in how she finds it.

**What the creator and the editor receive:**

- **The instruction, stated the way a person would say it.** Say the big thing like it is not a big deal. Do not push. Do not build. Do not let the voice do the work the fact is already doing. If it sounds like an ad read, it is wrong; if it sounds like she is telling a friend something ordinary that happens to be shocking, it is right.
- **References, because a reference does more than a paragraph of description.** Three attach and each does a different job:
  - **@kaseyjaneanderson** — **this is the register.** Watch the first ten seconds and match the level of it. Do not match the content.
  - `moms-63 2b` — **this is the footage and the body.** A well-dressed mother walks out of a house holding her young son's hand, outdoors, in motion. $13.26 leads, 40.15% hook. This is what is being iterated on.
  - `moms54-3` — **this is what we are correcting.** 57.02% hook, 13.66% hold, and a $29.85 lead. Stops more people, costs half again as much.
- **Creator vocabulary.** Direct-to-camera or handheld selfie, which is the account's default and carries almost every pitch segment in the library. The lifestyle montage clip stays as the visual bed — medium to wide, stable, warm, three seconds or so each — because that is the `moms-63` grammar and it is what is working. **What changes is the voice on top of it.**
- **Pacing direction for the editor.** Variation B specifically: let shots hold. The account's transitions grammar currently cuts to the rhythm of the read; this asks the read to slow down and the cuts to follow it.
- **Sound direction.** Variation C specifically: nothing in front of the voice in the first three seconds. `hooks.md` counts silence as a sound hook in its own right.

**The magic moment — the instant that stops the scroll.** It is **the gap between how big the sentence is and how ordinary it sounds.** That mismatch is the hook, and `hooks.md` format 13 names the shape: a flat statement that breaks a norm, delivered without drama. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 7 is why it works — the form must be effortless and the content must be a surprise, easy to process and hard to predict. **Protect the flatness. It is the whole trick and it is the first thing a nervous performer will lose.**

**One caution that applies hard here, carried from the idea bank.** `hooks.md` names the fakeout as what kills a calm opener: a Zone 1 open with a high-pressure body, which she clocks as dissonance even when she cannot name it. **The zone has to hold the whole way through**, and this account funnels nearly every script into the same state gate and the same zero-deductible promise within about twenty seconds. So the flat register is not just a hook note. It is a whole-ad note.

**The call to action mirrors her real apprehension.** Fifteen comments across ten ads guard the phone number, and the sharpest drew 33 likes [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. On a flat-register ad, an ask that suddenly turns urgent is the exact dissonance above.

## Validation — strategy

**Compliance runs before the shoot.** No fabricated statistics. Neither banned string — *"saving you 20% or more"*, *"Approved by thousands of Mom's across America"*. Savings always "up to 30%" with the "up to" intact; deductible always "$0 deductible option" or "options with no deductibles"; no hard guarantee; no named competitor; no government or ACA reference; **no scare tactic, which this concept is structurally the opposite of**; the villain is the system; no synthetic presenter. The dramatization disclaimer the account already carries travels with these.

**And one that is specific to an iteration.** The `moms-63` family currently carries the shared body-copy block that Priority 1 exists to repair. **An iteration inherits the copy it is built on unless someone removes it.** Every one of these three variations gets the block stripped and rebuilt before it ships, or the round has quietly re-published the exposure it was meant to close.

## Confidence and what this tests

**The hypothesis, stated so it can fail.** Holding the register flat, on the same footage and the same body, **beats the distress register on cost per lead**.

**What would falsify it.** Three flat-register iterations landing at or above the distress lane's $33.23 blended cost per lead, or above the `moms-63` family's own current $13.26 to $15.44. The second is the sharper test, because it is a like-for-like comparison inside one family.

**Confidence: mixed to strong on the direction, inferred on causation** — which is exactly how the idea-bank entry graded itself, and this brief carries that mark rather than upgrading it.

- That calm openers in this account cost less per lead than distress openers is **verified** across nine ads over 30 days.
- That the register is *why* is **inferred**. The distress ads sit in a paused ad set at lower spend, and delivery differences could be doing some of the work.
- That the category's feed rewards the same register is **verified** across 17 of 19 relevant videos, from a genuinely independent source.
- That the register travels to a different woman than the source's confession does is **inferred**, and it is a judgment rather than an observation.

**Gate two is dark.** 44 `Call` events across 16 ads against 4,421 leads in 90 days. And there is a version of the gate-two worry that points the *other* way here, worth saying because it is unusual: `performance-targets-and-metrics.md` warns that a last-click form fill over-rewards fast, low-friction clicks. A distress opener that stops 57% of viewers and holds 13.66% is producing exactly the kind of engaged, slow, emotionally invested watcher that might convert **better** downstream even while costing more per lead. **Nobody can see that.** It is one more reason to ask the partner agencies about the August creative against the April creative — the roadmap names it as the one answer that outranks everything else in this brain.

## Open loops

Two questions this brief left behind. The prompt's output template does not name a loops section; it is here because this brain carries one on every context doc and because the run instruction requires it.

**1. The account is still building the lane its own numbers say is expensive, and nobody has recorded why.**

Six distress ads and climbing. Its own thirty-day read puts that lane at $33.23 blended against a calm lane spanning $13.29 to $24.45. Somebody chose to make six of them and no document in this brain records the reason.

*Pull: Tension.* Two things this brain treats as settled cannot both be right: the team steers by cost per lead, and its current production line is pointed at the more expensive register.

*Question: What is the team seeing in the distress ads that the cost-per-lead read does not show?*

*Why it matters:* if the answer is a lead-quality signal from the partner agencies, then gate two is already informing production and this brain does not know it — which would be the single most valuable fact anyone could hand Parker. If the answer is that nobody has looked at the numbers by register, this brief is the correction and it should ship first. Either way it is one conversation.

*Territory: Messaging.*

**2. The account's best-priced ads hold 2.39% and 2.88%, and this concept is being briefed to hold them there.**

`moms-63 2b` delivers $13.26 leads on a 2.39% hold, a fifth of the 12% craft floor. The account's best-holding ads sit in its worst cost bands. This brief deliberately does not try to raise hold, because raising it in this account has historically come with a higher lead cost.

*Pull: Surprise.* Every craft doc assumes deeper attention converts better, and this account's own money has run the other way consistently enough that a brief is now written around the inversion rather than against it.

*Question: Is a low hold rate on this account a defect, or is it what a fast, well-qualified click looks like on a two-minute funnel?*

*Why it matters:* it decides whether hold belongs on this brand's scoreboard at all. If a 2.39% hold on a 40% hook is simply what a good ad looks like when the whole path from impression to lead can happen in under two minutes, then every audit in this brain that flags hold as broken is measuring the wrong thing, and several concepts in this round are briefed against a floor that does not apply here.

*Territory: Messaging.*
