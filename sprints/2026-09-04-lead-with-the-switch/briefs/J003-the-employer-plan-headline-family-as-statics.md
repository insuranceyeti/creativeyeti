# Brief — The employer-plan headline family, as statics

## The concept

**A short set of single-frame statics that carry the switching message in the audience's own vocabulary, one line and one designed frame each. No person. No platform mockup. No printed engagement numbers.**

This is the useful half of the Priority 2 rebuild. J001 rebuilds the permission line as the message. **J003 rebuilds the words around it** — because the `B1 samar- Copy` static family carries headlines nothing else in this account carries, and those headlines speak the register the comment corpus actually uses.

**The register, and the number that decides it.** "Deductible" appears in **207 of 1,342 comments** and "uninsured" in **2** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. That is a ratio of roughly a hundred to one, and it is the single sharpest fact in this brain. She is not somebody who has never had coverage. She is somebody else's customer and she is angry about it. Every ad that opens by treating her as uninsured is talking past the person it just paid to reach.

The brand's own rule points the same way and is more specific: *"don't over-explain concepts the audience already understands (e.g., what a deductible is) — assume problem-awareness and reframe the cause instead"* [`stated`, guidelines, in `brand-lens.md`]. She knows the word. She wants to know why the number is what it is.

### What already ran, and what it proves

Three headlines from the `B1 samar- Copy` statics, quoted here as **evidence of what already ran** and never as copy to reuse [all `verified live`, 2026-09-04]:

- *"Finally found a health plan that doesn't make me Google "is a $7000 deductible normal?" at midnight."*
- *"I can handle tantrums. Insurance hold music? Not so much."*
- *"Switched to a mom-focused health plan and finally relaxed / saved on premiums."*

Read what the first one is doing. It is not a claim. It is a **scene** — a specific woman at a specific hour doing a specific thing, and the thing she is doing is checking whether her own life is normal. That is her register, and it is the only place in this account's entire library where the copy sounds like the comment section. `static-ad-design.md` calls the headline the single most important piece of copy on a static, and names the headline-style swap as often the highest-leverage static iteration there is — because it changes how the audience emotionally receives the message without changing the message.

**And the family it sits in has real money behind it.** `B1 samar- Copy` on a static-filtered read: **$54,224.86 lifetime across 39 variants, 4,441 leads at $12.21**, against the account's $18.78 lifetime blend and its $22.29 trailing 30 days [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. `ADSET_PAUSED`.

### The rebuild rule, and why "keep the headline" is the whole instruction

The frame around those headlines cannot be reused. The `B1 samar- Copy` family is the dark-mode fake tweet: solid black, a small circular profile photo, a display name with a **blue verified checkmark**, an invented handle, and metadata reading *"23:51 · 30/05/2024 from Earth · 333.3k Views"*, then *"390 Retweets, 7,314 Likes, 751 Bookmarks"* [`verified`, `sub-context-docs/visual-vocabulary.md` visual hook 14; the sizing sweep across the pattern is `verified live`, 2026-09-04]. Seven ad-name groups carry that device, holding **$35,234.91 lifetime and 2,871 leads at $12.27**, across three invented personas: `@cindie_mama_23`, `@thehealthymom34`, `@Motherooffthree`. Printed engagement and view counts are statistics, and they are invented, against the brand's own non-negotiable **"No fabricated statistics."**

**All seven read `CAMPAIGN_PAUSED` and spent $0 in the last 30 days** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. So this is an asset-library problem, not live burn — but nothing new inherits any of it.

**The rule in one line, from `brand-lens.md`: strip the fake platform frame, strip the banned body-copy block, keep the headline.**

And "keep the headline" means keep the **register and the mechanism** — a lived, specific, midnight-hour scene in her own words — not paste the exact string. The strings above already ran, inside assets that break a hard rule, in a family where two variants of a sibling sit at DISAPPROVED. New lines get written through the headlines skill, in that register, from the live corpus.

### What this concept deliberately does not build

`idea-bank/entries/2026-09-04-legal-scam-the-qualifier.md` sits right next to this as a headline-variant idea and is **not** built here. The parent theme is real and large — "scam" appears in **48 of 1,342 records, 3.58%, across 15 distinct ad names**, first on 2025-01-13 and most recently on 2026-09-03, with roughly two thirds aimed at the category rather than at this brand [`verified`]. But the qualifier itself, which is the actual idea, is **two exact instances in 1,342**, a quote candidate and not a pattern by the ten-record bar. And roughly a third of scam mentions in this corpus are aimed at Health For Moms itself, while the most-liked thing anyone has ever said about the service, at **33 likes**, is a warning not to give it your phone number. **That needs a compliance and reputation read before anyone briefs it, and this brief is not that read.**

## TEEP phase and emotional shift

**Where she is, in plain speech.** She is not comparing plans. She has just re-learned what her plan actually costs her and she is sitting in it. The proof is behavioural and it is large: the phrase "mine is" appears in **82 of 1,342 comments, 6.2%, across 8 distinct ad names, and of the 76 that name a figure, 64 — 84% — name a number above the ad's own** [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. She treats the ad's number as an opening bid. A woman evaluating an offer does not do that.

**So this ad's job is to make her feel accurately seen, in her own words, before it offers her anything.** `emotional-delivery-and-timing.md` is direct about this moment: creative that mirrors her internal state back with precision outperforms creative that introduces a solution before the problem has been fully felt.

**The shift the frame has to create:** *from* "nobody who makes these ads has any idea what this is actually like" *to* "that's my exact night, and somebody wrote it down."

**And the one thing that forbids.** **Do not lead with a figure.** Her documented response to a number is to beat it, and 84% of the time she does. `personas/personas-profile.md` states the implication plainly: leading with a specific figure invites her to top it and quietly disqualify the offer as unrealistic. The account's own evidence agrees from the other side — the one ad that opens by voicing her hesitation rather than correcting her, `moms-63 3e`, ran a **$15.46** cost per lead against a roughly $22.29 trailing account average [`verified`].

A number can appear *inside* a scene, as the thing she was Googling at midnight, because there it is her number and not the brand's claim. It cannot be the promise.

## Variations

Two. The count is deliberately small and the sprint plan gives the reason: **this is the second static concept in a round that already leans on paper.**

The evaluation's fourth coverage hole is exactly this. Five of twenty entries in the bank put a bill, a stub, a screen or a ledger in frame, and the account's one proven document ad buys the cheapest clicks in the file and converts them **worst among real spenders — 5.97% leads per link click against `moms-63 2b`'s 12.25% in the same window** [`verified`]. `creative-strategy-fundamentals.md` warns against exactly this shape: a device that looks like a pattern because it keeps recurring rather than because it keeps working. **Two here plus two on J008 is four paper assets out of twenty, which is a real bet without being the round.**

`static-ad-design.md` says the instinct to change one variable at a time works against congruency, because message and image have to move together. So these are **congruent variations**: two complete, internally coherent expressions that differ on the mechanism doing the work.

**Variation A — the midnight scene.** The frame carries a lived moment in her own vocabulary, with the deductible register in it, and the design supports the scene rather than a claim. The mechanism is self-reference: `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 6 says naming the viewer's exact situation is a direct line into involuntary attention and memory, and that it makes relevance beat raw attention — it wakes the right person and lets the wrong person keep scrolling, which is the whole game because wrong-person attention is what wasted spend is made of. This is the variation closest in register to the line that already ran.

**Variation B — the ordinary-thing comparison.** The frame sets an everyday parenting reality against the insurance experience, so the argument lands with no reading and no claim. The mechanism is processing fluency welded to surprise: `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 7 says the form must be effortless and the content must be unguessable, and that this is the single most useful rule in hook-writing. It is also, per `hooks.md` format 17, a Comparison shape — value demonstration **without an explicit claim**, which is precisely the substitution Priority 1 forces on a brand pulling two unsupported claims out of its copy.

**What both hold fixed:** black ground, white type, single designed frame, no person, no mockup, no engagement counts, one line carrying the weight, destination `www.healthformoms.co/save/`. **What they must not do:** be two typographic treatments of one sentence.

## Creator direction

**Talent need: none.** One line and one designed frame each. No creator, no shoot, no location, no casting call. That matters more here than it looks: the brand names **casting and creator sourcing as its number one creative bottleneck** and production as its fourth [`stated`, brand context]. Two of the round's twenty assets that need nobody at all is two assets that can ship this week.

`creator-briefs.md`'s freedom-versus-direction call resolves to the **director's brief** end — there is no performance to protect, so the design is specified and the judgment lives in typography and hierarchy.

**The magic moment.** On a static there is no first three seconds, there is a first fixation. `static-ad-design.md` is blunt that a static is not laid out, it is **directed** — you decide what the eye sees first, second and third, and you build the design to enforce that order. Here the magic moment is **the instant she recognises her own night in somebody else's sentence.** So the line is the largest thing on the frame and nothing competes with it.

**Direction the designer receives:**

- **Hierarchy, named explicitly.** Primary: the line. Secondary: the one supporting element that makes it concrete. Tertiary: the wordmark. If every element is the same size and weight, nothing leads and the viewer bounces before the message resolves.
- **The brand's own grammar, not an invention.** Solid black ground, white type, serif for the lead element and sans-serif beneath — the `B2 - 10TH JUNE - Copy 16` structure, which returned **$18,409.73 lifetime and 1,181 leads at $15.59** with no fabricated anything on it and the savings claim carried in its compliant form [`verified live`, re-pinned 2026-09-04; frame described at `sub-context-docs/visual-vocabulary.md` visual hook 15].
- **The scroll-stop devices, used on purpose rather than for decoration.** Lowercase where it fits, because it reads more organic and less ad-like. Colour contrast on the one phrase that matters. Font variation to separate hierarchy levels. White space as the counterweight — using every trick at once just recreates the wall of noise.
- **Congruency, which is the failure mode to watch.** `static-ad-design.md` names the common one: reusing one image under several headlines aimed at different audiences, so most variants are incongruent by definition. Whatever the line says, the frame has to look like. A scene-based line needs a frame that reads as a scene; a comparison line needs a frame that reads as a comparison.
- **Cognitive ease over cleverness.** `visuals.md` principle 8 — clever ideas that add cognitive load almost always lose once spend scales. And this brand's audience does not need the category explained to it.
- **The half-second test, run out loud.** Category and point in half a second, or simplify before anything else.

**The call to action mirrors her real apprehension.** Fifteen comments across ten ads guard the phone number and the sharpest drew 33 likes, the third-highest in the corpus [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. The ask stays quiet and names the hesitation rather than pushing through it.

## Validation — data

**One. The register is measured, not guessed.** "Deductible" in **207 of 1,342 comments**; "uninsured" in **2** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. `customer-review-mining-method.md`'s denominator discipline is what makes that usable: it is not a vibe about tone, it is a hundred-to-one ratio on the whole corpus.

**Two. The family that carried these headlines is the account's largest static family and it worked.** `B1 samar- Copy`, static-filtered: **$54,224.86 lifetime across 39 variants, 4,441 leads at $12.21** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. Against the brand's own tiers — under $15 fantastic — $12.21 sits inside the top band while the account has been in "meh" for six straight months.

**The caveat, and it is the same one that binds J001.** That family carries the fabricated frame, so nobody has separated how much of $12.21 was bought by the headline and how much by the fake badge and printed counts around it. **The $12.21 does not travel forward as a forecast.** What travels forward is the register.

**Three. The format is the account's own strongest converter and it has gone dark.** Lifetime, verified live on two matched pulls, 2026-09-04: statics turn a link click into a lead at **16.57%** against video's **8.28%**, on 82,871 and 312,052 link clicks respectively, at a **$12.30** cost per lead against video's $22.23, and they bought impressions 24% cheaper. And **a static-filtered pull of everything created since 2026-06-01 returns zero rows.** Not one static built in three months.

The caveat from `ad-account-analysis.md` survives: statics skew toward the bottom of the funnel and should not be judged against top-of-funnel video on a level field. It narrows the gap. It does not close it — 13,735 lifetime static leads is not a small ad picking off easy conversions.

**Four. The counterweight, stated rather than buried, because it is the reason this is two variations and not four.** `moms-53 3` — the Approved State List, the account's one proven document-in-frame ad — buys the cheapest link click in the file at **$0.96** and the best click-through rate in the account at **3.15%**, and then converts those clicks **worst among real spenders**: 4.29% leads per link click in the trailing 30 days and **5.97% over the 90 days to 2026-09-03**, against `moms-63 2b`'s 12.25% in the same window [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. **A document buys the stop. It does not automatically buy the form fill.**

The likely reading, and it is `inferred`: a full screen of information makes people stop and hunt, and the ones who do not find what they are looking for click anyway and get turned away. That is a warning about **information-dense** frames specifically, which is exactly why both variations here carry **one line**, not a list.

**Five. Do not lead with a number.** 82 of 1,342 comments post a figure unprompted across 8 distinct ad names, and 64 of the 76 that name one name a number above the ad's [`verified`]. This is not an aesthetic preference. It is a documented behavioural response to a specific creative choice, and it is the reason both variations here carry a scene rather than a claim.

**Six. The destination and the funding gate.** Matched pairs on the identical video file split only by destination returned `moms-63 2b` at **417 leads, $13.26** on `www./save/` against **6 leads, $144.23** on the quiz, and `moms-63 3e` at **339 leads, $15.46** against **8 leads, $67.15**; landing-page-view-to-link-click runs 85% to 88% on `www./save/` and 8% to 9% on the quiz [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. And of the **95 ad-name groups created since 2026-08-01, only 14 took $500 or more**, carrying **95.7%** of the cohort's money [`verified live`]. These two frames are wave two, which means they launch once wave one has readable numbers — and they need real budget or they join the eighty-one that never got read.

## Confidence and what this tests

**The hypothesis, stated so it can fail.** Her own vocabulary carries a static with no platform mockup around it, at a cost per lead better than the account's trailing **$22.29**.

Note the bar. Not $12.21, which came with a fake badge attached. Not $11.79, for the same reason. **Better than the account's own trailing number**, which is what "efficient" means in a brain with no unit economics.

**What would falsify it.** Two clean frames, funded past $500 each, landing at or above $22.29. That would say the register was not the thing — and combined with J001's four frames it would say the switching lane's economics went with the frame rather than the message, which is the most important thing this round could learn.

**Confidence: mixed.**

- The register is **verified** at a 207-to-2 ratio on the full 1,342-row corpus.
- The format advantage is **verified** on 82,871 and 312,052 link clicks.
- That these specific headlines were doing the work rather than the fake social proof is **inferred**, and it is the bet.
- That a document-style frame converts as well as it stops is **contradicted** by the account's own best evidence, which is why this is two variations and not four, and why both carry one line rather than a list.

**Gate two is dark on all of it.** 44 `Call` events across 16 ads against 4,421 leads in 90 days. And the warning in `performance-targets-and-metrics.md` bites hardest on exactly this kind of asset: on a phone-mediated, high-trust sale, a last-click form fill **over-rewards the creative that produces fast, low-friction clicks**. A cheap static click is the definition of that. **These clear gate one and nothing more.**

## Open loops

Two questions this brief left behind. The prompt's output template does not name a loops section; it is here because this brain carries one on every context doc and because the run instruction requires it.

**1. The account's one proven document ad buys the best clicks in the file and the worst form fills, and nobody has separated stopping from converting.**

`moms-53 3` holds the account's best CTR at 3.15% and its cheapest link click at $0.96, and converts those clicks at 5.97% leads per link click over 90 days against `moms-63 2b`'s 12.25%. Four assets in this round — two here, two on J008 — are designed frames betting the opposite way.

*Pull: Surprise.* The relationship every craft doc assumes, that a cheaper and better-qualified click converts better, is running backwards on this account's own money.

*Question: What happens between the click and the form on this account's designed frames that does not happen on its video?*

*Why it matters:* it decides how much of any future round can go to statics, which is the single largest format decision this brand faces. If the answer is that a text-dense frame pulls curiosity clicks, then a one-line frame is the fix and this round proves it. If the answer is something about the page, it is a product question and no amount of headline craft touches it.

*Territory: Product.*

**2. The best copy this brand has ever written is sitting inside assets it is not allowed to run, and nobody knows who wrote it.**

The `B1 samar- Copy` headlines are the only place in the whole library where the copy sounds like the comment section. They sit inside a fabricated-social-proof frame. No document in this brain records who produced that family, when, or whether that person or agency is still working with the brand.

*Pull: Gap.* It fired because the round's cheapest quality lever — whoever can write in that register again — is a person nobody has named.

*Question: Who wrote the `B1 samar- Copy` headlines, and can they write more?*

*Why it matters:* the headlines skill can work in that register from the corpus, and a human who already proved they can do it is faster and better. It costs one question to the team.

*Territory: Creators and talent.*
