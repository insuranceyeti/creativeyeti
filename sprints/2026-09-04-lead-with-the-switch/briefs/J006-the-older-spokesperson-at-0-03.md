# Brief — The older spokesperson at 0:03

## The concept

**Re-cut the two biggest ads in the account so the woman who currently arrives at 0:14 and 0:18 opens instead.**

That is the whole thing, and its power is that it is almost free. She is already cast. She is already filmed. She is already in both of these ads. **What changes is the edit order.**

**The comparison the whole call rests on**, re-verified live with full creative fields on both ads, 2026-09-04:

| | `Moms43 - 4 - V3` | `MOMS38 - 1 - V1` |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime spend | $57,506.21 | $54,173.20 |
| Leads | 2,668 | 2,689 |
| Cost per lead | $21.55 | **$20.15** |
| Hook rate | 45.84% | 32.34% |
| **Hold rate** | **3.81%** | **17.12%** |
| Share of spend to women 45+ | 10.6% (POV lifetime) | **24.8%** |
| The older woman arrives at | 0:18 | 0:14 |
| Status today | ACTIVE | ADSET_PAUSED |

Same brand, same offer, same state list, same kind of woman delivering the pitch. Two ads within $3,300 of each other in spend and within 21 leads of each other in volume. **The one that reaches her four seconds sooner holds four and a half times as many viewers, buys a cheaper lead, and reaches more than twice as many women over 45.** And the one holding 17.12% is switched off.

**The diagnosis, in craft terms rather than in numbers.** The hook floor is 30% and 45 to 50% is strong, so the flagship stops the scroll beautifully. The hold floor is 12 to 15%, and at **3.81%** the account's biggest spender misses it by a factor of three [`sub-context-docs/performance-targets-and-metrics.md`]. `killer-performance-ads.md` names the failure precisely: seconds one to three owe the viewer a reason to keep watching, and seconds three to five owe her the best benefit or a re-hook. **A silent montage with an acoustic track and no spoken word until 0:18 gives her neither.** `hook-psychology.md`'s diagnose-by-job rule says the same thing from the other side — a good hook rate with a poor hold is a job-2 failure, meaning no real gap and no relevance to the right viewer. The account's biggest ad wins the thumb and then has nothing to say to the person it just stopped.

**And there is a live cost attached.** Over the trailing 30 days `Moms43 - 4 - V3` took **$15,104.97 for 612 leads at $24.68**, on a 45.59% hook and a 3.32% hold [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. That is the brand's own "meh" band, on the largest single line item in the account.

### Why this is an iteration and specifically a re-cut

`iterations.md`'s Step Zero: an iteration is a focused modification to an ad that is already performing, inside the same concept and the same creative DNA. Someone watching the original and the re-cut back to back should recognise them as related. **Nothing here is new footage, new casting, new script or new claim. The material is reordered.**

Two of `iterations.md`'s historically highest-confidence moves apply directly:

- **Frankenstein stitching** — taking the strongest seconds from one place and using them as the opening of another. It is called one of the lowest-risk iterations available because every component has already been validated individually. The warning attached to it is the one that governs this edit: **if the energy, pacing or visual style clashes at the cut point, the stitch feels forced and the viewer drops off.**
- **Hook swap** — a new opening can get Meta to treat the ad as genuinely new while preserving the proven body. It is the go-to when an ad is fatiguing but the core message still converts, and it sits mid-level on the signal-change ladder — loud enough to reset the read, quiet enough to keep everything that works.

In the tier language of `persona-research-and-creative-strategy-process.md` this is **Tier 1**: the highest-confidence and fastest-to-ship kind of work there is.

### Why now, specifically

**Open Enrollment opens 2026-11-01**, 58 days out, and the brand's own two-month planning deadline already passed on **2026-09-01**. `seasonality.md`'s discipline is to plan and test months ahead and to layer new creative in beside evergreen winners rather than swapping them out. **A re-cut is one of the very few casting moves that can still land inside that window**, because it needs no shoot day, no creator booking and no new script approval. Everything else in this round that touches casting needs a person and a calendar.

## TEEP phase and emotional shift

**Where she is, in plain speech.** She is scrolling. She has managed this problem for twenty years. She has seen a hundred insurance ads and none of them has ever been about her, and she has quietly concluded the word "mom" in this brand's name means young moms. Sixteen comments across seven ads ask a version of that question, politely, and nobody answers on the thread [`verified`, carried on 1,322].

**So this ad's job is to introduce something to a woman who is already primed for the category, in the first second, by showing her someone she recognises.** She does not need the problem explained. She needs to see that the ad is for her before she scrolls past it.

**The shift the edit has to create:** *from* "another insurance ad for young moms with babies" *to* "oh — she's my age, and she's about to tell me something."

**Why frame zero is where that happens and nowhere else.** `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 1: before the viewer chooses to look at anything, the visual system has already locked onto whatever is most salient — motion, contrast, an anomaly, and above all **a human face**. It is involuntary and essentially done in about 100 milliseconds. **You do not choose what the viewer notices first; the frame does.** Mechanism 6 is the other half: a self-relevant signal wakes the right person and lets the wrong person keep scrolling, which is the entire game, because wrong-person attention is what wasted spend is made of.

Right now the account spends that one free involuntary glance on a montage and a music bed, and pays a person to appear fourteen seconds later.

**What this forbids.** No lab coat as the speaker — that experiment already ran, and the clinical setting delivered **52.7% of its spend to women 25 to 34** and 3.8% to women 45 to 54, the youngest delivery of anything inspected. No synthetic presenter — six separate rejections across three years and four ad families, the closest thing to a rule in this brand's whole customer record. No husband on camera — the literal string "wife of the year" returns **0 of 1,342 comments**, re-checked live, so nobody repeats the line and nobody rejects it, which means the idea rests on a naming pattern rather than a customer signal.

## Variations

Two. One per source ad, which is what a re-cut of two ads means and why the count is not a judgment call.

`iterations.md`'s isolate-your-variables rule governs both: **change the opening order and nothing else.** If the re-cut also changes the music, the script and the end card, the round learns nothing regardless of the result.

**Variation A — `Moms43 - 4 - V3`, re-cut.** The account's largest single line item, `ACTIVE`, holding 3.81% against a 12% floor and running $24.68 over the trailing 30 days. She currently arrives at 0:18. Move her to 0:03. Everything after her entry stays as it is.

This is the higher-value of the two and also the riskier one, and `iterations.md` says why: **ads with a large amount of spend typically need higher iteration swings**, because the audience has been saturated with the current creative. It also says the opposite risk out loud — **protect the winners ruthlessly.** So this variation is a layer, not a replacement. The original keeps running.

**Variation B — `MOMS38 - 1 - V1`, re-cut.** The paused ad that already clears the hold floor at 17.12% and already sends 24.8% of its spend to women 45 and over. She arrives at 0:14. Move her to 0:03.

This is the cleaner read of the hypothesis, because the ad is already doing the thing partially and the edit sharpens it rather than transplanting it. It also answers a question sitting under this whole concept: **why is the better-holding, cheaper flagship the one that is switched off?**

**What both hold fixed:** the footage, the words, the message, the end card, the destination, and the claim structure. **What they must not do:** turn into new ads. `iterations.md`'s line on iteration versus recreation is the gut check — would someone watching the original and the re-cut back to back recognise them as related? If not, the drift has gone too far and this is a different task.

**One production note.** `iterations.md`'s warning about the cut point is the single most likely way this fails in the edit bay. Moving a spoken segment from 0:14 to 0:03 means the energy, the audio level and the visual style at the new seam have to flow. If the montage returns after her, the return has to feel like a continuation rather than a restart.

## Creator direction

**Talent need: none new. This is an edit brief, not a shoot brief.** No creator, no casting call, no location, no shoot day. The brand names casting and creator sourcing as its number one creative bottleneck and production as its fourth [`stated`, brand context]. **This concept touches neither.**

`creator-briefs.md` applies thinly, but its reference-led discipline shapes what the editor receives — a reference does more than a paragraph of description, and here the references are the brand's own two ads.

**What the editor receives:**

- **The two source ads, with timecodes.** `Moms43 - 4 - V3`, her entry at 0:18. `MOMS38 - 1 - V1`, her entry at 0:14. Those two entries are the material.
- **The instruction in one line.** She opens. Everything else keeps its order.
- **A named seam risk.** The cut back into the existing body has to match on energy, pacing and visual style, or the stitch feels forced and the viewer drops. Watch the first two seconds after her opening line as carefully as the opening itself.
- **Pacing direction, from `advertising-to-older-audiences.md`.** Slower edits, fewer cuts, shots that hold. Fast jumpy cutting reads as chaos rather than energy to this band, and this ad is being re-cut specifically to reach them. The account's own evidence agrees from the other side: the better-holding of the two source ads is the slower one.
- **Sound direction.** Nothing in front of her voice in the first three seconds. The current flagship opens on an acoustic track playing mid-phrase over a silent shot, with no spoken word until 0:18, against an average play time of about four seconds. **In this category the voice is the sound design and it has to arrive immediately.** And 10 of the 19 relevant videos in the category feed carry no background music at all [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/monthly-organic-tiktok-audit.md`].
- **Legibility on any overlay that survives the re-cut.** Big, high contrast, one clear thing on screen at a time. Small or compressed text sinks otherwise-fine ads with this audience, and legibility is not a nicety here, it is the whole game.

**The magic moment — the visual instant that stops the scroll.** It is **her face, already speaking, in frame zero.** `visuals.md` principle 4: the feed is a social graph, not a broadcast channel, so visuals with people and especially faces outperform — a face anchors attention, signals relevance and shrinks the felt distance of "this is an ad." Principle 5 adds the sharper version: emotional specificity beats generic positivity, and a face mid-expression carries a specific state where a warm montage carries none. **Protect frame zero. Nothing goes in front of her.**

**If a new line or overlay is needed to make the new opening land**, that is a copy deliverable and it leaves this brief. It routes through the hooks or headlines skill with both gate receipts. **It is not written here and it is not written in the edit bay.**

**The call to action mirrors her real apprehension.** Hers is eligibility, not price — she assumes she is not included and stops before the form. That is a line of copy rather than a trust rebuild, which `personas/personas-profile.md` calls the cheapest fix among all three personas' objections. If the re-cut has room for one added reassurance, that is the one worth adding, and it goes through the headlines skill.

**Two. The honest weakness, named rather than buried.** The cost-per-lead gap is **$1.40, about 6.5%**, on two ads that differ in more than one way. The hold gap is four and a half times and much harder to explain away. **The cost gap is small**, and this brief does not lean on it.

**Three. The thing that genuinely undercuts this concept, and the sprint plan says to state it plainly rather than sell around it.** **In this account, the ads that hold attention cost more per lead, not less.** The three formats with the strongest hold sit at **$32.00, $31.82 and $33.62** against the account's trailing **$22.29**, all on under about $2,100 of lifetime spend each. **Four. ## Validation — strategy

**Which priority.** Roadmap **Priority 3**, built directly and almost literally: open the ads at 0:03 with the woman who already delivers the pitch at 0:14.

**Which persona.** `already-knows`, secondary standing, **mixed** confidence, emotional-intensity rank 1, roughly 19% of spend today and **none of it built for her**. Three of eight concepts this round go to her and this is the cheapest of the three.

**Which product priority.** Same product, same destination, same funnel, same script. The change is who appears first.

**How it fits the diagnosis.** The diagnosis says the account addresses a woman who is uninsured and wants rescuing while reaching a woman who is insured and broke. `already-knows` is the sharpest version of that gap: she is insured, has been for decades, is expensive enough to care and often healthy enough to pass underwriting, and Meta finds her without anyone aiming. **This concept stops finding her by accident and does it with an edit.**

**Why the roadmap puts it third rather than second.** It is a casting and edit move, not a premise move, so it serves Priority 2 rather than replacing it. **Why third rather than lower.** It is the cheapest real thing on the list to produce and one of very few casting moves that can still land before Open Enrollment.

The efficiency-before-volume rule decides it — a 41.5% premium is not defensible while the gate that would justify it is invisible. **So this brief is a sized bet at the cheapest possible price, and it says so.**

## Confidence and what this tests

**The hypothesis, stated so it can fail.** Moving the existing spokesperson from 0:14 and 0:18 to **0:03 lifts hold above the 12% floor without raising cost per lead.**

Read the second clause carefully. It is *without raising* cost per lead, not *lowering* it. That is deliberate, and it is the sprint plan's instruction.

**What would falsify it.** A re-cut that clears the hold floor and raises cost per lead would confirm roadmap loop 2 — that in this account, holding attention and buying a cheap lead are different jobs — and that answer is worth more than the two ads. A re-cut that fails to lift hold at all would say the four-second gap between the two flagships was explained by something other than when she arrives, which matters because **J002 rests on the same read.**

**Confidence: moderate.**

- That she already delivers the pitch in both giants is **verified** from `creator_demographic` and `ad_summary`.
- That casting acts as an age lever is **verified**, reproducing three independent ways.
- That leading with her lifts hold is **inferred** from two ads that differ in more than one variable — but it is the strongest available inference, because the hold gap is 4.5x rather than marginal.
- That leading with her lowers cost per lead is **inferred from a $1.40 gap and is explicitly not what this brief claims.**
- What she actually responds to is **data-limited**, because nothing has ever been built for her.

**Gate two is dark.** 44 `Call` events across 16 ads against 4,421 leads in 90 days. And it matters here in a specific and hopeful way: this band is often healthy enough to pass underwriting and expensive enough to care, which makes her plausibly closer to the product's real buyer than a 28-year-old with a newborn. **That read is `inferred`, resting on the shape of who employer coverage and medical underwriting select for rather than on any enrollment data.** It is the single most attractive unverified idea in this round and it costs one conversation with the partner agencies to check.

**What this concept genuinely is.** The lowest-cost, fastest-to-ship, most-reversible bet in the round. It needs no person, no shoot and no new claim, and it can land inside the Open Enrollment window when almost nothing else in this round can. **It is also the concept most likely to be quietly skipped**, because a re-cut does not feel like new creative and there is no shoot day to schedule. That is worth naming to whoever owns the round.

## Open loops

Two questions this brief left behind. The prompt's output template does not name a loops section; it is here because this brain carries one on every context doc and because the run instruction requires it.

**1. The account's better-holding, cheaper flagship is the one that is switched off, and no document records the decision.**

`MOMS38 - 1 - V1` holds 17.12%, clears the craft floor, costs $20.15 a lead and sends 24.8% of its spend to women 45 and over. It reads `PAUSED`. `Moms43 - 4 - V3` holds 3.81%, costs $21.55 lifetime and $24.68 over the last 30 days, and is `ACTIVE` with $15,104.97 behind it in that window.

*Pull: Tension.* Two things this brain treats as settled cannot both be right: the team steers by cost per lead, and the cheaper of its two flagships is the one that is off.

*Question: What decided that the better-holding, cheaper flagship got switched off and the other one kept the budget?*

*Why it matters:* if it was a deliberate call on a signal nobody wrote down — a lead-quality read from the partner agencies, a fatigue call, a fulfilment constraint — this round is building straight into it, and this concept re-cuts the very ad that was rejected. If it was drift, then **re-enabling one existing ad is cheaper than this entire brief and should be tested first.** That is a real possibility and it should be checked before an editor is booked.

*Territory: Messaging.*

**2. Nobody has asked whether a re-cut of a heavily delivered ad gets read as new.**

Of 95 ad-name groups created since 2026-08-01, 81 never took $500. This concept's whole advantage is that it re-uses assets delivery already understands. But a re-cut is also a new ad object, and `iterations.md` describes a hook change as effectively resetting the entity ID — which is normally the *point*, and here might mean the re-cut lands in the same eighty-one that never got read.

*Pull: Gap.* It fired because the round's cheapest concept depends on an assumption about Meta's delivery that nobody in this brain has tested.

*Question: Does a re-cut of a high-spend ad inherit any of that ad's delivery, or does it start cold like every other new file?*

*Why it matters:* it decides how this concept gets launched. If it starts cold, it needs the same deliberate funding as everything else in the round and the "it is nearly free" framing is only half true — the edit is free, the read is not. This is the roll-up's Tier 1 loop 5 arriving from the iteration side.

*Territory: Messaging.*
