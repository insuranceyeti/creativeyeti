# Brief — Two numbers, two colours, no adjective

## The concept

**A full-frame static that sets two of the brand's own figures side by side in contrasting colour, so the gap reads in a glance, with no claim written anywhere on it.**

That last clause is the whole strategic point, and it is why this concept sits in Lane C rather than Lane A. **Priority 1 pulls two unsupported claims out of essentially every ad this account runs.** A comparison demonstrates value **without making an explicit claim**, which is exactly what a brand in that position has to replace them with. `hooks.md` names the shape as format 17, Comparison, and names its whole move as **value demonstration without an explicit claim** — which matters enormously for a brand whose claims are the thing under legal pressure.

### Where it comes from

The device is lifted from the largest video in this brand's mining library, and the media read is pulled rather than inferred from a label. **@drashleehendry** phones hospitals asking what a delivery costs a self-pay patient. As each figure comes back down the line she writes it into a notebook and mirrors it on screen — **red for the price billed with insurance in play, green for the cash price**, stacked so the two sit side by side. SVD, $2,760 in red against $2,208 in green. C-section, $3,103 in red against $2,482 in green. SVD, $5,840 red against $2,244 green. They stay on screen. She narrates the call around them and **she never characterises the numbers. There is no adjective anywhere in the device. The colour does the whole job of saying which one you want.**

**6,200,000 views, 661,800 likes, 208,000 shares, 22,700 comments, 108,770 saves**, re-pinned live 2026-09-04 — **66.1% of all 9,376,300 views in the pool**, roughly seven times the next video [`verified`, Parker MCP `search_tiktok_videos`].

**What transfers is the device, not the video.** `adapting-scripts.md`'s discipline and `ideation-and-brainstorming.md`'s rule agree: translate the mechanism, not the surface. The mechanism here is two numbers, two colours, no adjective. **It does not need a phone call and it does not need the pregnancy subject**, which is roads-not-taken 2 in the roadmap and out of bounds. It is a static.

### Why the brand can make this tomorrow

`sub-context-docs/visual-vocabulary.md` files "colour as the argument" as **adjacent, inferred producible**, and the capability read is specific: **the brand already renders full-frame text graphics with green checkmarks and already animates numbers inflating inside its claymation, so a two-colour number comparison is a design change, not a production one.** Nothing in this brand's library currently uses colour to make a comparison, and the whole grievance in this category is a price.

`iterations.md` puts format at the top of the signal-change ladder and calls a format swap on a proven message one of the historically highest-confidence iterations available. **Here the proven format is the dormant one:** statics turn a link click into a lead at **16.57% against video's 8.28%** lifetime, and a static-filtered pull of everything created since 2026-06-01 returns **zero rows** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04].

### The compliance question that has to be settled before design

**A two-colour comparison that puts a rival's price in red breaks the brand's ban on named competitor attacks outright.** That ban sits in the section the brand itself marks non-negotiable, alongside the rule that **the villain in any narrative is always the system or the mechanism, never a named company or individual.**

**So the comparison has to be one of two things: between two of the brand's own figures, or between a figure and a category norm with no company named.** That distinction is the whole compliance question and the sprint plan says plainly it has to be settled before anything is briefed. This brief settles it: **the two numbers are the brand's own, or one of them is an unattributed category norm. No rival. No named company. No implied one.**

**And a second constraint that binds harder than it looks.** Any figure on this frame has to be real and checkable. The brand's non-negotiable section bans fabricated statistics, and **nothing anywhere in this brain states what the 20%, 24% or 30% compares against** [`verified`]. So a comparison built on "up to 30%" cannot show a derived dollar figure unless somebody can say what it was derived from. **That is roadmap validation item 3 and it is unanswered.** If it stays unanswered, the honest version of this concept compares two things the brand can actually stand behind rather than two things it can only assert.

### The one thing this concept must not do

**Do not lead with a number as a promise.** Her documented response to a figure is to beat it. "Mine is" appears in **82 of 1,342 comments, 6.2%, across 8 distinct ad names, and of the 76 that name a figure, 64 — 84% — name a number above the ad's** [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. On 2025-07-28, under a different ad with a different figure, someone already wrote *"600? Try 1000+, then this ad would be more realistic."*

**A comparison is not the same move as a claim, and that is why this survives the rule.** A single figure presented as what she will save invites a counter-bid. Two figures presented as a gap invites a comparison. The distinction is real and the design has to protect it: **the frame shows a relationship, not a promise.**

## TEEP phase and emotional shift

**Where she is, in plain speech.** Mid-scroll, not shopping, carrying a number she has stopped questioning because she has no other number to hold it against. She has never seen what the same thing costs a different way.

**So this ad's job is to wake her up to a gap she has never been shown, in one glance, without telling her what to think about it.** That is an awakening rather than a switch: the switch concepts in this round ask her to change something she is already doing, and this one shows her a fact she did not have.

**The shift the frame has to create:** *from* "this is what it costs, and that's that" *to* "wait — those two numbers are for the same thing?"

**The emotion the sprint plan names is quiet shock**, and the word quiet is doing real work. `emotional-delivery-and-timing.md` explains why: high-arousal states narrow attention and suppress the reflective processing that builds identification; low-arousal states expand it. **A frame that shouts about a gap is a scare tactic, which is a brand rule breach. A frame that simply shows one is not.** The colour carries the intensity so the words do not have to, which is exactly the source's own trick — she never says the word expensive.

**And this is the concept in the round that reaches furthest across personas.** `advertising-to-older-audiences.md` puts **big, high-contrast, one clear thing on screen** at the top of what works for the 45-and-over band, and adds that this group is value-conscious and rewards value made legible rather than urgency or hype. **A two-number colour comparison is that instruction rendered literally.** `personas/personas-profile.md` reaches the same place for `already-knows` at the Purchase phase: legibility, big text, high contrast, the value spelled out plainly.

## Variations

Two. The sprint plan set the count and the reason is the counterweight below, plus the round-level guardrail: **two here plus two on J003 is four paper assets out of twenty, which is a real bet without being the round.**

`static-ad-design.md` says the instinct to change one variable at a time works against congruency on a static, because message and image have to move together. So these are **congruent variations** — two complete expressions of the same device, differing on what the two numbers are.

**Variation A — the brand's own two figures.** Both numbers belong to the brand and both are checkable. The comparison is internal, which means it names nobody and cannot be read as an attack on anyone. This is the compliant control and it is the one that ships regardless of how validation item 3 is answered.

**Variation B — a figure against an unattributed category norm.** One number is the brand's; the other is what the category does, with **no company named anywhere on the frame** and the villain kept structural. This is the sharper ad and it is the one carrying the compliance risk, so it gets a legal read before it is designed rather than after it is built.

**What both hold fixed:** two numbers, two colours, no adjective, no claim, no person, no mockup, no engagement counts. Full-frame graphic in the brand's own black-ground grammar. Destination `www.healthformoms.co/save/`.

**What they must not do:** become a list. The account's one proven document ad is a **wall of state names**, and it converts its clicks worst among real spenders. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 10 is the constraint — the first frame is a budget, not a canvas, and one channel does the real work while the others stay quiet. **Two numbers is the whole frame. A third number is a different, worse ad.**

## Creator direction

**Talent need: none.** No creator, no shoot, no location, no casting call. This is the cheapest asset in the round, and the brand names casting and creator sourcing as its number one creative bottleneck. `creator-briefs.md`'s freedom-versus-direction call resolves to the **director's brief** end: the design is specified and the judgment lives in hierarchy, colour and type.

**The magic moment — the visual instant that stops the scroll.** `static-ad-design.md` calls it the **money shot**: the image that communicates the benefit instantly, with no reading required. **Two numbers side by side in two colours is a money shot for a category whose whole grievance is a price.** `visuals.md` says the same thing from the principles side — demonstration beats symbolism, because demonstration survives the sound being off, and half-second clarity is non-negotiable.

**Direction the designer receives:**

- **Hierarchy, named explicitly.** Primary: the two numbers and the gap between them. Secondary: the smallest possible label that says what they are. Tertiary: the wordmark. **Nothing else exists on the frame.** If every element is the same size and weight, nothing leads.
- **Colour does the argument.** Red and green in the source, and the brand's own grammar already uses green as its affirmative colour — the wall of state names runs under a heading with a green checkmark, and the account's full-frame graphic cards use green checkmarks throughout [`verified`, `sub-context-docs/visual-vocabulary.md`]. **The palette is already the brand's. What is new is using it to compare rather than to confirm.**
- **Stack them so the gap is the shape.** In the source the two figures sit stacked and stay on screen, so the difference reads as a distance rather than as two facts. That spatial relationship is the device. **Do not place them side by side in a way that makes them read as two options.**
- **No adjective. No claim. No exclamation.** The source never characterises the numbers and neither does this. `hooks.md` format 17 is explicit that the psychological basis is the contrast effect and value demonstration without an explicit claim. **The moment an adjective appears, the frame becomes a claim and inherits every compliance problem this concept exists to avoid.**
- **Specific over round.** The brand's own stated preference: *"odd, specific numbers (e.g., $587) are more believable than round ones ($600)."* The source's numbers are $2,760, $2,208, $3,103, $2,482 — none of them round. That is not a coincidence.
- **Legibility above everything.** Big, high contrast, one clear thing at a time. Small or compressed text sinks otherwise-fine ads with the 45-and-over band, and this concept is deliberately aimed to run wide.
- **The scroll-stop devices, used on purpose.** Colour contrast is doing the primary work here, so the rest stay quiet: white space so the two numbers have room, minimal font variation, no arrows fighting the colour. Using every trick at once recreates the wall of noise.
- **Native, not corporate.** `visuals.md` principle 6, and the brand's own tone rule that it should never sound like a corporate insurance company. A clean designed card can still look like something a person made.

**The call to action mirrors her real apprehension.** Fifteen comments across ten ads guard the phone number and the sharpest drew 33 likes [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. On a frame with almost no words, the ask is the only place tone can go wrong. **Keep it quiet and name the hesitation rather than pushing through it.**

## Validation — strategy

**Which priority.** Roadmap **Priority 1** — it does the specific job Priority 1 creates, which is to demonstrate value once the two unsupported claims come out. It serves **Priority 2** on format, as a static pointed at the destination that works.

**Which persona.** `pays-and-still-owes` nominally, and it **runs wide**. `already-knows` in particular, because big high-contrast one-thing-on-screen is what works for the 45-and-over band and because the account's own state-list device already reaches her. That fit is **inferred** — it rests on a craft doc plus delivery data, and nobody has put a colour comparison in front of this audience, so it is a reasoned bet rather than a measured one.

**Which product priority.** This is the only concept in the round that touches how the offer is **presented** rather than what is said about it, which is the closest thing in the bank to a product lever. The evaluation's second coverage hole is worth repeating here because it frames what this concept is and is not: counting primary levers across the bank breaks down **messaging 13, creator and talent 6, persona 1, product 0.** Not one entry in the bank is about the destination, the form, the plan, the price or the handoff to the agency. **This round briefs messaging and hopes the funnel keeps up.** That is a named risk, and J008 is the only asset in it that even brushes the product surface.

**How it fits the diagnosis.** The diagnosis says the account addresses a woman who is uninsured while reaching a woman who is insured and broke, and that the creative has the wrong premise. **A comparison is premise-neutral**: it does not assume she is uninsured, it does not assume she is rescued, and it does not tell her what to feel. It shows her a gap and lets her do the arithmetic. On a brand that has to remove two claims and cannot yet say what they compared against, that is the safest strong thing available.

If Priority 1 is confirmed as the clear first priority, Lane C deserves more than two concepts and this one is the cheapest to multiply.

**And on this concept, one gate outranks the copy gates entirely: the numbers themselves have to be real.**

2. **No company named, implied or logo'd.** Not a rival's name, not its colours, not a recognisable stand-in. The villain is the system.
3. Neither banned string: *"saving you 20% or more"*, *"Approved by thousands of Mom's across America"*.
4. No verification badge, no invented handle, no printed engagement counts, no platform mockup of any kind.

6. No government or ACA reference, no scare tactic, no political framing.

## Confidence and what this tests

**The hypothesis, stated so it can fail.** A colour comparison of the brand's own figures **converts clicks better than the account's one existing document ad's 5.97% leads per link click**, and delivers at a cost per lead better than the account's trailing **$22.29**.

Two clauses, and the first is the sharper one, because it is a like-for-like test of a specific idea: **that the state list's conversion problem is information density rather than the document device itself.**

**What would falsify it.** Two frames converting at or below 5.97% leads per link click. That would say a designed frame in this account buys curiosity clicks regardless of how simple it is, and it would settle a question sitting under four assets in this round and three ideas in the bank. **That is a genuinely useful loss.**

**Confidence: mixed.**

- That the source device works at scale is **verified** at 6,200,000 views on one organic video.
- That the parent device stops this audience cheaply is **verified** in-account at a 3.15% CTR and a $0.96 cost per link click.
- That the parent device converts poorly is also **verified**, at 5.97% against 12.25%.
- That colour comparison fixes the conversion half is **inferred**, and it is the bet. The link between the organic source and the in-account device is reasoned, not tested.
- That the brand can produce it without a shoot is **inferred producible** from its own library, which already renders full-frame text graphics with green checkmarks and already animates numbers.
- That it reaches the 45-and-over band well is **inferred** from a craft doc plus delivery data.

**Gate two is dark.** 44 `Call` events across 16 ads against 4,421 leads in 90 days. And the standing warning applies with particular force to a cheap, fast, low-friction static click: `performance-targets-and-metrics.md` says a last-click form fill **over-rewards the creative that produces fast, low-friction clicks** on a phone-mediated sale. **This clears gate one and nothing more.**

**One honest note about what this concept is.** It is the cheapest asset in the round and the one most likely to be built well in an afternoon. It is also the one whose evidence chain has the most inference in it — one organic video, one in-account parent device that half-works, and a reasoned link between them. **Cheap and uncertain is a good combination for a two-variation test and a bad one for a lane.** Build two, read them, then decide about the other two document ideas the evaluation folded behind this.

## Open loops

Two questions this brief left behind. The prompt's output template does not name a loops section; it is here because this brain carries one on every context doc and because the run instruction requires it.

**1. Nobody can say what the brand's own headline number compares against, and this concept wants to draw it.**

The brand's non-negotiable rule requires savings to read "up to 30%" with the "up to" intact. **Nothing anywhere in this brain states what the 20%, 24% or 30% is measured against** — not a benchmark plan, not a national average, not a prior premium. A comparison ad is the first asset in this account that would have to answer that question in order to exist.

*Pull: Gap.* It fired because the round's cheapest concept runs straight into the one number the brand has never substantiated.

*Question: What does "up to 30%" compare against?*

*Why it matters:* it is roadmap validation item 3, and it decides whether Priority 1 is a repair or a rebuild. It also decides whether the sharper version of this concept can be built at all, or whether the brand is limited to comparing two internal figures it can stand behind. **One conversation with whoever set the number.**

*Territory: Product.*

**2. Four assets in this round are designed frames, and the account's only real evidence about designed frames says they stop well and convert badly.**

`moms-53 3` buys the cheapest link click in the file at $0.96 and the best CTR at 3.15%, then converts at 5.97% leads per link click against 12.25% on the account's best video. Two J003 frames and two J008 frames are betting that simplicity is the difference.

*Pull: Tension.* Two things this round treats as settled cannot both be right: statics convert link clicks to leads at twice video's rate lifetime, and this account's only current document ad converts them worst among real spenders.

*Question: Is the state list's conversion gap about the format, the density, or the page it lands on?*

*Why it matters:* it is the same question J003's loop asks from the copy side, and together the four frames in this round are the instrument for it. If the answer is the page, then no amount of static craft moves it and the next hunt has to open on the product lever the bank has zero entries for — which the evaluation already named as its second-largest hole.

*Territory: Product.*
