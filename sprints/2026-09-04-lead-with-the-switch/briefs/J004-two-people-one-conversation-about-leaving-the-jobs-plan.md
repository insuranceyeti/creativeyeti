# Brief — Two people, one conversation, about leaving the job's plan

## The concept

**The switch message carried inside a real exchange between two women** — either two people in frame, or one on each end of a line.

This is the evaluation's first alternate, re-aimed. `the-real-call-not-the-skit` ranked fifth overall and it ranked there on two honest grounds: its lead economics are unproven, and the thing that made its source work is a genuinely recorded call the brand may not be able to make. **So the sprint plan did not run it as its own idea. It pointed the mechanic at the hero message**, which is what turns a strong format with no message into a concept.

**Why a second person is the single most reliable structural lever in this account.** Every configuration in this account that puts a second character in frame or on the line clears or nearly clears the 12% hold floor. Every configuration that leaves one woman alone with her phone sits under 6% [`verified`, carried from the roadmap's live pulls]:

| `MOMS38 - 2 - V3` | one actor, two roles, phone call | $1,823.59 | 59 | $30.91 | 51.78% | **24.48%** |
| `MOMS38 - 2 - V4` | one actor, two roles, phone call | $2,172.45 | 95 | $22.87 | 55.57% | **27.31%** |

**27.31% and 24.48% are the two highest hold rates in this account's entire history**, against a 12% craft floor and a biggest-spender hold of 3.81%. Two of the phone-call builds also run at or below the window's own cost per lead while doing it. And the two-woman podcast conversation bought **175 leads at $19.90** — inside the brand's own "good" band — and **has not been repeated in ten months**, created 2025-11-24.

**Why the mechanic works, named properly.** `hooks.md` puts **precognitive sound** among only four things that reliably stop a scroll inside the first second: familiar audio with half of it missing — one side of a phone call, an audible pause, no reply. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 3 says why the stop turns into a stay: the absent half is a **felt gap**, and an unresolved loop stays active in working memory and nags until it closes. Mechanic 9, narrative transportation, is the other half — a conversation is the one mechanism that genuinely **buys time**, because the viewer is reaching for the resolution rather than tolerating a pitch.

**And there is a second, softer reason it fits this brand specifically.** The `MOMS38 - 2` skit family is the only creative in the entire corpus that earns **agreement instead of argument**. Nearly all of the roughly 12 clear recognitions in 1,342 comments sit there [`verified`, `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md`]. *"'It's out of network' 'But it's in the same building!' 'In a different hallway' 🤣🤣🤣🤣🤣 whoever came up with this ad deserves a raise!! Love this!"* 2026-04-03. *"TRUTH! A SAD TRUTH."* *"Had a similar conversation recently with my soon to be former insurance company."* Everything else in this account gets corrected, counter-bid or warned about. This one gets recognised.

### What the source is, and what travels across from it

The organic anchor is **@drashleehendry's cold call**: a doctor films herself phoning hospitals to ask what a delivery costs a self-pay patient. **6,200,000 views, 661,800 likes, 208,000 shares, 22,700 comments, 108,770 saves**, re-pinned live 2026-09-04 — **66.1% of all 9,376,300 views in the brand's mining library**, roughly seven times the next video. Like rate 10.67%, share rate 3.36%, save rate 1.75%, against the library's 5.78% like and 1.31% save middle. `global_is_ad` is recorded false, and a 3.36% share rate is not what a boosted post looks like [`verified`, Parker MCP `search_tiktok_videos`].

`adapting-scripts.md`'s discipline applies: **translate the mechanism, not the surface.** What transfers is the shape — one side of a real conversation, an audible pause, a stranger's voice supplying an answer the viewer has personally wondered about. What does **not** transfer is the subject. Hendry is asking about pregnancy pricing, and pregnancy is roads-not-taken 2 in the roadmap for a hard reason: the plans are medically underwritten and the funnel screens her out. **Borrow the call. Leave the topic.**

### The line this concept does not cross

**A genuinely unscripted recorded call with a real institution is marked out of play** in `sub-context-docs/visual-vocabulary.md`, and the reasoning is not squeamishness. Every filmed unit in this account carries a paid-actor disclaimer, and the account's own three phone-call ads are comedy written in advance — the team's own ad set is literally named "20k skit v2", and the transcript of `MOMS38 - 2 - V3` has both voices performed by actors, including the line *"You need to be declared clinically dead and revived twice"* [`verified` from the script block]. The capability the brand has never shown is filming a real interaction it does not control, with a second party who consents.

**So this brief builds the version that does not need one**, and names the version that would. Two women having a conversation about leaving an employer plan is a conversation the brand can stage honestly with a dramatization disclaimer, the way it already does. A recorded call to a real insurer is a different asset with a different legal question, and it is the brand's to answer.

## TEEP phase and emotional shift

**Where she is, in plain speech.** She has decided nothing. She is carrying a belief she has never said out loud — that the plan her job picked is the only plan she is allowed — and she has never had a reason to check it. Twenty-four comments name an employer and seven name "my job", carried on 1,322, and the family keeps arriving from March through August while nearly everything else went quiet after May [`verified`].

**So this ad's job is to let her overhear the fact instead of being told it.** That is the whole reason the concept is a conversation rather than a monologue. A woman being sold to has her guard up. A woman listening to two other women talk does not.

**The shift the ad has to create:** *from* "this is just how it is, and everyone I know is in the same boat" *to* "hang on — she just said you can say no to that."

**And the delivery mechanism that makes it land: the relief arrives through somebody else's mouth.** The sprint plan names the leading emotion exactly that way. `emotional-delivery-and-timing.md` explains why it matters: the quadrant most brands underinvest in is the calm, positive, low-intensity one, and it wins because it creates the identification high-intensity creative cannot. A second voice granting permission is that quadrant, structurally — `hooks.md` format 21 even names it, saying a permission hook **pairs well with a two-voice structure**, a peer naming the feeling and someone kinder or more credentialed granting the release.

**What that forbids.** No distress opener — the account's own thirty-day numbers put distress openers at $29.85 to $38.09 against calm openers at $13.29 to $24.45 [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/monthly-hook-audit.md`], and the roadmap rules distress out of this round entirely. No mocking a named company: the brand's rule is that **the villain is always the system, never a named company or individual**, which matters a lot here, because the mechanic's natural comic target is a specific insurer's phone line. **Keep the antagonist structural.** The `MOMS38 - 2` skit already does this — the villain is the hallway rule, not a brand.

## Variations

Two. The sprint plan set the count and the reason is the honest counterweight: **all three in-account versions of this mechanic sit under about $2,100 of lifetime spend each and deliver at $30.91, $22.90 and $16.65.** The attention is proven. The lead cost is not. A handful of leads swings each of those numbers, so the round funds a real test rather than a big bet.

They diverge on **who the second person is**, which is the loudest variable in the mechanic.

**Variation A — two women in frame, talking to each other.** The proven shape here is `MOMS25 BLAST - X1 - Copy 2`, two women in a podcast studio, **175 leads at $19.90 on $3,483.03 with a 10.29% hold**, created 2025-11-24 and never repeated in ten months. One of them is carrying the belief; the other knows the fact. The mechanic is overhearing. `visuals.md` principle 4 says a face anchors attention and shrinks the felt distance of "this is an ad" — two faces in a real exchange does that harder than one talking at a lens.

**Variation B — one side of a call, the other side audible.** The proven shape is the `MOMS38 - 2` family, the two highest hold rates in the account's history. This is the precognitive-sound version: the viewer arrives mid-conversation, hears half of it, and stays to close the gap. It can be one actor playing two roles, which the account has already done three times, or two performers.

**What both hold fixed:** the switching message, the calm register, the system as the villain, `www.healthformoms.co/save/` as the destination, and a dramatization disclaimer. **What they must not do:** land in the same room with the same energy. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 2, habituation, is blunt about it — familiarity works at the level of the *format*, so when you reach for a proven shape the real work is the execution variable, the unexpected setting or the fresh visual, because that is the only part the brain has not filed yet. The account has run the phone skit three times already. **The setting has to be new.**

## Creator direction

**Talent need: two women, or one actor playing two roles.** Both are proven in this account, which is unusually comfortable ground for a casting-constrained brand.

**And the flag that sits on top of it:** this concept is **gated on the same brand answer as J007** — whether a real licensed partner advisor can be filmed and what compliance would let her say. That is roadmap validation item 4 and it is one conversation. **If the answer is yes, J004 and J007 merge into one shoot day**, and the second voice in variation B becomes the credentialed one, which is a materially stronger ad. If the answer is no, this concept still ships in the form written above, with two ordinary women.

**The brief shape: freestyle, deliberately.** `creator-briefs.md` is explicit that when the power of an ad is a creator's natural, unscripted reaction, scripting it word for word kills the exact thing that made the source work. Part of this concept rides on a real reaction — the beat where one woman actually hears the fact for the first time. **So this stays open-ended: a reference, a story structure, and freedom on delivery.** Direction on structure, freedom on delivery, which `creator-briefs.md` calls the right split more often than not.

**What the creators receive:**

- **The story shape, three beats, not the words.** One woman states the belief the way this audience states it. The other supplies the fact. The first one reacts — and that reaction is the asset, so nobody rehearses it flat.
- **References, because a reference does more than a paragraph of description.** Three attach and each does a different job:
  - `MOMS38 - 2 - V4` — **the mechanic, at its best.** 55.57% hook, 27.31% hold, 15-second average play. Watch how fast it drops you into the middle of something.
  - `MOMS25 BLAST - X1 - Copy 2` — **the two-in-frame version**, and the proof this shape converts as well as it holds.
  - @drashleehendry's cold call — **the source, for the shape of a real conversation only.** Do not borrow the subject.
- **Creator vocabulary.** Direct-to-camera is what the account defaults to and is not what this is. This is a two-hander: either both in frame, or one in frame with the other's voice on a speakerphone. Handheld. One take where the reaction can breathe.
- **The setting is the fresh variable.** The account has already filmed the porch phone call and the podcast studio. `sub-context-docs/visual-vocabulary.md` marks **the unstyled room** as adjacent and cheap — a real bedroom, a real kitchen, a room that looks unplanned rather than staged, which the brand has never filmed. `visuals.md` principle 6 agrees: native context beats aesthetic polish, and a bit of mess beats pristine perfection.
- **Sound is the hook, so protect it.** No music over the first three seconds. **10 of the 19 relevant videos in the category feed have no background music at all** [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/monthly-organic-tiktok-audit.md`], and the account's biggest ad opens on an acoustic track playing mid-phrase over a silent shot with no spoken word until 0:18, against an average play time of about four seconds. In this category the voice is the sound design and it has to arrive immediately.

**The magic moment — the visual and audible instant that stops the scroll.** It is **the pause**. The half-second where one woman has said the thing and the other has not answered yet. `hooks.md` names silence as a sound hook in its own right. Everything in the edit protects that beat: it does not get cut tight, it does not get scored, and nothing overlays it.

**The call to action mirrors her real apprehension.** Fifteen comments across ten ads guard the phone number and the sharpest drew 33 likes [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. On a concept whose whole texture is two people talking honestly, an ask that suddenly turns into a hard pitch is the exact dissonance `hooks.md` calls the fakeout — a calm open with a high-pressure body, clocked even when she cannot name it. **The zone has to hold the whole way through.**

**Every line of customer-facing copy ships with both gate receipts** — the Grounding Review (`context-grounding-review` agent plus `scripts/grounding-check.py`) and the Voice Review (`creative-voice-review` agent plus `scripts/voice-lint.py`). Nothing gets filmed without both.

**Compliance runs before the shoot.** No fabricated statistics. Neither banned string — *"saving you 20% or more"*, *"Approved by thousands of Mom's across America"*. Savings always "up to 30%" with the "up to" intact; deductible always "$0 deductible option" or "options with no deductibles"; no hard guarantee; no government or ACA reference; no scare tactic; no synthetic presenter.

**Two that are specific to this concept and both are easy to trip:**

1. **The villain stays structural.** The comic energy of a call ad pulls hard toward a named insurer's phone line. The brand's rule is that the villain is always the system or the mechanism, never a named company or individual, and its own ban on named competitor attacks sits in the non-negotiable section. The `MOMS38 - 2` skit does this correctly — the antagonist is a hallway rule.
2. **Neither speaker implies a credential she does not hold.** The brand's rule requires a real licensed professional or language that implies no false credential. Two ordinary women talking about their own plans is clean. The moment one of them starts advising, this becomes J007 and it needs J007's casting answer.

The dramatization disclaimer the account already carries on every filmed unit travels with this one.

## Confidence and what this tests

**The hypothesis, stated so it can fail.** A second person in frame or on the line **clears the 12% hold floor while carrying the switch message**, at a cost per lead at or below the account's trailing **$22.29**.

Two clauses, deliberately. The hold clause is close to a redeploy — the shape has cleared the floor twice already. **The cost clause is the actual bet**, and it is the one that has never been proven at spend.

**What would falsify it.** Either half. Holding under 12% would say the shape does not survive being pointed at a permission message instead of a denial joke. Clearing the floor and landing above $22.29 would confirm the pattern in roadmap loop 2 — that in this account, holding attention and buying a cheap lead are different jobs — and that answer is worth more than the ads.

**Confidence: mixed.**

- That a second person clears the hold floor and one woman alone does not is **verified** across six ads with a consistent split.
- That the mechanic converts at a defensible cost is **not established**. Three tests, all under about $2,100, delivering $30.91, $22.90 and $16.65. A handful of leads swings each.
- That the message converts is **verified** on the statics, and **inferred** as travelling into this format.
- That two women in frame is the better of the two variations is **inferred**, from a single unrepeated ad at $19.90.

**Gate two is dark.** 44 `Call` events across 16 ads against 4,421 leads in 90 days. And there is a specific reason to care about it here: this is the only concept in the round whose mechanic reliably makes people **watch longer**, and if longer watching produces a better-informed lead, that would show up at gate two and nowhere else in this brain. **That is a real reason to ask the partner agencies about this concept's leads specifically when it ships.**

## Open loops

Two questions this brief left behind. The prompt's output template does not name a loops section; it is here because this brain carries one on every context doc and because the run instruction requires it.

**1. The account's best-priced conversational ad has sat unrepeated for ten months and nobody wrote down why.**

`MOMS25 BLAST - X1 - Copy 2` — two women in a podcast studio — bought 175 leads at $19.90 on $3,483.03 with a 10.29% hold, created 2025-11-24. That is inside the brand's own "good" band, in an account that has been in "meh" for six straight months. It has never been made again.

*Pull: Gap.* It fired because the account's own cheapest conversational asset stopped being produced with no recorded decision, exactly the way the statics did.

*Question: Why did this account stop making two-person conversation ads after November 2025?*

*Why it matters:* it is the same question as the statics one, on a different format, and the answer has the same two shapes. A staffing or production change means this concept ships fast. A learned constraint nobody wrote down means the round is walking into it twice.

*Territory: Creators and talent.*

**2. The funniest ad this brand has ever made buys its most expensive lead, and nobody has asked whether the comedy is the problem.**

`MOMS38 - 2 - V3` holds 24.48%, the highest in the account's history, and delivers a $30.91 lead — the brand's own worst tier. Its sibling `MOMS38 - 2 - V4` holds even higher at 27.31% and delivers $22.87. The plainest of the three, `OMC-Health for Moms-[B2-C13-V2]`, holds lowest at 9.15% and delivers the cheapest lead at $16.65.

*Pull: Surprise.* Across three ads of one mechanic, hold and cost per lead run in exactly opposite directions, and the ordering is clean rather than noisy.

*Question: Does entertainment in this account buy attention from people who were never going to fill in the form?*

*Why it matters:* it is roadmap loop 2 with a much sharper instrument, because these three ads share a format, a family and a production. If comedy attracts the wrong watcher, the register instruction for this whole lane changes — from funny to plainly overheard — and that is a brief-level change, not a media-buying one. All three sit under about $2,100, so the read is thin and worth widening deliberately.

*Territory: Messaging.*
