# Brief — The correction, delivered by somebody who holds the credential

## The concept

**A real licensed partner advisor on camera, using the category's vocabulary correctly, replacing an assertion with a person.**

This is Lane C of the round, and Lane C exists because of a hole nobody had named until the evaluation found it. **Priority 1 pulls two claims out of essentially every ad this account runs. Nothing in the idea bank was about what goes in their place.** Two of twenty entries touch Priority 1 and neither is about the repair itself. **A round that repairs the copy and puts nothing behind it has removed the brand's only two credibility markers and replaced them with silence.**

### The finding this concept is built on

**There is a standing volunteer army in this brand's comment sections whose entire job is correcting the ad's vocabulary.** They tell the brand, and each other, that a deductible is not an out-of-pocket maximum. **103 comments out of 1,342, across 13 distinct ad names** [`verified`, carried on the 1,322 denominator]. The spread across 13 ads is what makes it a pattern rather than an artifact of one creative — `customer-review-mining-method.md`'s denominator discipline is exactly what a count this size sitting on one ad would fail.

Several of them sign a credential. Cited here as customer language, verbatim, never as copy:

*"That's not a deductible that's a medical maximum out of pocket. You should educate people, not misinform!!"*

*"As a licensed insurance broker this is VERY misleading in the beginning. The first half is talking about a medical maximum out of pocket. A deductible is something you pay before your insurance kicks in with co insurance."*

*"Deductible is different than maximum out of pocket 😃 -an insurance agent"*

*"Bc careful woth that Max Out of Pocket. U can have a $0 Deductible but an $8,000 Max Out of Pocket. Pay attention to the fine lines bc that's how they get u. Working with health insurance. This is kinda a sneak trick that's hidden out in the open."*

And the sharpest one, which corrects the funnel rather than the vocabulary: *"And they're holding pre-existing conditions against you, which is illegal. Their system automatically bounced me out. Since I'm a certified application counselor for the healthcare marketplace I know these things I've done medical billing for 30 years plus. I wouldn't waste my time on this page."*

**Notice what they are not doing. They are not asking a question and they are not buying. They are teaching, in public, unpaid, under an ad they think is wrong.**

**The reusable idea is what that behaviour proves about the category: using the words right is itself a credential here.** And the absence on the other side is total — **across 147 delivering ads, nobody credentialed has ever spoken** [`verified`, `audits/2026-Q3/gaps-opportunities-inspo.md`].

### Why the brand needs this specifically, right now

**Every credibility marker this brand carries today is its own assertion.** Zero customer reviews. Zero press. No Better Business Bureau profile for the operating entity. Two pieces of praise in 1,342 comments, both from women who were told no [`verified`, `sub-context-docs/reputation-analysis.md`, 2026-09-04].

And the claim that currently does the credibility work is the one Priority 1 removes. A live sweep on 2026-09-04 for *"Approved by thousands of Mom's across America"* returned **127 of the account's 128 ad-name groups carrying $100,066.79 of the trailing 90-day window's $100,067.42**, identical to the sweep for the flat savings claim — same count, same dollar to the cent, which is the proof the two travel in one shared body-copy block [`verified live`]. **Nothing in this brain backs that approval claim.**

`killer-performance-ads.md` puts promise at rule two and proof at rule three, together, right after emotion. **This account has the promise without the proof**, and it is about to lose the fake version of the proof.

**And the strategic half is the useful half.** `sub-context-docs/category-and-market-research.md` documents **six enforcement events between 2024 and April 2026, four of them federal, all aimed at the marketing layer rather than at carriers** — an FTC suit in April 2026 over roughly $91 million alleging carrier and government impersonation; warnings to 21 health plan marketers and lead generators in December 2024 citing prior judgments of $195 million and $100 million; and the Benefytt case, which named **deceptive lead-generation websites** as part of the violation. **When regulators publicly define what bad looks like, a brand that is visibly and checkably not that has something real to stand on.** A real licensed advisor saying real things correctly is the cheapest version of that available.

### What this concept is not

**It is not the lab coat again.** That experiment already ran. `MOMS30 - 1 - V20` opens on a pediatrician in a white lab coat checking a baby's heartbeat and ran **$7,407.34 lifetime across 7 variants, 381 leads at $19.44, on a 39.38% hook rate and a 2.44% hold rate**, with **52.7% of its spend delivered to women 25 to 34** — the youngest delivery of anything inspected. The whole transcript is one line at 0:11 from an unnamed narrator. **The doctor never speaks** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04].

`killer-performance-ads.md` predicts that 2.44% hold exactly: an opener that promises authority and a body that never delivers it. **That is an unfinished test, not a failed one.** This concept is the opposite move: **the credential talks.**

**And it is not a doctor.** The brand's own rule requires that a doctor character be a real licensed physician or that the language imply no false credential. A licensed insurance advisor is not a physician and must not be dressed, framed or shot as one. **The credential this concept uses is the one the brand actually owns**: it routes every lead to real licensed partner advisors, so a credentialed speaker is **compliant by construction if the casting is real.**

### How this is a combination

- **The insight** comes from customer language: 103 corrections across 13 ad names, several signing a credential.
- **The absence it fills** comes from the account: 147 delivering ads and nobody credentialed has ever spoken; and against the `hooks.md` taxonomy, the account **never runs Authority** — one of six named formats it has never run, and the one the hook audit calls the loudest gap given the category is trust-sensitive.
- **The format proof** comes from the category feed: the single best-performing video in the entire 23-video mining library is a doctor doing investigative work on camera, at **6,200,000 views, roughly seven times the next video**, and it is a pure Authority hook.
- **The room** comes from the brand's own library. It has already filmed a clinical exam room and already stitched a real credentialed creator once. `sub-context-docs/visual-vocabulary.md` marks the credentialed voice speaking as **adjacent and inferred producible**, with a compliance condition attached.
- **The persona craft** comes from `advertising-to-older-audiences.md`: authority is the trust shortcut for this band, *"the news are their influencers"*, and framing the expert explicitly does more trust work than the same claim said by the brand.

## TEEP phase and emotional shift

**Where she is, in plain speech.** She knows the whole landscape. She has managed this for twenty years, she uses the terms correctly, and she corrects people who do not. What she does not know is whether **this specific company is real.** Her contextual identity in `personas/personas-profile.md` is literally the credentialed guard who steps in when a stranger asks for help in the thread — *"Quick tip for anyone with private health insurance. Ask about ancillary products... From a former insurance agent who is only offering advice since I am no longer selling,"* 2026-04-13.

**So this ad's job is to introduce a company to someone who already knows the category cold, by proving in the first seconds that whoever made it knows what they are talking about.** Not to explain a deductible. Not to sell a plan. To pass an examination she is already administering in the comments.

**The shift the ad has to create:** *from* "this is another ad that doesn't understand the thing it's selling" *to* "okay — she actually knows. I'll hear this out."

**Why the phase matters here specifically.** `emotional-delivery-and-timing.md` says the cognitive work in Exploration is *differentiation*, and what lands is **brand feeling, point of view and the emotional texture of what it is like to be a customer** — not features and not offers. `personas/personas-profile.md` reaches the same place independently for her: the work is credibility, and the format that carries it is something that looks like a report or an authority explaining, not a trend.

**And what it forbids.** Do not explain the category to her. The brand's own rule: *"don't over-explain concepts the audience already understands (e.g., what a deductible is) — assume problem-awareness and reframe the cause instead."* Her sophistication read in the persona doc is blunt about the consequence — **explaining the category to this audience would be doing the wrong thing well.** What she rewards is a specific receipt, because in a category where every brand claims savings, precision is the only thing that separates them.

No pressure at the ask. No hype. `advertising-to-older-audiences.md` says this band reads hype as a reason to distrust and rewards value made plain.

## Variations

Two. The sprint plan set the count and the reason is honest: **the outcome is unknown.** The absence of a credentialed speaker is verified; whether a correction delivered by the brand converts, or simply invites more corrections, is not. A funded two-variation test is the right size for a concept whose downside is a comment section full of people correcting the correction.

They diverge on **what the credential is doing**, which is the real variable.

**Variation A — she uses the words right, on purpose, in the first seconds.** The credential is demonstrated rather than announced: she opens by getting the distinction right, plainly, the way somebody who does this for a living would. The mechanism is `hooks.md` format 19, Authority. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 8 explains why it lands as risk reduction rather than bragging — under uncertainty, people treat evidence of competence as evidence of the right move, and the proof only fires if it is specific and hard to fake. **A person using the vocabulary correctly is the hardest-to-fake proof this brand can produce.**

**Variation B — she answers the thing people keep saying under the ads.** The credential is applied to a real objection rather than to vocabulary. The mechanism is `hooks.md` format 1, Comment Response — a format the hook audit confirms **the account has never truly run**, because the screenshot device it uses composites strangers' posts rather than answering its own commenters. And the material ranks itself, because the comments carry like counts: *"How many calls will I get if I try to see how this works?"* at 17 likes; *"Anyone actually have this and have insight?"* at 15; *"A Google search doesn't pull anything about this. Is it legit?"*

**One hard warning on variation B, and it decides whether it ships.** The most answerable questions here are about calls, eligibility and legitimacy, and **some honest answers are not flattering.** Pregnancy and pre-existing conditions screen applicants out at **42 comments across 15 ads and 55 across 17** respectively [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. **Answering a soft question in a thread where the hard ones are visible reads as evasive.** So variation B either picks a question the brand can answer completely and honestly, or it does not get made. That is a judgment for the team, not for a skill.

**What both hold fixed:** a real credential, the calm register, the destination, and the rule that the villain is the system. **What they must not do:** be two takes of one talking head. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 2, habituation, applies to format rather than topic — and an authority explainer is a format this audience has seen, so the execution variable carries the freshness.

**And one structural note the sprint plan flagged.** **If a licensed partner advisor can be filmed, J004 and J007 merge into one shoot day**, and the second voice in J004's phone-call variation becomes the credentialed one. That is a materially stronger asset than either alone and it costs the same day.

## Creator direction

**Talent need: a real licensed partner advisor. This is a hard gate and it is the whole risk of the concept.**

**Nobody has confirmed one can be filmed or what compliance would let her say.** That is roadmap validation item 4, it is the roll-up's question E1, and it is **one conversation.** The roadmap names it as the cheapest unlock on its list, because an advisor in her late forties satisfies Priority 1's proof problem and Priority 3's casting call at the same time, in one shoot.

**If the answer is no, this concept does not get built as written.** The sprint plan says so plainly and names the fallback: **the corrections are already written and already carry job titles**, so a cheaper version exists that uses the *material* rather than the *person*. That version is not this brief — it is a different concept with a different compliance profile and it would need its own brief, because presenting a stranger's credentialed comment as proof raises its own questions about consent and about what the brand is implying. **Do not let the fallback get built off this document.**

**The brief shape: freestyle, and `creator-briefs.md` is unusually direct about why.** **Authorities do better with more freedom.** A credible expert performs worse on a tight script and better with room to talk in her own voice, and the more the ad rides on who the person is, the looser the brief should be. This ad rides entirely on who she is. **Direction on structure, freedom on delivery.**

**What she receives:**

- **The story shape, not the words.** Two or three beats she builds through in her own voice. A tight script would make a licensed professional sound like an actor reading insurance copy, which is precisely the thing this concept exists to stop being.
- **The compliance envelope, up front and in writing.** She is a licensed advisor and not a physician. Savings are "up to 30%" with the "up to" intact. A deductible is a "$0 deductible option" or "options with no deductibles", never a flat or guaranteed outcome. No hard guarantees. No named competitor. No government or ACA reference — **and this is the constraint most likely to trip a real advisor**, because it is a natural part of how someone in her job explains the landscape. She needs to know that boundary before the camera rolls, not in the edit.
- **References, because a reference does more than a paragraph of description:**
  - **@drashleehendry** — **the register and the setting**, not the subject. A woman in her thirties in a white lab coat and navy medical polo at her own desk in her own clinic — neutral wall, abstract painting, exam table behind her, her practice logo in frame, phone on speaker, notebook open, pen in hand. **The only professional setting in all 23 videos, and it belongs to the video with two thirds of the library's views.** Her subject is pregnancy pricing and pregnancy is roads-not-taken 2. **Borrow the credibility and the room. Leave the topic.**
  - `MOMS30 - 1 - V20` — **the failure to avoid.** The doctor who never speaks, 2.44% hold.
  - `MOMS38 - 1 - V1` — **the pacing and the age match**, 17.12% hold, 24.8% of spend to women 45 and over.
- **The setting.** Her own workplace if she has one, or a real room that looks like somewhere a professional actually sits. `advertising-to-older-audiences.md` says familiar formats feel safe to this band and that credibility comes from the format looking like something they already trust — a report, an expert explaining, a channel that feels established — more than from follower counts. **And `visuals.md` principle 6 still applies: native context beats polish.** High-production location shooting is marked out of play in this brand's library. The room should look real, not produced.
- **Pacing.** Slower, warmer, unhurried. `advertising-to-older-audiences.md` calls the single most common misread treating this band as short-attention; they will follow a longer, problem-aware story. The account's own best-holding ad is the slower one.
- **Age match on the proof.** If she reads forty-five to fifty-five, this concept and Priority 3's casting call become the same asset. Proof from people their own age lands with this band; proof from a much younger face does not transfer.

**The magic moment — the instant that stops the scroll.** It is **the moment she says a thing correctly that ads in this category habitually say wrong.** That is the whole hook, and it is unusual because it is not a visual and it is not a claim — it is precision. `hook-psychology.md` mechanism 7 covers why it works anyway: the form must be effortless and the content must be a surprise. The surprise here is competence. **Protect it by not putting a graphic, a music sting or a benefit line in front of it.**

**No synthetic presenter, under any circumstances.** Six separate rejections across three years and four ad families, the closest thing to a rule in this brand's whole customer record. **On a concept whose entire product is credibility, an AI-generated authority is the single worst possible failure**, and it would also be the one place in this round where the legal disclosure question about synthetic humans would bite.

**The call to action mirrors her real apprehension.** Fifteen comments across ten ads guard the phone number and the sharpest drew 33 likes [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. A credentialed speaker is the one person in this round who can address that honestly, because she is the person on the other end of the call. **Say what actually happens when the form is filled in.** That is a proof asset in its own right and it costs nothing but honesty.

## Validation — data

**One. The behaviour is large, spread and unpaid.** **103 comments out of 1,342, across 13 distinct ad names**, correcting the deductible versus out-of-pocket-maximum distinction, several signing a credential [`verified`, carried on 1,322]. `personas/voice-of-customer/voc-category-jargon.md` calls this corpus's richest category and names the behaviour the correction economy.

**Two. The absence on the other side is total.** **Across 147 delivering ads, nobody credentialed has ever spoken** [`verified`]. And against the `hooks.md` taxonomy the account **never runs Authority**, alongside Investment, a true Comment Response, Comparison, Give Me Time and Viral Product. **Authority is the loudest of those gaps**, because the category is trust-sensitive and the niche organic shows a doctor outperforming everything else in the feed by a factor of seven [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/monthly-hook-audit.md`].

**Three. The one time the account came close, and why it failed.** `MOMS30 - 1 - V20`: **$7,407.34 lifetime across 7 variants, 381 leads at $19.44, 39.38% hook, 2.44% hold, 52.7% of spend to women 25 to 34.** The doctor never speaks. `killer-performance-ads.md` predicts that hold precisely from an opener that promises authority and a body that never delivers it.

Read the numbers honestly, both ways. **$19.44 is inside the brand's own "good" band** and better than the account's $18.78 lifetime blend is not — it is marginally worse. So the lab-coat ad was not a disaster on cost; it was a failure on attention and on audience, delivering to the youngest band in the account. **It is an unfinished test.**

**Four. This persona is the most attentive audience the account has.** She watches three to five times longer than the woman the account courts [`verified`, from delivery data]. And the format that reaches her — the skit family — sends **31.5% of its lifetime spend to women 45 and over** against POV's 10.6% [`verified`].

**Five. The register is settled.** Calm openers in this account delivered leads at **$13.29 to $24.45** while high-distress openers delivered at **$29.85 to $38.09**, blending to $33.23 across six ads, while posting the higher hook rates [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/monthly-hook-audit.md`]. And not one of the 17 hooks pulled from the relevant category videos opens on crying, shouting or visible distress. An authority explaining calmly is the natural home of that register.

**Six. What is NOT known, stated as plainly as the rest.** **Whether a correction delivered by the brand converts, or simply invites more corrections, is untested.** Nothing has ever been built for this persona and she has never been asked. The evidence is **strong on the absence and thin on the outcome**, and this brief carries that mark rather than upgrading it.

**Seven. Destination and funding.** This concept points at `www.healthformoms.co/save/`. Matched pairs on an identical video file split only by destination returned **417 leads at $13.26** on `www./save/` against **6 leads at $144.23** on the quiz, and **339 leads at $15.46** against **8 at $67.15** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. And of 95 ad-name groups created since 2026-08-01, **only 14 took $500 or more**, carrying 95.7% of the cohort's money — so two assets from a gated shoot day need real budget in wave one.

And the highest-performing element of the 6.2-million-view source, a genuinely unscripted recorded call with a real institution, is marked **out of play** until the brand says otherwise.

## Validation — strategy

**Which priority.** Roadmap **Priority 1** — and this is the important part, because Priority 1 is **the only strong-confidence call on the roadmap** and the idea bank has almost nothing pointed at it. This concept does not do the repair; it builds what stands in the repair's place. It also carries **Priority 3**'s casting call inside it, which is why the two merge into one shoot.

**Which persona.** `already-knows`, whose contextual identity in `personas/personas-profile.md` is literally the credentialed guard. Three of eight concepts this round go to her and this is the one built directly on her own documented behaviour.

**Which product priority.** The product is unchanged. What changes is the proof. `strategy/product-priority.md` says this brand has no catalogue and no SKU — what it has is four real decisions, and one of them is which qualification path the buyer walks. **A licensed advisor on camera is the first frame of that path made visible.**

**How it fits the diagnosis.** The diagnosis says the creative has the wrong premise, not the wrong audience. But it also says something quieter that this concept answers: **the account has spent twenty-one months asserting things about itself in a category under an enforcement wave, to an audience that fact-checks it in public for free.** The gap between what the ad claims and what the commenters know is not a compliance problem alone. It is a conversion problem.

**And the caveat.** **The roadmap is drafted and unapproved.** `status: drafted, awaiting review`, `approved_by: null`. The team was asked and expressed no preference. If Priority 1 is re-approved as the clear first priority, Lane C should be bigger than two concepts, not smaller.

**Which open loops it rests on.** Roadmap validation item 4 — whether a licensed partner advisor can be filmed and what compliance would let her say — is not a loop this brief rests on, it is a **gate this brief cannot open.** Roadmap loop 1, what is actually doing the work in this account's cheapest ads, prices the whole Priority 1 repair. And the roll-up's Tier 1 loop 6 — *what does a mother in this category treat as proof that a health insurance offer is real* — is the question this concept is a first answer to, and it is unclosed.

**Every line of customer-facing copy ships with both gate receipts** — the Grounding Review (`context-grounding-review` agent plus `scripts/grounding-check.py`) and the Voice Review (`creative-voice-review` agent plus `scripts/voice-lint.py`). Nothing gets filmed without both.

**And on this concept the compliance pass is not a formality — it is the concept.** In order:

1. **The gate.** Roadmap validation item 4 is answered in writing before a camera is booked. Who she is, that she is licensed, what compliance permits her to say, and whether she consents to appear.
2. **No false credential.** She is a licensed insurance advisor, not a physician. Not dressed as one, not framed as one, not implied to be one. Her title on screen, if shown, is her real one.
3. **No government or ACA reference**, which is the constraint a real advisor is most likely to breach naturally. It is briefed to her before the shoot.
4. Neither banned string: *"saving you 20% or more"*, *"Approved by thousands of Mom's across America"*. **This concept exists because those are coming out; it must not reintroduce them.**
5. No fabricated statistics. **This applies to her too** — if she cites a figure, it needs a real, checkable source, and this brand currently has none for the claims it already makes.
6. Claim qualifiers held verbatim; no hard guarantees; no named competitor; no scare tactic; the villain is the system.
7. **No synthetic presenter, and no AI-generated person anywhere in this asset.**

The dramatization disclaimer the account carries on its filmed units does not apply cleanly here and should be reviewed: **an ad whose entire product is that the person is real should not carry a disclaimer implying she is not.** That is a question for whoever owns compliance, and it is worth asking before the shoot rather than after.

## Confidence and what this tests

**The hypothesis, stated so it can fail.** A real credentialed speaker **replaces an unsupported claim and clears gate one at or below the account's trailing cost per lead of $22.29**, while holding above the 12% craft floor.

**What would falsify it.** Two credentialed assets landing above $22.29, or holding below 12%, or — the specific risk this concept carries and no other in the round does — **generating a comment section that corrects the correction.** That last one would not show up in cost per lead at all, and somebody has to actually read the comments in the first week to catch it.

**Confidence: mixed, and the halves are very different.**

- That the correction economy exists is **verified**: 103 comments across 13 ad names, with credentialed signatures, on the 1,322 denominator.
- That nobody credentialed has ever spoken in this account is **verified** across 147 delivering ads.
- That Authority is a format the account has never run is **verified** against the `hooks.md` taxonomy.
- That the format works in this category is **verified** at 6,200,000 views on one organic video — and that transfer to paid is **inferred**, from a single source in a different subject area.
- **That a correction delivered by the brand converts is untested.** Strong on the absence, thin on the outcome. This is the bet.
- That a credentialed speaker is compliant by construction is **inferred** from the brand's own funnel — it routes every lead to licensed advisors — and **it is not confirmed**, because nobody has asked.

**Gate two is dark, and on this concept there is a genuine reason to think it might point the other way.** 44 `Call` events across 16 ads against 4,421 leads in 90 days. `performance-targets-and-metrics.md` warns that a last-click form fill **over-rewards fast, low-friction clicks** and under-rewards creative that does the trust work. **This concept is nothing but trust work.** If it produces a slightly more expensive lead that a licensed advisor closes materially better, gate one will call it a loss and gate two would call it the best thing in the round — and nobody can see gate two. **When this ships, ask the partner agencies about its leads specifically.** That is the cheapest way this round could learn something that reorders the whole roadmap.

## Open loops

Two questions this brief left behind. The prompt's output template does not name a loops section; it is here because this brain carries one on every context doc and because the run instruction requires it.

**1. The brand has never replied to a comment in twenty months, and this concept asks it to start speaking with authority in the same threads.**

1,342 comments over twenty months and the brand has never replied once, while rival brokers answer in its place — 39 poaching comments across 10 ads, one operator posting fifteen personalised pitches under a single ad in 54 minutes [`verified`, `sub-context-docs/competitive-landscape.md`, carried on 1,322].

*Pull: Tension.* An ad that establishes the brand as the one who knows will draw questions in its own comment section, and the brand's twenty-month practice is silence.

*Question: If the brand puts a credentialed voice in an ad, who answers the questions that voice attracts?*

*Why it matters:* it decides whether this concept builds credibility or spends it. An authority ad with an unanswered comment section is a worse trust signal than no authority ad at all, and it hands rival brokers a warmer thread to work. This is a resourcing question, not a creative one, and it should be settled before wave one launches.

*Territory: Messaging.*

**2. Nobody has asked what this audience actually accepts as proof.**

Every credibility marker this brand carries is its own assertion. Zero reviews, zero press, no Better Business Bureau profile for the operating entity. This concept bets that a licensed person using the words correctly is the substitute — and that is a reasoned bet from the correction economy, not a measured one.

*Pull: Gap.* It fired because the round is removing the brand's only two credibility claims and replacing them with a device nobody has tested on this audience.

*Question: What does a mother in this category treat as proof that a health insurance offer is real?*

*Why it matters:* it is the roll-up's Tier 1 loop 6, and it decides what Lane C should be full of in the next round rather than what it happens to hold in this one. Two entries in a bank of twenty touch Priority 1 and neither is about the substitute. **That is the gap the next hunt should open with**, and this concept is one candidate answer rather than the answer.

*Territory: Messaging.*
