# Sprint plan — Health For Moms — Lead with the switch — 2026-09-04

## The round at a glance

**Eight concepts. Twenty assets. Two waves. And the whole thing is provisional.**

Read that last word first, because it governs everything below. The roadmap this round is built on carries `status: drafted, awaiting review` and `approved_by: null`. The team was asked to approve it and expressed no preference. It is the best-evidenced direction in this brain and four independent Phase-2 reads converged on it, and it is still a proposal. **If the team approves it with the three priorities in a different order, this round changes shape.** Nothing here is agreed direction, and no line below should be read as authorised.

Here is the call.

| Lane | What it is | Concepts | Assets | Priority it serves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The switch, rebuilt clean** | The employer-plan message, on a compliant frame, static-led, pointed at `www./save/` | **4** | 11 | Priority 2, plus Priority 3 casting inside it |
| **B. The working lane** | The calm lifestyle video that is carrying the account right now, plus the casting re-cut | **2** | 5 | Priority 3, protecting Priority 2's read |
| **C. The proof substitute** | What the brand says instead of the two claims coming out of the copy block | **2** | 4 | Priority 1 |
| | | **8** | **20** | |

Persona split: **5 to `pays-and-still-owes`, 3 to `already-knows`, 0 to `built-it-herself`.** The third persona is dropped from this round on purpose and the reason is written out below.

Format split: **3 statics and 5 videos.** That is the biggest single change in the plan, and it is the one that will look strange next to what the account has been building. Statics turn a link click into a lead at **16.57%** against video's **8.28%** lifetime, and **not one static has been built in this account since 2026-06-01** [`verified live`, a static-filtered pull of everything created since that date returned zero rows, 2026-09-04].

Talent split: **three of the eight concepts need nobody on camera at all.** The brand names casting and creator sourcing as its number one creative bottleneck. Three slots that walk around that bottleneck entirely is a deliberate design choice, not an accident of the pile.

**And the one thing that must not be misread.** Roadmap Priority 2 says restart the two employer-plan statics that returned 3,817 leads at $11.79. That reads like flipping a switch. It is not. Seven static ad-name groups holding **$35,234.91 lifetime and 2,871 leads at $12.27** are mock X posts carrying a blue verified checkmark on an invented persona and printed engagement counts, and **`B1 - Copy 7` at $30,339.32 is one of the two the roadmap wants back** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04, recorded in `running-notes/missing-context.md`]. Printed retweet, like, bookmark and view counts are statistics, and they are invented, against the brand's own non-negotiable **"No fabricated statistics."** So **Lane A is a rebuild, and the $11.79 is not a forecast.** The message is proven. The vehicle around it is not reusable, and part of why it converted may have been the fake social proof nobody has separated out.

## How the round is sized

The number is backed out of three things, in this order: the money, the real cadence once the duplicates are stripped, and the hardest gate, which is how much new work this account's delivery will actually read.

### One. The money

**Trailing 30 days, 2026-08-05 to 2026-09-03: $48,381.47, 2,171 leads, $22.29 cost per lead, $23.60 CPM, 1.93% CTR.** 94 ad-name groups delivered. [`verified live`, 2026-09-04.]

**Trailing 365 days, 2025-09-04 to 2026-09-03: $639,555.59, 31,033 leads, $20.61 cost per lead.** [`verified live`, 2026-09-04.]

Two things follow. First, at roughly $48,000 a month this account sits in the middle band described in `creative-strategy-by-brand-size.md`, and it is arriving at that band from underneath after an April peak of $156,716.43. That band's two levers are output volume as a real lever rather than an occasional push, and reaching genuinely new audiences instead of sweeping up demand that is already there. Both point at a round bigger than the brand's habit.

Second, $22.29 sits in the middle of the brand's own "meh" band. Its stated tiers are under $15 fantastic, $15 to $20 good, $20 to $25 meh, over $25 not good. The account has been parked in "meh" for six straight months and was in "fantastic" as recently as October 2025 at $12.27. So there is real headroom, and it is headroom the account has already occupied once.

### Two. The cadence, once the duplicates come out

The method says strip the sale and promotional creative before you count, because a flood of sale variations makes a brand look like it produces more than it does. **This brand has no sale creative at all.** There is no purchase event, no discount, no promo calendar. So the strip that matters here is a different one and it does the same job: **duplicates and re-aims are not net-new concepts.**

Raw count first. **95 distinct ad-name groups were created between 2026-08-01 and 2026-09-04**, which is about five weeks, so roughly 19 a week [`verified live`, created-time count, 2026-09-04]. That number is not the cadence and reading it as one would size this round three times too big.

Here is what comes out of it:

- **The duplicates.** In the week to 2026-09-04, 58 ads were built and **28 of them are three already-proven files copied and re-pointed at `quiz.healthformoms.com`**, with 3 more re-pointed at the broken `go.` page [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/weekly-performance-snapshot.md`, from a created-time pull cross-checked against grouped media hashes]. Those are destination tests wearing new ad names. They are not new creative.
- **The variants of one concept.** `moms-63 2b` and `moms-63 3e` are two ad-name groups and one concept, built the same day from one shoot.

Once both come out, the genuinely distinct net-new concepts in that five-week window run to about **seven**: the new footage under the proven POV line, the crying-mom set, the state list shot as a person, the calm lifestyle POV family, the school-pickup arithmetic opener, the tweet-overlay deductible family, and the fifteen satirical skit files of 2026-08-31. The monthly performance report reached the same place independently, naming six numbered tests inside the month.

**So the real net-new evergreen cadence is about 1.4 concepts a week** [`inferred`, from the created-time drops read against the account's own test list; the underlying counts are `verified`].

One more number belongs here because it is the brand's own. **The team states its production volume as one to five net-new concepts and one to five iterations a month** [`stated`, brand context, recorded in `sub-context-docs/ad-account-evaluation.md`]. August ran above its own stated ceiling because it is ramping for Open Enrollment. Both numbers are true and the gap between them is worth knowing before anyone commits to eight.

### Three. The multiple, and then the gate that actually binds

The account ships a fresh batch every two to four weeks when it is healthy [`inferred` from `created_time` on the dated drops, in `sub-context-docs/marketing-calendar-and-campaigns.md`]. Take a three-week round. At the current cadence that is **4.2 concepts**. The method's default is to roughly double the current evergreen cadence, which lands on **8.4, so eight**.

Now the gates.

**The budget gate says yes.** $48,381 in thirty days and pacing above August is enough money to fund eight new things without starving the incumbents.

**The confidence gate says be careful.** One HIGH band in a bank of twenty. Gate two invisible. A roadmap nobody signed. That argues against a big swing, and it is why eight rather than twelve.

**And the gate that actually binds is one nobody in this brain had put a number on. This account cannot read more than about a dozen new things at a time.**

Of the **95 ad-name groups built since 2026-08-01, only 14 took $500 or more of lifetime spend — and those 14 carry $24,804.82 of the $25,908.79 the whole 95 carry, which is 95.7%** [`verified live`, two matched created-time pulls, 2026-09-04]. Eighty-one of ninety-five never got read at all. The sharpest single example: **fifteen satirical skit files went live on 2026-08-31 and drew $4.23 between them across 163 impressions in five days**, while $2,080.15 in the same week went to three duplicates of already-proven ads pointed at a page that eats most of its traffic [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/weekly-performance-snapshot.md`].

Read through the breakdown effect in `ad-account-analysis.md`, that is not a mistake. It is Meta pacing budget toward the pocket it predicts will stay cheapest, which is exactly what the system is built to do. But the consequence is blunt and it should shape this round: **every format test this brand runs dies before it can be measured.** A round bigger than what delivery will fund is not a bigger test. It is a bigger pile of files nobody ever learns anything from.

**So: eight concepts, twenty assets, launched in two waves.** Wave one is four concepts and twelve assets, which sits just under the fourteen the account actually funded past $500 last month. Wave two is four concepts and eight assets, launched once wave one has readable numbers.

And the honest posture, stated plainly the way the method asks: **this is a first shot, not a maximum load.** The plan is to find what works and put money behind it next round, not to put every asset on swing one. If wave one produces a clear read, scale the round up.

**Every concept in this round names a hypothesis.** That is what keeps a doubled round from being noise, and any concept below that could not name one would have been cut or merged. None were.

### Message first, not format first

`ideation-and-brainstorming.md` is clear that working format-first is a luxury a mature, high-spend account with a proven hero earns, and a brand with a message problem cannot afford. The roadmap's own diagnosis is that this account has spent twenty-one months and $743,218.09 addressing the wrong premise. That is a message problem by definition. **So this round is message-first: what each ad says is decided here, and how it looks gets settled at brief time.** The one place format is decided up front is Lane A, and that is an economic decision rather than a craft one, for the reason in the next section.

The creative read behind that, and it comes from the media rather than the names. `moms-63 2b` and `3e` carry the exact same text hook as the April incumbent, word for word, and the picture is completely different: instead of a mother and baby lying still on white bedding under a high-angle camera, a well-dressed mother walks out of a house holding her young son's hand, outdoors, in motion [`verified` from the `visual_hook` fields on all three ads]. `moms-53 3` is the angle the brand itself names as its strongest, finally shot as a person [`stated`, brand context, verbatim: *"State angle has worked very well if you see the account. **So the working lane is: a proven line, a genuinely different first three seconds, a calm register, and `www./save/` as the destination.** Per `iterations.md`, a format or vehicle change on an ad that already converts is one of the highest-confidence moves available, and this account just ran that experiment on its own money and won it.

**The lowest-risk unpushed channel for that lane is the one it has never been pushed through.** Statics turn a link click into a lead at **16.57%** against video's **8.28%** lifetime, on 82,871 and 312,052 link clicks respectively, and they bought impressions 24% cheaper. And zero statics have been built since 2026-06-01. That is the classic opening the method names, running in reverse: here the proven format is the dormant one.

### What has been tried, and what failed

Named explicitly, and ruled out of this round.

- **Grief in the opener.** The Moms54 set, three ads created 2026-08-13. `moms54-3` posted a **57.02% hook rate, a 13.66% hold rate and an 8.08-second average play**, the strongest attention numbers anywhere in the account, and delivered leads at **$29.85**. - **A synthetic presenter.** Six separate rejections across three years and four ad families, the closest thing to a rule in this brand's whole customer record. **Ruled out.**
- **The flat savings number and the approval headline.** Both run on essentially the whole account and both break the brand's own non-negotiable rules. Neither appears in any concept below, and no brief from this round may carry either string forward "to match the account."
- **Pregnancy and chronic-condition demand.** Real demand, and the plans are medically underwritten so the funnel screens her out. Roads not taken 2 and 3. **Ruled out.**
- **The income-gap story.** Blocked on a hard constraint rather than on evidence: her whole story is a comparison to two public programs the brand's own rules forbid naming. **Ruled out until the brand reconciles it.**

## The split — by lane and persona

### Why the lanes are lanes and not SKUs

This brand has no catalogue and no SKU. `strategy/product-priority.md` says so directly and refuses to invent one. What it does have is four real decisions: which offer opens the relationship, which destination the money points at, which qualification path the buyer walks, and which format carries it. **So the round splits across lanes rather than SKUs, and the two forces the method names still apply exactly.**

**Scale the hero. Lane A takes 4 of 8.** The employer-plan switch is the thing the strategy is trying to make win, and all four Phase-2 inputs converged on it independently. It gets the majority.

**Protect what already works. Lane B takes 2 of 8.** The calm lifestyle video on `www./save/` is what is carrying the account today at $13.26 and $15.44. It keeps a real share.

**And Lane C takes 2 of 8** because the evaluation's single loudest finding is that Priority 1 is the only strong-confidence priority on the roadmap and the bank has almost nothing pointed at it. Two of twenty entries touch it, and **neither is about what the brand says instead** of the two claims coming out of that copy block. A round that repairs the copy and puts nothing in its place has removed the brand's only two credibility markers and replaced them with silence.

**The two guardrails, held.**

*Do not give the working thing full rein.* Lane B gets 2 slots, not 5. The savings and spousal-win family already carries roughly 68% of spend through one line. Loading this round onto it would eat the budget and make a clean read on the switch impossible, which is the whole point of the round.

*Do not over-swing away from it.* Lane B keeps 2 slots and **the incumbents stay running.** `seasonality.md` is explicit that evergreen winners stay on while new creative layers in, and only get displaced when the data says so. With Open Enrollment 58 days out, switching off the thing that currently buys leads in order to test something unproven would be the expensive kind of brave. **Layer. Do not swap.**

### Personas, allocated by confidence

Pulled from the reference matrix in `personas/personas-profile.md`. Every persona in this brain is capped at mixed confidence and one is thinner than that, for the sourcing reason in the data limitations.

| Persona | Standing | Confidence | Share of spend today | Concepts this round |

| `already-knows` | Secondary | mixed | roughly 19%, none of it built for her | **3** |

**`pays-and-still-owes` gets five** because she is volume rank 1, because the account already reaches her, and because the whole diagnosis is that the creative aimed at her has the wrong premise rather than the wrong audience. Nothing in this round touches targeting. It changes what she is told.

**`already-knows` gets three, and that is a sized bet, not a lead call.** She is emotional-intensity rank 1, the account finds her without anyone aiming, and **no ad has ever been built for her on purpose.** The roadmap's phrase is exact: *"She gets the casting work in Priority 3 and a sized bet, not the lead."* Three of eight is that bet. It stops at three because creative that reaches her costs **$26.58 a lead against the account's $18.78 lifetime blend, 41.5% more**, and her share is falling from 21.2% lifetime to 16.2% last quarter. The team's stated objective is efficiency before volume, so a 41.5% premium cannot take the lead slot while the gate that would justify it is invisible.

## Per-concept rationale

### J001 — The permission static, rebuilt clean

**Why it earns the lead slot.** It is the only HIGH band in a bank of twenty, and it earns that on the brand's own money rather than on a hunch. Two statics carrying this message returned **3,817 leads at $11.79 across $44,993.83, turning 19.04% of their link clicks into leads** [`verified live` in the evaluation, 2026-09-04]. That is against the account's $18.78 lifetime blend and its $22.29 trailing thirty days, and it sits 21% under the brand's own "fantastic" ceiling of $15.

And the absence around it is total. **A lifetime keyword sweep of every searchable ad field for the permission verb "decline" returns zero ad-name groups in the account's entire history.** A sweep for "employer" returns exactly one, and its visible copy names self-employment rather than an employer plan [both `verified live`, 2026-09-04]. Twenty-one months, $743,218.09, and the sentence has never been said. Meanwhile a stranger said it for free under an ad the brand paid for, on 2026-05-05 under `MOMS38 - 1 - V2`: *"And if it's an employer insurance, you don't have to sign up with that employer insurance you can decline the coverage and go out and find your own insurance carrier."* That is a commenter under the brand's own paid ad, not a customer, and the brand has never replied to a comment in 1,342 of them.

**Its hypothesis.** The employer-switch message clears gate one on a compliant frame. Put plainly: the $11.79 was bought by what the ad said, not by the blue checkmark and the printed engagement counts around it.

**That hypothesis is the whole reason this concept is a rebuild.** `B1 - Copy 7` is one of the two proven files and it is a mock X post with a verified badge on an invented persona and *"390 Retweets 7,314 Likes 751 Bookmarks"* and *"333.3k Views"* printed on the image [`verified live`, 2026-09-04]. So the brief must rebuild the frame from scratch: no platform mockup, no invented handle, no printed engagement numbers, no fabricated timestamp. And **the $11.79 does not travel into the brief as a forecast.** The fabricated social proof is plausibly part of why it converted, and nobody has separated the two.

**Why static, and why four variations.** Statics convert clicks to leads at twice video's rate here, they need no creator and no shoot day, and none has been built since 2026-06-01. Four variations is the highest count in the round because conviction is highest here and because the asset is the cheapest thing in the plan to make. Four genuinely different single frames cost less than one video shoot.

**Talent need: none.** No creator, no casting call, no shoot. That is a large part of why it leads, given the brand names casting as its number one bottleneck.

**Doorway and emotion.** A *switch*: it asks a woman using an old solution to change. The emotion is low-intensity positive, which is the zone `emotional-delivery-and-timing.md` says most brands underinvest in and which this account's own money agrees with. Over the 30 days to 2026-09-02 the calm openers delivered leads at **$13.29 to $24.45** while the high-distress openers delivered at **$29.85 to $38.09** [`verified`, `audits/2026-09/monthly-hook-audit.md`]. In TEEP terms this sits at **Trigger**, mirroring her state back rather than introducing a solution before the problem is felt.

## Open loops

Three questions this planning pass left behind. The prompt's output template does not name a loops section; it is here because this brain carries one on every context doc and because the run instruction requires it. Each is about **the shape of the round** rather than about the account, and each could change what gets built rather than how.

**1. Eighty-one of the ninety-five new files this account built in five weeks never got read, and nobody has asked what decides which fourteen do.**

Of 95 ad-name groups created since 2026-08-01, 14 took $500 or more and carry 95.7% of the cohort's money. Fifteen satirical skit files drew $4.23 between them across 163 impressions in five days. Meanwhile three duplicates of already-proven ads took $2,080.15 in the same week.

*Pull: Gap.* It fired because the sizing math in every craft doc assumes a round gets tested, and this account's own delivery data says most of a round does not.

*Question: What decides which new files in this account get delivery in their first week?*

*Why it matters:* it sets the real ceiling on every future round, not just this one. If launch structure is what decides it, then the fix is a media-buying change and the round could be bigger. If Meta is simply pacing toward whatever it already knows, then every format test this brand runs will keep dying before it can be read, and the round should be smaller and staged harder. This is Tier 1 loop 5 in the roll-up, arriving here from the production side.

*Territory: Messaging.*

**2. The round's lead concept is a rebuild of an asset whose economics nobody has separated from its fake social proof.**

Seven static groups holding $35,234.91 and 2,871 leads at $12.27 are mock platform posts with an invented verified persona and printed engagement counts. Two of them are the files Priority 2 wants back. The message inside them has never been tested without that frame around it.

*Pull: Tension.* Two things this brain treats as settled cannot both be right: the switching lane is a proven idea that only needs restarting, and the asset that proved it breaks a rule the brand marked non-negotiable.

*Question: How much of this account's cheapest lead cost was bought by the switching message, and how much by the fake social frame around it?*

*Why it matters:* J001 is the answer to it, which is unusual and worth saying. This round is the test. But it also means the round's lead concept carries more risk than its HIGH band suggests, and the team should know that before wave one launches rather than after.

*Territory: Messaging.*

**3. The round bets four of eight slots on statics, and this account has not built one in three months.**

Statics turn clicks into leads at twice video's rate lifetime, and a static-filtered pull of everything created since 2026-06-01 returns zero rows. Nobody has written down why they stopped.

*Pull: Gap.* It fired because a proven format went dark and no document in this brain records a decision to stop making it.

*Question: Why did this account stop making statics around March, and can it start again this week?*

*Why it matters:* it is the single question that most directly gates wave one. If it was a staffing or agency change, three of the eight concepts ship in days. If something was learned — a lead-quality problem, a platform issue — that is a hard constraint nobody wrote down and it would overturn the largest piece of this plan. Brand-routed, and it costs one conversation.

*Territory: Product.*
