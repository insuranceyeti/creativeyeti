# Success definition — what winning means for Health For Moms

> Source: brand intake, 2026-09-03. Everything here is **stated** brand input, not verified fact.
> Where the data later contradicts a line, surface the conflict rather than swallowing it.

## Main business objective right now

**Both scale and efficiency, but efficiency first.** (`stated` — user, 2026-09-03 intake.)

Lead volume matters, but the unit economics have to work before more budget goes in. The
sequence the team wants is: fix cost per lead, then scale on the ads that proved out.

**What this means for every read in this brain:** an efficiency finding outranks a volume
finding when the two conflict. Do not recommend scaling spend behind an ad whose CPL is
drifting up, even if it is producing the most leads. Identify the efficient winners first,
then say what scaling them would cost.

## North-star metric

**CPL first, then lead quality, before really scaling.** (`stated` — user, 2026-09-03 intake,
verbatim: *"CPL is important to scale but then we look at the lead quality as well before
really scaling"*.)

This is a two-gate definition of a winner, not a single number:

1. **Gate one — CPL.** The ad has to be efficient enough to justify more budget. Parker can
   see this directly on every ad.
2. **Gate two — lead quality.** Before real scale goes behind it, the lead has to hold up
   downstream. **Parker cannot see this.** Lead quality lives with the partner agencies, not
   in Meta.

**The standing rule this creates:** never call an ad a winner on CPL alone. A low-CPL ad is a
*candidate* that has passed gate one. Say so in those words, and name gate two as the open
question, every time. Recommending scale on gate-one evidence alone misrepresents the team's
own bar.

**Open question:** where lead-quality data lives and whether Parker can ever reach it. Logged
in `missing-context.md`. Until it is answered, every scale recommendation in this brain carries
the gate-two caveat.

## Secondary metrics

Not separately specified at intake. From the account's own shape, the working set is lead
volume, CPM, CTR, hook rate and hold rate. ROAS, AOV and purchase value are meaningless here
and must never be reported — this is lead generation with zero purchase events.

**In-platform, Meta Ads Manager.** (`stated` — user, 2026-09-03 intake.)

No third-party attribution tool. Northbeam is not connected, which the Phase 0 surface test
confirmed independently. So Parker's Meta-reported numbers and the team's working numbers
should agree. Do not imply multi-touch attribution anywhere in this brain.
