# Hooks grounding bounce — the script's premise stood in for the persona

**What the gate caught.** The user's script had the guest say insurance "felt like this thing I was never going to fully understand." Two of four hooks were built on that confusion premise. `personas-profile.md` says the opposite of the flagship persona: "She is not confused about insurance... Explaining the category to this audience would be doing the wrong thing well," and `voc-category-jargon.md` open loop 1 asks how much of the audience understands her plan better than the ads assume. The obligation on record is dutiful payment with no payback (`paying-twice`), not not-understanding.

**Candidate rule.** When a user supplies a script, the script's self-description of the customer is input, not persona evidence. Load the persona doc's sophistication and message-signal sections before naming the ICP and emotion, and grep `personas/` for the script's key premise words. If the premise has zero corpus hits, say so in the ICP block and label any hook that carries it illustrative. A premise that contradicts the persona doc gets one flag line in the deliverable (SKILL.md step 1), and the untapped-format hooks get built on the persona's obligation, not the script's.

**Smaller catches.** Investment (#2) must be time or money *spent on attempts*, not time avoiding; a "story cold-open" needs rising sound, otherwise it is candid face mid-emotion; Permission was not fully untapped, the "At peace" ad brushed it at 2.30% hold; the flagship overlay is 23 words, not 25; `brand-lens.md` names the cheaper two-voice proof (`MOMS25 BLAST - X1 - Copy 2`, $19.90) the draft missed for the pricier street interviews.
